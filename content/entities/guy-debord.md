# Guy Debord

Named in the Scheme of Work for **The Society of the Spectacle**, as an anchor for the [[concepts/postmodern-media|Postmodern Media]] debate.

## What Cambridge attributes to him

> "**Guy Debord's 1967 work The Society of the Spectacle** is a critique of media-saturated capitalist culture is still relevant in diagnosing aspects of the postmodern condition" (SoW p.23)

In short: Debord wrote a 1967 critique of media-saturated capitalist culture. Cambridge treats it as still useful for diagnosing the postmodern condition.

## Where he is used

The Scheme of Work sets one exercise (SoW p.24):

> "**Exercise:** Watch a TV news story. What features does it have of 'The Society of the Spectacle'?"

This task asks you to turn an abstract diagnosis into observable features of a real text. Anchoring your arguments in specific texts or phenomena is what marks a sophisticated answer.

## Spectacle and détournement

**détournement** appears in [[concepts/power-and-the-media|Power and the Media]] as a strategy of resistance (SoW p.25). Cambridge names it but credits no one.

Both ideas come from the same tradition: Debord and the Situationists. The tradition supplies both the diagnosis, the spectacle, and the counter-move, détournement. Linking them is a genuine synthesis you can make in a strong answer.
