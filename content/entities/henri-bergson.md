# Henri Bergson

**1859 to 1941.**

## The claim in one line

Lived time is **duration** — accumulated and indivisible, not a sequence of separable states — and memory divides into **habit** performed by the body and **pure memory** that survives whole.

## Key quote

> The past survives under two distinct forms: in motor mechanisms, and in independent recollections.

## How this course uses him

Bergson appears in two places:

- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w09-is-the-human-and-machine-line-essential-or-contingent|Week 9]]** — he supplies the strongest objection to machine minds: **a system that can be reset to a previous state has no duration.**
- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w10-what-survives-forgetting|Week 10]]** — he reframes forgetting as a failure of *attention* rather than of *storage*. On this account we forget nothing; we simply cannot attend to most of it.

The two uses connect through a question raised in Week 10: if a machine forgot the way a body forgets, by accumulating distortion rather than deleting files, **would the week-9 objection survive?**

## Paired with Freud

Week 10 runs Bergson and [[entities/sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]] as two incompatible accounts converging on one conclusion — **forgetting is a function of memory, not a failure of it.** They disagree about almost everything else, and that is the point: it is the course's cleanest example of an argument that does not depend on picking a winner.

## Reading

*Matter and Memory*, on habit memory and pure memory, is on the extension list. It is not assigned.
