# Roland Barthes

> [!note] Taught in two courses
> He appears in two courses for two different arguments. Everything below is Media Studies (9607), where he anchors semiotics and narrative codes. For the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]], where he is a one-sentence statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]], skip to [the last section](#in-the-oxbridge-discussion-group).

The most-used theorist in 9607, and one of two theorists named for the AS year (the other is [[entities/stuart-hall|Stuart Hall]]). He is the only theorist named in Component 1.

## What Cambridge attributes to him

Two distinct contributions:

**Semiotics — signs, codes and meaning.** The theory hook for C1 research on both briefs: "Relevant theory: semiotics – **Roland Barthes'** ideas about signs, codes and meaning."

**Narrative codes and signification.** In the Component 2 textual-analysis material:

> "This work does not need to be densely theoretical, but there is an opportunity to use some of **Roland Barthes'** narrative codes to provide some explanatory depth… as well as notions of signification – what is **denoted** at a descriptive level, and what is suggested, implied or coded at a **connotative** level"

Barthes here is optional explanatory depth, not required content.

## Where he fits in the course

- **[[concepts/media-language]]** — denotation and connotation is the ladder the whole of Unit 2 climbs. The denotation-before-connotation discipline is built into CS1 in [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-01-the-four-key-concepts|L01]] and marked in A1.
- **[[concepts/narrative|Narrative]]** — the enigma code gives narrative analysis explanatory depth without heavy theory, and connects to the C1 film-opening research question "how does the film establish an enigma from the outset?"
- **C1 research** — signs and codes are the vocabulary for annotating real openings and covers.

## BNDS usage

Taught across [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-02-semiotics-how-images-mean|L02]] and [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-03-sign-types-and-codes|L03]] as the semiotics spine of Unit 2.

## In the Oxbridge Discussion Group

A completely different Barthes here, and a much smaller one: a single sentence, delivered once.

### The claim in one line

Ideology works best when it presents itself as **nature** rather than as a position, so the most ideological text is the one that appears to have no author.

### The quote

> The author who disappears behind the text is the most ideological author of all.

### How that course uses him

Barthes arrives at the end of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]], to name what you have already built out of maps and video-game boundaries — never at the start. He is the cleanest single-sentence statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]]. The ordering matters: the line is compressed enough to be memorized without being understood if it arrives before the examples.

### The connection between the two courses

Both are the same underlying claim about signification in different registers. 9607 teaches denotation and connotation as a method for reading a text; Oxbridge takes the political consequence — that a text which hides its own authorship is doing the most ideological work — and never touches the semiotic apparatus at all. In the two rooms he can look like two different figures.

The Oxbridge quotation is given without a source text, as with every quotation in that course.
