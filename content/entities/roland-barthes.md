> **Taught in two courses, for two arguments.** Everything below is Media Studies (9607), where Barthes anchors semiotics and the narrative codes. For the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]], where he gives a one-sentence statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]], skip to [the last section](#in-the-oxbridge-discussion-group).

Barthes is the most-used theorist in 9607. The from-2021 Scheme of Work names only two theorists for the AS year: Barthes and [[entities/stuart-hall|Stuart Hall]].

## What Cambridge attributes to him

Two distinct contributions, in two places.

**Semiotics: signs, codes and meaning.** Named as the theory hook for C1 research on both briefs: "Relevant theory: semiotics – **Roland Barthes'** ideas about signs, codes and meaning" (SoW p.8 for film, p.9 for magazine).

**Narrative codes and signification.** In the Component 2 textual-analysis material (SoW p.14):

> "This work does not need to be densely theoretical, but there is an opportunity to use some of **Roland Barthes'** narrative codes to provide some explanatory depth… as well as notions of signification – what is **denoted** at a descriptive level, and what is suggested, implied or coded at a **connotative** level"

Cambridge lists Barthes here as optional explanatory depth, not required content.

## Where he earns his place in the course

- **[[concepts/media-language]]**: **denotation** and **connotation** is the ladder the whole of Unit 2 climbs. The denotation-before-connotation discipline is built into CS1 in [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-01-the-four-key-concepts|L01]] and marked in A1.
- **[[concepts/narrative|Narrative]]**: the **enigma code** gives narrative analysis explanatory depth without heavy theory. It connects to the C1 film-opening research question "how does the film establish an enigma from the outset?" (CWH p.11).
- **C1 research**: signs and codes are the vocabulary you use to annotate real openings and covers.

## In the Oxbridge Discussion Group

A different Barthes, and a smaller one: a single sentence, delivered once.

### The claim in one line

Ideology works best when it presents itself as **nature** rather than as a position. So the most ideological text is the one that appears to have no author.

### The quote

> The author who disappears behind the text is the most ideological author of all.

### How that course uses him

Barthes arrives at the end of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]], to name what you have already built out of maps and video-game boundaries. He is the cleanest statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]] in a single sentence. The examples come first: the line is compressed enough to memorize without understanding if it arrives too early.

### The connection between the two courses

Both are the same claim about signification in different registers. 9607 teaches denotation and connotation as a method for reading a text. Oxbridge takes the political consequence: a text that hides its own authorship is doing the most ideological work, and never touches the semiotic apparatus at all. A student in both rooms would not obviously recognize him as the same figure.
