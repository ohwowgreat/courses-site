> **Taught in two courses, for two different arguments.** Everything below is Media Studies (9607), where he anchors semiotics and narrative codes. For the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]], where he is the one-sentence statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]], skip to [the last section](#in-the-oxbridge-discussion-group).

He is the most-used theorist in 9607, and one of only two named for the AS year. The other is [[entities/stuart-hall|Stuart Hall]]. In Component 1, he is the only theorist named: the film row lists "Mise-en-Scène – the production of visual meaning: **Roland Barthes' 5 Narrative Codes**".

## What Cambridge attributes to him

Two distinct contributions, in two places:

**Semiotics: signs, codes and meaning.** He is the theory hook for C1 research on both briefs: "Relevant theory: semiotics – **Roland Barthes'** ideas about signs, codes and meaning" (SoW p.8 for film, p.9 for magazine).

**Narrative codes and signification.** In the Component 2 textual-analysis material (SoW p.14):

> "This work does not need to be densely theoretical, but there is an opportunity to use some of **Roland Barthes'** narrative codes to provide some explanatory depth… as well as notions of signification – what is **denoted** at a descriptive level, and what is suggested, implied or coded at a **connotative** level"

Cambridge positions Barthes here as optional explanatory depth, not required content.

## Where he earns his place in the course

- **[[concepts/media-language]]**: denotation and connotation is the ladder the whole of Unit 2 climbs. The denotation-before-connotation discipline is built into CS1 in [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-01-the-four-key-concepts|L01]] and marked in A1.
- **[[concepts/narrative|Narrative]]**: the enigma code gives narrative analysis explanatory depth without heavy theory. It connects directly to the C1 film-opening research question "how does the film establish an enigma from the outset?" (CWH p.11).
- **C1 research**: signs and codes are the vocabulary for annotating real openings and covers.

Taught across [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-02-semiotics-signs-and-codes|L02]] and [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-03-camera|L03]] as the semiotics spine of Unit 2.

## In the Oxbridge Discussion Group

A completely different Barthes, and a much smaller one: a single sentence, delivered once.

### The claim in one line

Ideology works best when it presents itself as **nature** rather than as a position, so the most ideological text is the one that appears to have no author.

### The quote

> The author who disappears behind the text is the most ideological author of all.

### How that course uses him

Barthes arrives at the end of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]], to name what you have already built out of maps and video-game boundaries. He is the cleanest statement of [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]] in a single sentence. The order matters: the line is compressed enough to be memorized without being understood if it arrives before the examples.

### The connection between the two courses

Both are the same underlying claim about signification, arriving in different registers. 9607 teaches denotation and connotation as a method for reading a text. Oxbridge takes the political consequence, that a text which hides its own authorship is doing the most ideological work, and never touches the semiotic apparatus at all. A student in both rooms would not obviously recognize him as the same figure.
