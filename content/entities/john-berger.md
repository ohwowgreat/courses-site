# John Berger

**1926 to 2017.**

## The claim in one line

The way we see things is affected by what we know or what we believe. So **seeing is never a neutral reception of what is there.**

## Quote

> We never look at just one thing; we are always looking at the relation between things and ourselves.

## How the course uses him

Berger **opens the course**. His prose is plain enough to enter right away, and his claim is strong enough to hold up under a whole semester of pressure.

His sentence goes on the board in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|W01]]. It is the clearest example of [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|principle 2]] in the course.

One question to test the claim: Berger says we never look at one thing, we look at the relation between things and ourselves. Is that true of a photograph of a stranger?

## Set text

*Ways of Seeing*, chapter 1, first six pages. The BBC episode of the same name is offered as a second entry point if you find the prose hard. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-reading-list|the reading list]].

## Also taught in Art Appreciation

Berger anchors [[classes/art-appreciation/lesson-plans/art-appreciation-s1-lesson-10-berger-and-the-reproduction|Art Appreciation L10]] ("a reproduction is not a neutral copy") and heads its theorist spine.

The two courses take different chapters of the same argument: Art Appreciation runs Berger on **reproduction**, Oxbridge runs him on **perception**.
