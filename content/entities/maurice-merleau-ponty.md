# Maurice Merleau-Ponty

**1908 to 1961.**

## The claim in one line

Perception is not a mental picture assembled from data. **The body is the medium through which there is a world for us at all.**

## Quote to put on the board

> The body is our general medium for having a world.

*Phenomenology of Perception*, 1945. You get this quotation before [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w08-can-the-body-be-trusted|W08]], a session with no reading that still arrives with something prepared.

## How the Oxbridge course uses him

**The anchor of week 8.** The follow-up question is the one you generate yourself:

> If the body is the medium, **what happens when the medium is wrong?**

Phantom limb and chronic pain are the test cases. Synaesthesia and thermal adaptation extend them. The sharpest form of the question, once the cases are on the table: if the medium is wrong, are you wrong about the world, or do you have a different world?

The quotation is also [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]]'s closing question, so it sits on the board for a week before the session that uses it.

## Where the session goes next

Merleau-Ponty establishes the body as medium rather than window. Spinoza's reframe closes the session by changing the question from what a body **is** to what a body **can do**. That shift is what makes [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w09-is-the-human-and-machine-line-essential-or-contingent|W09]] continuous with W08 rather than a new subject. Both map capacities instead of testing essences.
