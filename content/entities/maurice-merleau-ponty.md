# Maurice Merleau-Ponty

**1908 to 1961.**

## The claim in one line

Perception is not a mental picture assembled from data; **the body is the medium through which there is a world for us at all.**

## Key quote

> The body is our general medium for having a world.

*Phenomenology of Perception*, 1945. You receive this reading before [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w08-can-the-body-be-trusted|W08]].

## How the course uses him

He is the anchor of week 8. The question to follow up:

> If the body is the medium, **what happens when the medium is wrong?**

Phantom limb and chronic pain are the test cases; synaesthesia and thermal adaptation extend them. The sharpest form of the question, once the cases are on the table: *if the medium is wrong, are you wrong about the world, or do you have a different world?*

The quotation is also [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]]'s closing question, so it appears a week before the session that uses it.

## Where the session goes next

Merleau-Ponty establishes the body as medium rather than window; Spinoza's reframe closes the session by changing the question from what a body **is** to what a body **can do**. That shift makes [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w09-is-the-human-and-machine-line-essential-or-contingent|W09]] continuous with W08 — both map capacities instead of testing essences.
