# Friedrich Nietzsche

**1844 to 1900.**

## The claim in one line

There are no facts, only interpretations. **The demand for a disinterested view is itself an interested move.**

## A key quote

> There is only a perspective seeing, only a perspective knowing.

## Where he appears in the course

Nietzsche gives the strongest statement of **perspectivism**: the view that there is no neutral, unframed knowledge, and that all seeing and knowing happens from a perspective. This is the position [[concepts/view-from-nowhere|the closing question]] tests.

He shows up in two places:

- As a counterweight when you try to defend an unframed view.
- In [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w08-can-the-body-be-trusted|W08]], as the suspicion that the body, not the mind, is doing the reasoning.

He has no session of his own and no set text.

## The risk

Nietzsche is the thinker most likely to be used as a conversation-ender: "there are no facts, so nothing you say counts." Watch for that move and name it. Perspectivism is a claim, and a claim still has to be argued for. If it is true of itself, then it is one perspective among others. That is the fourth question of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]] arriving early, which is fine, as long as you name it.
