# Friedrich Nietzsche

**1844 to 1900.**

## The claim in one line

There are no facts, only interpretations, and **the demand for a disinterested view is itself an interested move.**

## Quote

> There is only a perspective seeing, only a perspective knowing.

## Where he appears in the course

He has no session of his own and no set text. He comes up in two places:

- As the strongest statement of the position [[concepts/view-from-nowhere|the closing question]] tests.
- In [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w08-can-the-body-be-trusted|W08]], as the suspicion that **the body, not the mind, is doing the reasoning.**

## The risk

Nietzsche is easy to use as a conversation-ender ("there are no facts, so nothing you say counts"). Notice that move when it happens: perspectivism as a claim still has to be argued for, and if it is true of itself it is one perspective among others. That is the fourth question of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]] arriving early — which is fine, as long as you name it.
