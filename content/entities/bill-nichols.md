# Bill Nichols

Bill Nichols is named for **documentary modes** in the Component 3 documentary brief. The reference points to Nichols' six modes of documentary.

## What Cambridge attributes to him

> "It would also be useful to introduce the notion of documentary modes, using the work of **Bill Nichols**" (SoW p.20)

The Scheme of Work links to an outside article on "Nichols' 6 modes of documentary." It does not name or list the modes. The attribution is a pointer, not content.

## When it becomes relevant

This matters only if you take the **documentary package** for [[classes/media-studies/unit-plans/9607-course-map|Component 3]]. Cambridge lets a centre limit the choice: "you may decide to offer only one of the Set Briefs outlined in the syllabus, or you may decide to give learners free choice" (SoW p.17).

You may not be offered the documentary brief. The promotion briefs, music and film, fit a five-period week better than documentary or short film, which need much more shooting and editing time. Which briefs run will be confirmed in class.

If the documentary brief does run, the modes are needed. The pitch-feedback prompts ask "What mode would be most appropriate" (SoW p.20), so you cannot pitch without them.

## Where Nichols would sit

Cambridge suggests these documentary research questions (SoW p.20): "What different kinds of documentaries are there? / What is the purpose of a documentary? / What is the audience for documentary?" A modes framework answers exactly these questions. You can also reach the same material by sorting a published documentary list: "What different categories of documentary are listed here? What stories get told? Whose stories get told?" This is an empirical route into the material without leading with the theory.
