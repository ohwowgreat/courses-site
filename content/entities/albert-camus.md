# Albert Camus

**1913 to 1960.**

## The claim in one line

The absurd is not in us and not in the world but **in the meeting** between our demand for clarity and the world's silence, and the honest response is neither despair nor false hope.

## Two quotes

> The absurd is born of the confrontation between the human need and the unreasonable silence of the world.

> We must imagine Sisyphus happy.

Both are used in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|W05]], and not together — the first opens the position, the second tests whether it is livable.

## How the course uses him

Camus is used to test whether the **condition** position is livable. Two questions:

- Camus locates the absurd in the meeting, not in us and not in the world. **Does that change anything?**
- Is *We must imagine Sisyphus happy* convincing, or a rhetorical trick? Is happiness inside the condition the same as accepting a loss?

> [!note] What to be ready for
> Be ready to argue whether **revolt is a defensible philosophical position or a mood dressed as one** — with argument, not feeling.
