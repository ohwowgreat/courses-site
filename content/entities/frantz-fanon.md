# Frantz Fanon

**1925 to 1961.**

## The claim in one line

The look does not merely observe; **it fixes** a person in place, arriving before they speak and deciding in advance what they are.

## Quote

> I was given not one but two, three places.

## How the course uses him

Fanon is paired with [[entities/michel-foucault|Foucault]] to introduce asymmetry:

> Foucault describes a general condition of visibility. Fanon describes a **distributed** one. A theory of visibility that cannot register **who is seen how** is exactly the kind of framework this course dismantles.

Questions to consider:

- Foucault says visibility is a trap. Fanon says invisibility is also one. **Can both be true?**
- Is demanding to be seen a demand for power, or a request for permission?
- **Who has the option of being unmarked** — of being seen as simply a person rather than as a type?

Both easy exits are closed. "The solution is not to be seen" meets *who can afford invisibility, and who is punished by it?* — and "more representation fixes it" meets *does being represented give you the position of the one who shows, or only of the one shown?*

## Set text

The passage from *Black Skin, White Masks* in which he is fixed by a look and a phrase before he has spoken. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-reading-list|the reading list]].
