# Frantz Fanon

**1925 to 1961.**

## The claim in one line

The look does not merely observe. **It fixes** a person in place. It arrives before they speak and decides in advance what they are.

## Key quote

> I was given not one but two, three places.

## How the course uses him

He is paired with [[entities/michel-foucault|Foucault]] to introduce **asymmetry**. Foucault describes a general condition of visibility. Fanon describes a **distributed** one: visibility that falls on different people in different ways. A theory of visibility that cannot register who is seen how is exactly the kind of framework this course takes apart.

The questions that follow from him are the hardest in the course:

- Foucault says visibility is a trap. Fanon says invisibility is also one. **Can both be true?**
- Is demanding to be seen a demand for power, or a request for permission?
- **Who in this room has the option of being unmarked**, of being seen as simply a person rather than as a type?

Both easy exits are closed. One exit says "the solution is not to be seen." That meets a question: who can afford invisibility, and who is punished by it? The other exit says "more representation fixes it." That meets another question: does being represented give you the position of the one who shows, or only of the one shown?

## Set text

The passage from *Black Skin, White Masks* in which he is fixed by a look and a phrase before he has spoken. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-reading-list|the reading list]].
