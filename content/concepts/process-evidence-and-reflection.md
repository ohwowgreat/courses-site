# Process Evidence and Reflection

This page explains how you prove you made your coursework product, and what you say about it afterward. Two of the five C1 marking criteria attach directly to this evidence. A weak evidence layer often caps an otherwise strong product.

> [!note] Which spec applies to you
> G11 sits the 2027 spec: a blog plus a creative critical reflection. G12 sits the 2028–30 spec: a slide portfolio plus a roughly 2000-word evaluative essay. Everything below describes the 2027 regime unless marked. For the crossover, see [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]].

## The blog (2027 spec)

You set up and maintain your own blog, from the start of research: "Learners should set up and maintain a blog outlining the creative processes… The blog should be **set up at the outset of the research stage** and should be **maintained regularly**." In a group, each member keeps a separate blog: "All learners are required to maintain **separate blogs**… These should include details of their individual role within the group task."

### What must be in it

- the process of research, planning and production
- refinement, changes or edits and reflections on key moments
- all individual contributions to group tasks
- evidence of any preliminary exercises
- the final finished product, clearly labeled
- the creative critical reflection, clearly labelled

You must also cover the key areas: **forms and conventions, production contexts, the role of technologies, audiences and representations**.

The process your blog should narrate runs: set up the blog, decide groupings and select the brief, research codes and conventions, storyboard, cast, script, write recce reports and risk assessments, carry out production, reflect and edit, then evaluate in light of the contemporary media landscape.

### Dating and freezing

Update your blog regularly to keep "a **continuous narrative of the production process**." There is no minimum post count or frequency. "Regularly" is as specific as Cambridge gets.

The freeze rule is hard: "**Blogs must be accessible online for the duration of the moderation period. During this period no changes may be made to the portfolios**." Your center presents one hub URL linking every candidate's blog, recorded on the Individual Candidate Record Card.

This is why the BNDS rule is: post dated the day it happens, never backfilled. A blog assembled afterward cannot show a continuous narrative, and you cannot fix it after submission.

### Why a thin blog caps a good product

Research and planning is criterion 4 of the five C1 criteria, and the mark scheme judges the blog, the product, and the reflection together at every level. Level 5 requires research "drawn on to inform the production and explored in the creative critical reflection." Level 2 describes a blog that "may make reference to the research but may not fully engage with it." A strong product with a thin blog cannot reach the top band, because the evidence for the top band lives in the blog.

## Group work

Groups are a maximum of **four** candidates, with "clearly defined roles."

Form the group by skill set, not friendship: "**Each learner in the group should adopt an area of expertise. This does not necessarily mean that they are the only person to work on this element, but that they lead on that part of the production.**" Roles like director, technical, creative, and editor are examples, not a required set.

Authentication is tighter for groups: "Sufficient work must be carried out under **direct supervision** to enable you to authenticate the coursework marks, with confidence. This is particularly important if the production work is carried out as a group." Your teacher comments on your own contribution on the record card.

For the magazine option under the 2027 spec, each group member produces **a unique edition in the same house style**, with "a minimum of four images per candidate."

## The creative critical reflection (C1, 2027 spec)

The creative critical reflection is individual, in a digital format of your choice: "any digital format, such as director commentaries, a presentation with voiceover, podcasts or screencasts." You may use a **different format for each question**.

### The four compulsory questions

1. "How does your product use or challenge conventions and how does it represent social groups or issues?"
2. "How does your product engage with audiences and how would it be distributed as a real media text?"
3. "How did your production skills develop throughout this project?"
4. "How did you integrate technologies – software, hardware and online – in this project?"

Note the shape: Q1 is Language plus Representation, Q2 is Audience plus Industry, Q3 and Q4 are AO3 process. The four questions are the [[concepts/four-key-concepts|Four Key Concepts]] wearing a different hat.

The reflection must also state "the role they adopted if working as part of a group" and "draw on evidence from the research, production and editing stages." It sits **before the final post** in the blog.

### The failure mode Cambridge names

> "there is often a tendency for learners to provide a **blow by blow account** of the production process instead of a genuinely critical evaluation of the final work and how it was developed. One way round this is to ensure that there is a **focus on critical incidents and turning points**"

The prompts that produce turning points: "What were the breakthroughs in developing a story opening? How were particular technical challenges overcome? What new understanding was gained at key points in the process? **Is the final product successful? According to what criteria?**"

That last question is the one most students never answer, and it is where AO2 marks live.

There is **no word count or duration** for the C1 reflection. The 1000–1500 word figure belongs to C3, not C1.

## The C3 critical reflection (2027 spec)

This is "An evaluative essay of around 1000–1500 words," individual "even if learners had collaborated in the creation of their products," guided by four compulsory questions:

1. "How do your products represent social groups or issues?"
2. "How do the elements of your production work together to create a sense of 'branding'?"
3. "How do your products engage with the audience?"
4. "How did your research inform your products and the way they use or challenge conventions?"

Note: the syllabus gives two figures for this essay. Its assessment overview says "around 1000 words," while the detail section says "around 1000–1500 words." Your teacher will confirm which applies in class.

## How it is marked (C1, 2027 spec)

Five criteria, 10 marks each, 50 total:

| Criterion | AO |
|---|---|
| Analyse and evaluate own work | **AO2** (10 marks) |
| Use of media language | AO3 |
| Use of technical skills | AO3 |
| Research, planning and application of key concepts | AO3 |
| Creative Critical Reflection skills | AO3 |

**AO3 Practical Application = 40 marks; AO2 Evaluation = 10 marks.**

Evidence per objective: AO2 is evidenced **solely** by the reflection. AO3 is evidenced by the blog, the finished product, and, at AS only, the reflection as well. So the reflection feeds both objectives at AS. Marking is best-fit against generic grids, with part marks rounded up.

**AO1 is not assessed in the C1 grid at all.** It is taught at this component, but not marked here.

## What changes for G12 (2028–30 spec)

Under the 2028–30 syllabus, the evidence moves from an online blog to a **dated chronological slide portfolio** on Submit for Assessment. The reflection becomes a single **roughly 2000-word evaluative essay**. The coursework AO split changes from AO2 20 / AO3 80 to **AO1 20 / AO2 20 / AO3 60**. C3's third medium becomes a **website home page**. The C1 magazine task becomes **individual only**.

The habits carry over: dated evidence, decisions traceable to research, turning points over blow-by-blow. The format does not. Do not carry blog-shaped assumptions into a slide portfolio.

## A worked C1 exemplar: Candidate A, Level 3

Cambridge publishes a complete Foundation Portfolio Film exemplar on the School Support Hub, openly accessible, marked and commented: **Candidate A**, a **secure Level 3** across most objectives. Fourteen blog pages, five preliminary and nine final-production, ending in the Critical Reflection and Final Draft. It is from the 2021/2027 regime, so it fits G11's C1 and says nothing about G12's slide portfolio.

The examiner's diagnosis inverts the warning above. Candidate A's blog was not thin, and its posts were properly dated and showed progression. It capped at Level 3 because the volume sat in the wrong place: far too much preliminary material and not enough assessed material focused on the set brief, with some preliminary work not relevant to the final outcome at all.

So there are two ways the evidence layer caps a good product, not one:

| Failure | Looks like | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Thin blog | few posts, backfilled, research not carried into the product | the dating rule, already taught |
| **Misweighted blog** | plenty of posts, but the mass sits in preliminary work and off-brief material | **audit the ratio, not just the count** |

The product itself was the strongest element and pulled the grade up. A good film opening does not rescue a misweighted portfolio any more than it rescues a thin one.

### What Cambridge wanted in planning

Planning was the named shortfall: adequate but too narrow. The examiner lists the expected range: **recce reports, casting sessions, scripting and screenplay production, test shots (especially for any effects used), storyboarding, risk assessments.**

Five of these six already appear in the process chain above. **Test shots for effects is the addition:** cheap to run, easy to evidence, and missing from the chain.

### What capped the reflection

Two things, both fixable in teaching rather than in production:

1. **One digital application, used throughout.** The syllabus lets you use a different format per question, and the examiner explicitly wanted a wider creative range of applications. Answering all four questions in one tool leaves AO3 marks on the table for no production reason.
2. **The four questions were answered, but thinly.** The examiner's point is that short responses do not let you demonstrate your understanding of the production process. The diagnosis is **descriptive rather than analytical**.

For more, see [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-07-reading-systematically-toward-the-blog-midpoint|L07]] (the mid-point audit) and [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-10-the-creative-critical-reflection|L10]] (the reflection build). Use it as calibration for [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a3-c1-portfolio|A3]].

## Candidate B: the top of the range, and the one mark it dropped

The same Hub page publishes a second portfolio: **Candidate B**, film brief, marked at the **top of Level 5** across most objectives. Research is called sophisticated with detailed evidence throughout the blog, the product is entirely convincing as a film opening, and the moderator calls it an excellent example of what is expected at this level.

Its planning is the direct answer to Candidate A's shortfall. Where A's planning needed a wider range of tasks, B's is listed as **recce reports, casting information, scripting and storyboarding**: four of the six artefact types Cambridge names. The gap between a Level 3 and a Level 5 portfolio here is not talent. It is which planning documents exist.

### Both exemplars lose the same mark, in the same place

Candidate B drops below top band on exactly one strand: **AO3iv, the reflection strand**, placed at the top of the band below. The reason is the same one the moderator gave for Candidate A: digital creative tools used to limited effect, **only one application employed**.

Two published exemplars, one at Level 3 and one at the top of Level 5, and both are held back by the same avoidable choice. This is the cheapest credit in Component 1:

| | Candidate A (Level 3) | Candidate B (top of Level 5) |
|---|---|---|
| Product | strongest element, pulled the grade up | entirely convincing |
| Research and planning | too narrow; preliminary-heavy | sophisticated; recce, casting, scripting, storyboards |
| Preliminary relevance | far too much, some off-brief | some material not directly connected |
| **Reflection applications** | **one** | **one** |
| Reflection outcome | thin answers, capped | **the only strand below top band** |

The consequence is blunt: you can do everything else at the top of the range and still lose the strand, and the fix costs a single afternoon. Treat the format spread as a requirement, not a suggestion. See [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-10-the-creative-critical-reflection|L10]].
