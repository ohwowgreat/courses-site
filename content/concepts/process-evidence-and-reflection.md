# Process Evidence and Reflection

How you prove you made the thing, and what you say about it afterwards. Two of the five C1 marking criteria attach directly to this material, and the evidence layer is the part of the coursework that most often caps an otherwise strong product.

**Which spec applies to you depends on your year.** G11 sits the 2027 spec (blog + creative critical reflection). G12 sits the 2028–30 spec (slide portfolio + ~2000-word evaluative essay). Everything below is the **2027 regime** unless marked otherwise. See [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]].

## The blog (2027 spec)

**Required, individually, from the start of research.** You set up and maintain a blog outlining the creative processes; it should be **set up at the outset of the research stage** and **maintained regularly** (CWH p.6). Each group member keeps their own: all learners maintain **separate blogs** including details of their individual role in the group task (CWH p.6).

### What must be in it (Syllabus 2027 p.21)

- the process of research, planning and production
- refinement, changes or edits and reflections on key moments
- all individual contributions to group tasks
- evidence of any preliminary exercises
- the final finished product, clearly labeled
- the creative critical reflection, clearly labelled

You should also engage with the key areas: **forms and conventions, production contexts, the role of technologies, audiences and representations** (CWH p.7).

The process chain your blog should narrate (CWH p.9): set up the blog → determine groupings and select the brief → research codes and conventions → storyboarding, casting, scripting, recce reports, risk assessments → carry out production → reflect and edit → evaluate in light of the contemporary media landscape.

### Dating and freezing

Update your blog regularly so it shows a **continuous narrative of the production process** (CWH p.10). There is no minimum post count or frequency — "regularly" is as specific as Cambridge gets.

The freeze rule is hard: **blogs must be accessible online for the duration of the moderation period, and no changes may be made during it** (CWH p.21). Your centre presents one hub URL linking every candidate's blog, recorded on the Individual Candidate Record Card (CWH pp.10, 20).

This is why the BNDS rule is: **post on the day things happen, never backfill.** A blog assembled retrospectively cannot show a continuous narrative, and there is no chance to fix it after submission.

### Why a thin blog caps a good product

Research and planning is criterion 4 of the five C1 criteria, and the mark scheme judges the blog, the product and the reflection **together at every level** (CWH pp.22–23). Level 5 requires research "drawn on to inform the production and explored in the creative critical reflection"; Level 2 describes a blog that "may make reference to the research but may not fully engage with it." A strong product with a thin blog cannot reach the top band, because the evidence for the top band lives in the blog.

## Group work

Maximum **four** candidates (Syllabus 2027 pp.20, 21; CWH p.6), with clearly defined roles.

On forming groups (CWH p.10): consider skill set rather than friendship. **Each learner adopts an area of expertise — not necessarily the only person to work on it, but the one who leads on that part of the production.** Suggested roles — director, technical, creative, editor — are examples, not a required set.

Authentication is tighter for groups: sufficient work must be carried out under **direct supervision** so marks can be authenticated with confidence, and your teacher comments on your individual contribution on the record card (CWH p.8).

For the magazine option under the 2027 spec, each group member produces **a unique edition in the same house style**, with **a minimum of four images per candidate** (Syllabus 2027 p.21).

## The creative critical reflection (C1, 2027 spec)

Individual, in a digital format of your choice — "any digital format, such as director commentaries, a presentation with voiceover, podcasts or screencasts," and you **may use a different format per question** (Syllabus 2027 p.22).

### The four compulsory questions (Syllabus 2027 p.22)

1. "How does your product use or challenge conventions and how does it represent social groups or issues?"
2. "How does your product engage with audiences and how would it be distributed as a real media text?"
3. "How did your production skills develop throughout this project?"
4. "How did you integrate technologies – software, hardware and online – in this project?"

Note the shape: Q1 is Language + Representation, Q2 is Audience + Industry, Q3 and Q4 are AO3 process. The four questions are the [[concepts/four-key-concepts|Four Key Concepts]] in a different form.

The CCR must also state "the role they adopted if working as part of a group" and "draw on evidence from the research, production and editing stages" (CWH p.13). It sits **before the final post** in the blog (CWH p.13).

### The failure mode Cambridge names

> [!warning] Avoid the blow-by-blow account
> "there is often a tendency for learners to provide a **blow by blow account** of the production process instead of a genuinely critical evaluation of the final work and how it was developed. One way round this is to ensure that there is a **focus on critical incidents and turning points**" (SoW p.10, repeated p.12)

Prompts that produce turning points: What were the breakthroughs in developing a story opening? How were particular technical challenges overcome? What new understanding was gained at key points? **Is the final product successful? According to what criteria?**

That last question is the one most students never answer, and it is where AO2 marks live.

**No word count or duration exists for the C1 CCR.** The 1000–1500 word figure belongs to C3, not C1.

## The C3 critical reflection (2027 spec)

An evaluative essay of around **1000–1500 words** (Syllabus 2027 p.25), individual even if you collaborated on the products (CWH p.7), guided by four compulsory questions:

1. "How do your products represent social groups or issues?"
2. "How do the elements of your production work together to create a sense of 'branding'?"
3. "How do your products engage with the audience?"
4. "How did your research inform your products and the way they use or challenge conventions?"

(The syllabus states the length as "around 1000 words" in its overview and "around 1000–1500 words" in the detail section; work to the 1000–1500 figure.)

## How it is marked (C1, 2027 spec)

Five criteria, 10 marks each, 50 total (Syllabus 2027 p.31; CWH pp.22–23):

| Criterion | AO |
|---|---|
| Analyse and evaluate own work | **AO2** (10 marks) |
| Use of media language | AO3 |
| Use of technical skills | AO3 |
| Research, planning and application of key concepts | AO3 |
| Creative Critical Reflection skills | AO3 |

**AO3 Practical Application = 40 marks; AO2 Evaluation = 10 marks** (Syllabus 2027 p.31).

Evidence sources per AO (CWH p.21): AO2 is evidenced **solely** by the reflection; AO3 is evidenced by the blog, the finished product, and — at AS only — the reflection as well. So at AS the CCR feeds both objectives. Marking is best-fit against generic grids, with part marks rounded up. AO1 is not assessed in the C1 grid.

## What changes for G12 (2028–30 spec)

Under the 2028–30 syllabus: evidence moves from an online blog to a **dated chronological slide portfolio** on Submit for Assessment; the reflection becomes a single **~2000-word evaluative essay**; the coursework AO split changes from AO2 20 / AO3 80 to **AO1 20 / AO2 20 / AO3 60**; C3's third medium becomes a **website home page**; and the C1 magazine task becomes **individual only**.

The habits transfer — dated evidence, decisions traceable to research, turning points over blow-by-blow. The format does not. The turning-points advice above applies to the evaluative essay too.

## Two ways the evidence layer caps a good product

A published, marked Foundation Portfolio Film exemplar shows how the blog can cap a good product even when it is not thin. **Candidate A** reached a secure **Level 3**: fourteen blog pages, properly dated and showing progression. It capped because the volume sat in the wrong place — far too much preliminary material and not enough assessed material focused on the set brief, with some preliminary work not relevant to the final outcome at all. The product was the strongest element and pulled the grade up, but a good film opening does not rescue a misweighted portfolio.

So there are two failures to guard against:

| Failure | Looks like | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Thin blog | few posts, backfilled, research not carried into the product | the dating rule |
| **Misweighted blog** | plenty of posts, but the mass sits in preliminary work and off-brief material | **audit the ratio, not just the count** |

### What Cambridge wanted in planning

Planning was the named shortfall — adequate but too narrow. The expected range: **recce reports, casting sessions, scripting and screenplay production, test shots (especially for any effects used), storyboarding, risk assessments.** Five of these already appear in the CWH p.9 process chain above; **test shots for effects** is the addition — cheap to run and easy to evidence.

### What capped the reflection

1. **Only one digital application, used throughout.** The syllabus permits a different format per question, and the examiner wanted a wider creative range of applications. Answering all four questions in one tool leaves AO3 marks on the table for no production reason.
2. **The four questions were answered thinly.** Short responses do not let you demonstrate your understanding of the production process — descriptive rather than analytical.

## The top of the range — and the one mark it dropped

A second portfolio from the same source, **Candidate B**, was marked at the **top of Level 5**: sophisticated research with detailed evidence throughout the blog, and a product entirely convincing as a film opening. Its planning is the answer to Candidate A's shortfall — **recce reports, casting information, scripting and storyboarding**, four of the six artefact types Cambridge names. The gap between Level 3 and Level 5 here is not talent; it is which planning documents exist.

Both exemplars lose the same mark in the same place: the **CCR strand (AO3iv)**, for using digital creative tools to limited effect — **only one application employed**.

| | Candidate A (Level 3) | Candidate B (top of Level 5) |
|---|---|---|
| Product | strongest element, pulled the grade up | entirely convincing |
| Research and planning | too narrow; preliminary-heavy | sophisticated; recce, casting, scripting, storyboards |
| Preliminary relevance | far too much, some off-brief | some material not directly connected |
| **CCR applications** | **one** | **one** |
| CCR outcome | thin answers, capped | **the only strand below top band** |

You can do everything else at the top of the range and still lose this strand. Use more than one digital format across your CCR questions — see [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-10-the-creative-critical-reflection|L10]].
