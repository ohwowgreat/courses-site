# Linear Perspective

Linear perspective is a datable invention that came to look like the structure of vision itself. It is a key example in the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]].

> Perspective does not show the world. It shows the world **from a point.** It assumes a single, motionless, monocular viewer, and it converts that assumption into geometry so convincing that for centuries it read as simply how things look.

## The philosophical payoff

When a perspective becomes **invisible as a perspective**, we mistake it for the world.

That is [[concepts/naturalization|naturalization]] made visible. You can watch it happen rather than being told about it: here is the construction, here is the date, here is the assumption it encodes. Other cases of naturalization in the course (the map, the video-game boundary, the category) have to be argued for; this one can be demonstrated.

## A question to think about

> Is perspective a **discovery** about vision, or an **invention** that trained vision?

Follow-ups from [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w04-is-there-a-reality-behind-appearances|W04]]: *perspective does not show us the world, it shows us the world from a point — what is the difference?* and *if a perspective becomes invisible as a perspective, what do we think we are looking at?*

## Where it comes from in the course

Not from art history. It arrives via [[entities/hito-steyerl|Steyerl]] in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w02-do-images-represent-reality-or-produce-it|W02]] and is developed in W04 alongside Plato — so you meet it as a claim about images and access before you meet it as a claim about vision.
