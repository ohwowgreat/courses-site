# Situated Knowledge

**[[entities/donna-haraway|Donna Haraway]]'s answer to the choice between objectivity and relativism**, and a useful counter-move in the final weeks of the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]].

## The argument

The view from nowhere and the view from everywhere are **the same evasion**, because both refuse responsibility for a position.

Knowledge is always from somewhere, and admitting where does not weaken the claim. **It makes the claim accountable.**

## Why it matters

By week 13 you can see that two positions are both bad: "science is neutral" and "everything is subjective." Situated knowledge is a third option you can actually defend:

> A partial view that names its own location is better than a total view that does not — and **better is a real word here rather than a rhetorical one.**

That last clause is what makes the concept do work in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]]. The session's first question asks what distinguishes a good frame from a bad one if closeness to an unframed reality is unavailable. Situated knowledge supplies a criterion — accountability of position — that does not require the unframed reality the course has spent fourteen weeks removing.

## The pressure it still faces

It does not dissolve W14's fourth question. If every position is situated, the claim that every position is situated is also situated, and the regress is the same one. What it changes is the consequence: on Haraway's account the regress is not a problem to be escaped but the reason for naming your location, which is a livable answer rather than a resolution. Whether that is enough is a genuinely open question.
