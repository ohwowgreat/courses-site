# Situated Knowledge

**[[entities/donna-haraway|Donna Haraway]]'s answer to the choice between objectivity and relativism**, and a useful counter-move in the final weeks of the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]].

## The argument

The view from nowhere and the view from everywhere are **the same evasion**. Both refuse responsibility for a position.

Knowledge is always from somewhere. Admitting where does not weaken the claim. **It makes the claim accountable.**

## Why it matters

By week 13 you can be stuck between two positions you can both see are bad: "science is neutral" and "everything is subjective." **Situated knowledge** is a third option, and one you can actually defend:

> A partial view that names its own location is better than a total view that does not. And **better is a real word here rather than a rhetorical one.**

That last point is what makes the concept do work in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]]. The session's first question asks what tells a good frame from a bad one if closeness to an unframed reality is unavailable. Situated knowledge gives you a test, accountability of position, that does not need the unframed reality the course has spent fourteen weeks removing.

## The pressure it still faces

It does not dissolve W14's fourth question. If every position is situated, then the claim that every position is situated is also situated, and the regress is the same one. What it changes is the consequence. On Haraway's account the regress is not a problem to escape but the reason for naming your location. That is a livable answer rather than a resolution. Whether it is enough stays an open question.
