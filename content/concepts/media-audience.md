# Media Audience

**Key Concept 4.** "How audiences are constructed and addressed by media texts and how audiences interpret and respond to media texts" (Syllabus 2027 p.15).

This Key Concept has two parts: audiences as **constructed by industry**, and audiences as **active interpreters**.

## What the syllabus requires

You study (Syllabus 2027 p.15):

- how audiences are grouped and categorized by media industries, including by age, gender and social class, as well as by lifestyle and taste
- how media producers target, attract, reach, address and construct audiences
- how media industries target audiences through the content and appeal of media products and through the ways in which they are marketed, distributed and consumed
- the interrelationship between media technologies and patterns of consumption and response
- how audiences interpret the media, including how they may interpret the same texts in different ways, using a variety of theories or ideologies
- how audiences interact with the media and can be actively involved in media production
- how specialized audiences can be reached, on a local, national and global scale, through different media technologies and platforms
- how media organizations reflect the different needs of mass and specialized audiences, including through targeting
- how audiences use media in different ways, reflecting demographic factors as well as aspects of identity and culture
- the role and significance of specialized audiences, including niche and fan, to the media

## Audiences as constructed

**Categorization**: grouping audiences by age, gender, social class, lifestyle and taste.

**Mode of address**: how a text encourages a reader to recognize themselves as the addressee. The Scheme of Work defines it in the magazine section: "how is a particular audience being addressed by these elements? How is a potential reader encouraged to see her/himself as the addressee?" (SoW p.9). Magazine covers show this well, because the address is on the surface.

**Mass and specialized audiences**: the syllabus's own pairing. The Scheme of Work reaches it through the magazine pitch: "an argument, perhaps, that a niche rather than mass market exists for the publication" (SoW p.12).

**Niche and fan audiences**: named by the syllabus as significant, and reachable globally through platforms.

## Audiences as active interpreters

The syllabus requires that you understand how audiences "may interpret the same texts in different ways, using a variety of theories or ideologies" (p.15). It names no theory.

The Scheme of Work supplies one, **Stuart Hall's** ideas of encoding and decoding:

> "explore Stuart Hall's ideas of encoding and decoding when making sense of a media text" (SoW p.14)

See [[entities/stuart-hall|Stuart Hall]] for the dominant / negotiated / oppositional model. At BNDS you study these three positions. One way to see them clearly: take a text the class disagrees about, then map the actual readings onto the three positions.

## The media diary

> "**Exercise: Keep a media diary for one day – list everything you watched, listened to, played, interacted with as well as where you did it, using what technology, whether alone or in company, and for what purpose.**"

Record these dimensions over exactly one day: what, where, on what technology, alone or with others, and why.

Its purpose is forward-looking (SoW p.16): "This simple exercise starts to build the foundations for the **Media Ecology** element of **Component 4** – it starts to reveal the '**mediascape**' that we all inhabit." Diaries you keep in Grade 11 become the raw contrast material for the Grade 12 synoptic essay. Keep them.

The seven insights the diary is expected to surface (SoW p.16): media consumable anywhere on mobile technology; media on demand; limited live viewing except events and sport; social media creating a new communal live experience; news as a stream of updates rather than a fixed form, place and time; "old media" competing and integrating rather than dying; and near-universal availability via piracy.

## The attention economy

You can go beyond describing your media use and consider conceptual positions such as the **attention economy** (SoW p.16). Ask what it is and "how does it explain how I use Facebook?" Then look at "the actual economy of intellectual and artistic property," which connects to work on regulation in Component 4.

The component closes with a short question: **"What media are worth paying for? Why?"** (SoW p.16).
