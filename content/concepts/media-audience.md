# Media Audience

**Key Concept 4.** "How audiences are constructed and addressed by media texts and how audiences interpret and respond to media texts" (Syllabus 2027 p.15).

Key Concept 4 has two clauses. They split the study cleanly: audiences as **constructed by industry**, and audiences as **active interpreters**.

## What the syllabus requires

You study (Syllabus 2027 p.15):

- how audiences are grouped and categorised by media industries, including by age, gender and social class, as well as by lifestyle and taste
- how media producers target, attract, reach, address and construct audiences
- how media industries target audiences through the content and appeal of media products and through the ways in which they are marketed, distributed and consumed
- the interrelationship between media technologies and patterns of consumption and response
- how audiences interpret the media, including how they may interpret the same texts in different ways, using a variety of theories or ideologies
- how audiences interact with the media and can be actively involved in media production
- how specialised audiences can be reached, on a local, national and global scale, through different media technologies and platforms
- how media organisations reflect the different needs of mass and specialised audiences, including through targeting
- how audiences use media in different ways, reflecting demographic factors as well as aspects of identity and culture
- the role and significance of specialised audiences, including niche and fan, to the media

## Audiences as constructed

**Categorization**: audiences grouped by age, gender, social class, lifestyle and taste.

**Mode of address**: how a text encourages a reader to recognize themselves as the addressee. "How is a particular audience being addressed by these elements? How is a potential reader encouraged to see her/himself as the addressee?" Magazine covers show this clearly, because the address is on the surface.

**Mass and specialized audiences**: the syllabus's own pairing. You can argue that a niche rather than mass market exists for a publication.

**Niche and fan audiences**: named by the syllabus as significant, and reachable globally through platforms.

## Audiences as active interpreters

The syllabus requires you to understand how audiences "may interpret the same texts in different ways, using a variety of theories or ideologies" (p.15). It names no theory.

You study **Stuart Hall's** ideas of encoding and decoding when making sense of a media text. See [[entities/stuart-hall|Stuart Hall]] for the dominant / negotiated / oppositional model.

One way to test the model: take a text the class genuinely disagrees about, then map the actual readings onto the three positions. Your own argument becomes the evidence.

## The media diary

> "**Exercise: Keep a media diary for one day – list everything you watched, listened to, played, interacted with as well as where you did it, using what technology, whether alone or in company, and for what purpose.**"

Six recorded dimensions: what, where, on what technology, alone or with others, and why, over exactly one day.

Its purpose looks forward: "This simple exercise starts to build the foundations for the **Media Ecology** element of **Component 4** – it starts to reveal the '**mediascape**' that we all inhabit." Diaries you keep in Grade 11 become the raw contrast material for the Grade 12 synoptic essay. Keep them.

The seven insights it is meant to surface: media consumable anywhere on mobile technology; media on demand; limited live viewing except events and sport; social media creating a new communal live experience; news as a stream of updates rather than a fixed form, place and time; "old media" competing and integrating rather than dying; and near-universal availability via piracy.

## The attention economy

The diary can go past description to consider conceptual positions, such as **the attention economy**. The extension asks what it is and "how does it explain how I use Facebook?", then turns to "the actual economy of intellectual and artistic property (which will call forwards to work on regulation in Component 4)."

The component closes with a short question: **"What media are worth paying for? Why?"**
