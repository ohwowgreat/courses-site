# Technical Elements

This is the most directly examinable content in 9607: the four-part toolkit for reading moving image. You practice it all year, you are tested on it directly in **Component 2 Section A**, and it doubles as the vocabulary of production.

The syllabus frames it as a means, not an end (Syllabus 2027 p.16):

> "Candidates should study a range of texts so that they can discuss, in response to the question, how the following technical elements create specific representations of individuals/groups/events/places, and help to articulate specific messages and values that have social significance."

**Naming alone earns little.** The mark is in technique → meaning → representation. This is the single most repeated correction in the whole course.

Every element exercise routes technique through representation of **class, age, gender, ethnicity**: the technique → meaning → representation chain in practice. For the theorists this uses, see [[concepts/representation|Representation]].

## The four elements, per the syllabus (p.16)

### Camera: shots, angle, movement, composition

- **shots**: establishing, master, close-up, mid-shot, long, wide, two-shot, aerial, point-of-view, over-the-shoulder, "and variations of these"
- **angle**: high, low, canted
- **movement**: pan, tilt, track, dolly, crane, steadicam, hand-held, zoom, reverse zoom
- **composition**: framing, rule of thirds, depth of field (deep and shallow focus), focus pulls

Learn these as meaning pairs, not as a list: a low angle grants power, a canted angle signals dissonance, shallow focus isolates, hand-held destabilizes.

### Sound

- diegetic and non-diegetic, synchronous/asynchronous, sound effects, sound motif, sound bridge, dialogue, voiceover, mode of address / direct address, sound mixing, sound perspective
- **soundtrack**: score, incidental music, themes and stings, ambient sound

Sound is often the weakest element in scripts, so it rewards targeted work. Build a vocabulary of sound terminology (SoW p.13):

> "Sound is often neglected, so it is worth doing some targeted work on this, in particularly helping learners to develop a vocabulary of sound terminology, e.g. harmony, dissonance, discordance, sound bed, rhythm, crescendo, pitch, timbre."

Because sound is neglected across the candidature, it is the easiest way for your script to stand out.

### Mise-en-scène

- **production design**: location, studio, set design, costume and make-up, properties
- lighting, colour design

> [!important] What is not here
> **Acting and performance are not listed under mise-en-scène.** The syllabus states separately that "acting is not a skill that is assessed in media studies" (p.30). Body language and expression are also absent from the syllabus bullet.

This is a high-yield element: costume, lighting, and color are visible, easy to name, and rich in connotation.

### Editing

"This includes transition of image and sound – continuity and non-continuity systems."

- **cutting**: shot/reverse shot, eyeline match, graphic match, action match, jump cut, crosscutting, parallel editing, cutaway, insert
- **other transitions**: dissolve, fade-in, fade-out, wipe, superimposition, long take, short take, slow motion, ellipsis and expansion of time, post-production, visual effects

> [!important] Filed under Editing
> **Long take, short take, and slow motion are filed under Editing**, not Camera. They are easy to misfile.

Editing is invisible until you make it visible. The cut-counting exercise in [[concepts/the-viewing-protocol|The Viewing Protocol]] does that.

## How it is examined

Component 2 Section A: an unseen extract "from a TV drama and will last for a maximum of five minutes. It will be played four times. The screening time will last a total of 30 minutes, including note-taking" (Syllabus 2027 p.23). Then 45 minutes of writing, 25 marks.

The syllabus also notes that not every element appears equally in every extract (p.23):

> "It is acknowledged that not every one of the technical areas will feature in equal measure in any given extract and other areas may also be credited where appropriate… We do not expect each aspect to be covered in the same degree of detail, but as appropriate to the genre and extract provided and to the discussion of the extract's representation."

So your script does not need to cover all four elements evenly. Cover what the extract actually does, and tie each observation to representation.

## The analytical paragraph frame

The expected shape of an analytical paragraph (SoW p.14): technique named → terminology → meaning created → link to representation → link to the question.

This frame is direct rehearsal for A1 and A4 in [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-assessments|the S1 register]], and CS7, CS10, and CS12 practice it.
