# View from Nowhere

**Thomas Nagel's phrase for the aspiration to a perspective that belongs to no particular position: complete, unlocated, unframed.**

The [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]] uses it as its closing question, and it names the course throughline in five words.

## Two live answers

**No, and the demand is a category error.** Asking for an unframed view is asking to see without being anywhere. That is not a failure we suffer — it is an incoherent request.

**No, but the aspiration still does work.** Frames can be compared even without access to an unframed reality: by their consequences, by what they let us do, by whose costs they hide. See [[concepts/situated-knowledge|Situated Knowledge]].

The question is genuinely open, and the course does not pick.

## The final trap

> Is "everything is a framework" itself a framework? **If yes, why accept it. If no, you have just produced the view from nowhere.**

This is the fourth question in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]] and the reason the session exists.

> [!note] Holding an open question
> The aim is to hold the paradox without either collapsing into relativism or fleeing back to naive realism. That capacity — holding a genuinely open question without needing to close it — is precisely what an Oxbridge interviewer is testing for.

## What the phrase collects

By W14 the term is doing retrospective work — every framework the course examined is an instance of a view **from somewhere** that presented itself as a view from nowhere:

the map · [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]] · the body as medium · machine cognition · the categories that decide who counts · and, if the fourth question lands, the course itself.

## About the phrase

The phrase is borrowed from Nagel; the concept as taught is the course's own. If you go looking for Nagel's *The View from Nowhere* (1986), you will find an argument with a different shape.
