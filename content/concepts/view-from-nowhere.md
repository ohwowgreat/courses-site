# View from Nowhere

**Thomas Nagel's phrase for the aspiration to a perspective that belongs to no particular position: complete, unlocated, unframed.**

The [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]] uses it as its closing question. It "names the course throughline in five words."

## Two live answers

**No, and the demand is a category error.** Asking for an unframed view is asking to see without being anywhere. That is not a failure we suffer. It is an incoherent request.

**No, but the aspiration still does work.** You can compare frames even without access to an unframed reality: by their consequences, by what they let you do, by whose costs they hide. See [[concepts/situated-knowledge|Situated Knowledge]].

The question is genuinely open, and the course does not pick a side.

## The final trap

> Is "everything is a framework" itself a framework? If yes, why accept it. If no, you have just produced the view from nowhere.

This is the fourth question in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]], and the reason the session exists. The course does not resolve it.

The aim is a room that can hold the paradox without either collapsing into relativism or fleeing back to naive realism. Holding a genuinely open question without needing to close it is precisely what an Oxbridge interviewer tests for.

## What the phrase collects

By W14 the term is doing retrospective work. Every framework the course examined is an instance of a view **from somewhere** that presented itself as a view from nowhere:

the map · [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]] · the body as medium · machine cognition · the categories that decide who counts · and, if the fourth question lands, the course itself.

## A note on the source

Nagel is named here for the phrase only. No Nagel text is assigned or read in this course. The concept as taught is the course's own, with the phrase borrowed. If you go looking for his book *The View from Nowhere* (1986), you will find an argument with a different shape.
