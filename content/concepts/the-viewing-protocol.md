# The Viewing Protocol

The **Viewing Protocol** is the course's core analytical method. Use it from week one: break an extract into its component parts by isolating one element at a time, then reassemble them.

The point of this is to reach insights into how a TV drama extract produces particular meanings.

## The four passes

You watch the same short extract several times. Each pass isolates one element.

| Pass | What runs | What you focus on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full extract, ~5 min | Nothing yet: no response is required |
| 2 | Sound down | Mise-en-scène |
| 3 | Sound still off | Camera: shots, angles, movement, composition |
| 4 | Screen covered or blanked | Sound |

For the sound pass, work through these prompts:

- What do we hear?
- Is it diegetic or non-diegetic?
- What mood is created?
- How could the music be described?
- To whom are the voices addressed?

## The editing pass

Editing gets its own move, because it is the element you cannot see. Count out loud, or tap, whenever there is a visual transition. This simple exercise makes something potentially "invisible" become visible.

Then ask:

- Is it a cut or a mix/dissolve?
- What is the effect of this?
- What visual elements are brought together?
- Does it create continuity? Contrast?
- Does it reveal something about character?
- Does it develop the story?

## The reassembly

The protocol is not finished until the parts go back together. Finally, bring all these elements together:

- What is the function of this opening?
- How does it set up the beginning of a story?
- How does it establish character, mood and setting?
- What kinds of competence does it require of the audience?
- How are particular representations of individuals, groups, events and places constructed through the technical elements identified?

The last question turns description into exam marks. It is where the protocol hands off to [[concepts/representation|Representation]].

## Choosing the extract

- Openings of first episodes usually work well, because they establish setting, tone, and character, and set up some narrative expectations.
- A range of examples helps, including ones that have previously featured in examinations, such as **The Handmaid's Tale**. Building expertise in what to look for and how to say it is an iterative process.

Using past exam extracts makes the practice authentic and matches you to the real difficulty level.

## Output

This work is formative. You practice the analytical process, possibly in groups and then present your findings, and/or individually in a short-form essay. Group analysis presented, then individual short-form writing: frequent and low stakes.

## Why it is worth the ritual

1. It is direct rehearsal for **Component 2 Section A**, where the extract is screened four times. Four screenings is the exam's own version of this protocol. If you have practiced element-isolation, you know what to do with each pass.
2. It rescues sound, the element most often neglected. A dedicated screen-covered pass is the only reliable way to make yourself hear.
3. It continues from C1: the approaches and terminology are the same as your analytical work for the Foundation Portfolio. The same protocol serves research for the portfolio and analysis for the exam.
