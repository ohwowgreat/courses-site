# The Gap

**The distance between a representation and the thing represented, which can be narrowed but not closed.**

The organizing term of [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2]] of the [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge Discussion Group]], and the concept the rest of the course argues from.

## Established in three moves

- **Measurement.** The thermometer changes the water. Observation changes the double slit result. **Measuring is an interaction, and interactions have two sides.**
- **Language.** Cold means different things to different speakers. Some words do not translate.
- **Perception.** Nothing reaches you except through a body that processes it.

## Beyond the first conclusion

A common conclusion is that science narrows the gap and language widens it. That is correct but insufficient. The real question follows:

> Is narrowing progress **toward** something, given that the target cannot be inspected?

That question turns an epistemological observation into [[concepts/the-ontological-turn|an ontological one]], and it is why [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w03-can-measurement-reach-the-thing-itself|W03]] deliberately does not resolve.

## Three positions on it

These are not three answers to be graded, but three stances you can adopt and defend:

| Position | The claim | The pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss** | There is a real world and we are locked out of it | Locked out implies you know what is on the other side. How? |
| **Liberation** | If there is no fixed reality, we get to make one | Make one out of what? |
| **Condition** | It is just what having a mind is like | If it is simply the condition, why does it feel like a loss to so many people? |

Worked in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|W05]] against Sartre, Camus and Heidegger. Heidegger's contribution threatens the term itself: if the framework is what makes understanding **possible** rather than what blocks it, does the word "gap" still fit? **Is a lens an obstacle to sight?**

## The point to hold

> The claim is not that measurement is unreliable. It is that measurement is an interaction, and an interaction has two sides. **Precision and disturbance are not opposites; the more precisely you interact, the more you interact.**

This is not the same as "everything is subjective." That reading makes the concept useless — it removes the difference between narrowing the gap and ignoring it.
