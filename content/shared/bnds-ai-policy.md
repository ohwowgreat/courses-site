# BNDS AI Policy

School-wide, from the **A-Level Student Guide 2025-26 §VI** (pp.22–24), inside the Academic Honesty Policy. It applies to every course.

The policy does not simply permit or ban AI. It defines **six graded levels**, and each assignment declares which level applies.

## The six levels (Guide pp.23–24)

| Level | Name | What is allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Human-Only Work | You produce everything without AI help. No disclosure required |
| **1** | AI for Ideation | AI only to clarify the task, surface key terms, or map existing knowledge. Not to generate fresh concepts. You still do your own brainstorming. Typical: definitions, locating foundational references, checking feasibility, keyword lists for literature searches |
| **2** | AI for Structuring | Level 1, plus AI organizes material: outlines, templates, flowcharts. No full paragraphs, code blocks, calculations or images generated |
| **3** | AI for Refinement | Levels 1–2, plus AI edits or polishes a human draft: grammar, style, citation format, code linting. Keep a before-and-after or tracked-changes file showing what was accepted |
| **4** | AI Co-Creation | Levels 1–3, plus you and AI share the work. Substantial parts AI-generated or transformed, then shaped by you. Track who did what and cite the model and version |
| **5** | AI-Generated for Review | Levels 1–4, plus the whole piece is AI-produced, submitted only so humans can critique or annotate it. Label it "AI-generated". Grading focuses on your analysis, not the AI text |

> *"Always check assignment AI Level"* (Guide p.23). Some assignments ban AI entirely: the handbook names exams and personal reflections. Others actively encourage it.

## Attribution requirements (Guide p.22)

Whenever you use AI, you must:

1. **Cite the tool**, for example "Generated with assistance from [AI Tool Name]"
2. **Explain how you used it**, for example "Used for brainstorming but all analysis is my own"
3. **Cite the prompt** you used

Instructors may also require a disclaimer or a reflection on AI use.

## Permitted and prohibited (Guide p.22)

**Permitted:** assisting learning (brainstorming, clarifying concepts, summarizing readings, generating practice questions); enhancing productivity (grammar checks, citation formatting, organizing research); encouraging creativity (AI output as a *starting point* for original work, with attribution); developing digital literacy.

**Prohibited:** submitting AI-generated work as your own without citation or permission; using AI to replace independent analysis, original thought or personal reflection unless explicitly allowed; spreading unverified AI content. You "must verify AI-generated content for accuracy."

Misuse follows the Academic Honesty Policy: resubmission, grade penalties, or disciplinary action (Guide p.23).

## The Art & Design worked example (Guide p.24)

The handbook's only subject-specific illustration is for Art & Design:

- **Level 1:** AI summarizes Bauhaus color theory so you can plan palette experiments
- **Level 2:** AI offers a clean portfolio layout grid (page order, caption placeholders, margins) that you fill with original work
- **Level 3:** AI polishes the grammar of an artist statement and reorganizes paragraphs for flow
- **Level 4:** Midjourney, Stable Diffusion or similar generates an image that you then collage, annotate and contextualize, detailing the AI's role in the process

## ⚠ Where school policy meets exam-board policy

These are separate rules, and the exam board is the stricter authority on assessed work. You must satisfy both.

| | Position |
|---|---|
| **BNDS** | Six levels; Level 4 co-creation explicitly permitted, including generated images in Art & Design, with attribution |
| **Cambridge 9479 (2028-30)** | AI-generated images permitted **only** with the candidate's own role demonstrated and justified — see [[classes/a-level-art-design/a-level-art-design]] |
| **Cambridge 9607 (2027)** | *"Cambridge International has a policy on the use of generative AI by candidates in coursework. The inappropriate use of AI should be treated as a form of plagiarism"* (Syllabus 2027 p.30). The 2027 spec does not otherwise address AI; the 2028-30 spec permits it with documented prompts and justification |

**The practical rule for coursework:** BNDS Level 4 may be allowed for classroom tasks, but anything going to Cambridge as assessed coursework is governed by the syllabus for the exam year. Where they differ, the syllabus wins. The handbook's own attribution requirements (cite the tool, explain the use, cite the prompt) are exactly what the 2028-30 Art & Design spec asks for, so satisfying BNDS properly also satisfies Cambridge.

⚠ This is the **2025-26** edition of the handbook. The AI policy may change in later editions, so check the current edition before relying on the level definitions.
