# BNDS Assessment Framework

This is the school-wide grading scheme from the **BNDS A-Level Student Guide 2025-26**. Every course uses it. This page is the single reference, so course pages do not restate it.

This is the current edition. Its rules stay valid except where they touch syllabus specifics, which change per subject and per exam year. Treat the framework, curving, notice, retake, and AI rules as live. Check anything syllabus-shaped against your syllabus.

## Grade composition

| Category | Weight | Definition (per handbook) |
|---|---|---|
| **Attainment** | **50%** | Major, summative. Syllabus content only, sat under exam conditions, curved to CIE past-paper data. Should give a reliable Forecast Grade. Project completions and portfolio milestones count. |
| **Course Skills** | **20%** | Minor, formative. Skills and content relevant to the syllabus. Flexible format: quizzes, drafts, practice tasks, homework. |
| **Learning Behaviour** | **10%** | Glossary upkeep, blog punctuality, screening and feedback participation, case-study contributions, correcting work, submitting on time. |
| **End of Term Exam** | **20%** | Summative, on previously covered content, sat in the school exam window (S1 2026-27: Mon 2027-01-11 – Mon 2027-01-18). |

## Notice rule

You get **at least one week's notice** before an Attainment (Guide p.13). For non-attainment assessments you get **72 hours**.

## Standing rules

- **4–6 attainments per semester.** None fall in the first two teaching weeks.
- **Attainment dropping.** One grade may be dropped per semester, late in the semester, but only if the class has at least 6 attainment grades in MB, and you have missed none, have no plagiarism or cheating record, no unexcused absences, are always on time, and attend all required grade and programme meetings (Guide p.12). All four courses here run four attainments, so no student in any of them is eligible. This is a result of course design, not of your conduct.
- **One grade per week** is posted on MB for each subject (Guide p.12). Learning Behaviour postings fill weeks with no CS task or attainment.
- **Marking is returned within 5 working days** (Guide p.12).
- **Homework is capped at 30 minutes per subject per day** (Guide p.13). Break homework is about 3–4 hours per ten-day holiday.
- **Extensions** must be requested at least 24 hours before the deadline (Guide p.13).
- **Absence from an assessment** means you must contact the subject teacher 24 hours before the due time, and illness needs a doctor's note. ⚠ *"If no communication is made with the subject teacher in advance you may be given 0, even if this is due to illness"* (Guide p.8).
- **Late-work cutoff:** *"three weeks before the last school day of the semester"* (Guide p.13). For S1 this date falls inside the winter break, so the exact cutoff will be confirmed. See [[calendar|school academic calendar]].
- **Assessment content:** only what has been taught and practised. *"You should have seen similar content or formats beforehand,"* though "the context in which the knowledge or skills are applied may be novel" (Guide p.9).
- Every formative (CS) task next to an attainment is **direct practice for it**.
- **Seniors get a reduced assessment load** in the week before the 1 November and 1 December university deadlines (Guide p.13).

## Curving and grade boundaries

Attainments are **not recorded as raw marks** (Guide pp.11–12):

- They are curved against **CIE past-paper data**, "allowing for where students are on their learning trajectory."
- Curves are more generous in G10 and stricter in G11 and later in the year. This applies to [[classes/pre-a-level-art-design/pre-a-level-art-design|PAL]], the G10 course here.
- Grades "reflect your learning trajectory, not what you would score on a CIE exam today."
- Past-paper grade thresholds are "the best starting point" for setting boundaries.
- **Moderation** is required where an attainment runs across multiple classes taught by different teachers. The Head of Department checks large between-class differences and may adjust curves or boundaries.
- *"You will always be told how grade boundaries and curves are decided."*

An attainment "should make a reliable **Forecast Grade**" (Guide p.11).

## Grade conversion (Guide p.11)

| CIE scale | A-Level grade | BNDS GPA |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | A* | 4.3 |
| 80–89 | A | 4.0 |
| 70–79 | B | 3.5 |
| 60–69 | C | 3.0 |
| 50–59 | D | 2.5 |
| 40–49 | E | 2.0 |
| below 40 | U | 0 |

**Failing is below 50%** (Guide p.15). A grade **at or below 70%** triggers the **Academic Concern Steps**: your advisor and the A-Level Director are informed, and support measures follow (Guide p.13).

## Retake policy (Guide §IV, pp.15–16)

You get one opportunity to retake a failed course, in the holiday after the semester, with 2–3 teacher meetings for support. The work is "just as challenging" and similar in quantity to the original. The grading policy is explained before finals and cannot be changed once agreed. You must sign an academic-honesty declaration. Plagiarism means immediate failure with no resubmission.

**A passed retake earns the equivalent of a D**, not the grade you actually achieved. A failed retake leaves the original grade standing. Under force majeure, more than 50% completion plus written, parent-signed evidence can pass. Without evidence, you fail.

Graduating seniors get only **two weeks** for a retake, because their Summer Holiday starts early.
