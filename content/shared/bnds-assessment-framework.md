# BNDS Assessment Framework

This is the school-wide grading scheme from the **BNDS A-Level Student Guide 2025-26**. Every course applies it. This page is the single reference, so course pages do not restate it.

This is the 2025-26 edition, and it is the current one. Its rules stay valid except where they touch syllabus specifics, which change by subject and by exam year. Treat the grading framework, curving, notice, and retake rules as current. Check anything syllabus-specific against your syllabus.

## Grade composition

| Category | Weight | Definition (per handbook) |
|---|---|---|
| **Attainment** | **50%** | Major, summative. Syllabus content only, sat under exam conditions, curved to CIE past-paper data. Should give a reliable Forecast Grade. Project completions and portfolio milestones count. |
| **Course Skills** | **20%** | Minor, formative. Skills and content relevant to the syllabus, flexible format: quizzes, drafts, practice tasks, homework. |
| **Learning Behaviour** | **10%** | Glossary upkeep, blog punctuality, screening/feedback participation, case-study contributions, correcting work, submitting on time. |
| **End of Term Exam** | **20%** | Summative, on previously covered content, sat in the school exam window (S1 2026-27: Mon 2027-01-11 – Mon 2027-01-18). |

## Notice rule

You get **at least one week's notice** of an attainment (AL Student Guide p.13, *"at least one week in advance"*). Non-attainment assessments carry **72 hours' notice**.

## Standing rules

- **4–6 attainments per semester** (Assessment Plan C27). None fall in the first two teaching weeks.
- **Attainment dropping.** One attainment may be dropped per semester, late in the semester, but only if the class has ≥6 attainment grades in MB, and you have missed none, have no plagiarism or cheating record, no unexcused absences, are always on time, and attend all required grade and programme meetings (Guide p.12). All four courses run four attainments, so no student is eligible to drop one.
- **One grade per week** posted on MB (Guide p.12). Learning Behaviour postings fill weeks with no CS task or attainment.
- **Marking returned within 5 working days** (Guide p.12).
- **Homework capped at 30 minutes per subject per day** (Guide p.13). Break homework is about 3–4 hours per ten-day holiday.
- **Extensions** must be requested at least 24 hours before the deadline (Guide p.13).
- **Absence from an assessment:** contact your subject teacher at least 24 hours before the due time, and bring a doctor's note for illness. ⚠ *"If no communication is made with the subject teacher in advance you may be given 0, even if this is due to illness"* (Guide p.8).
- **Late-work cutoff:** *"three weeks before the last school day of the semester"* (Guide p.13). The exact S1 date falls inside the winter break and will be confirmed. See [[calendar|school academic calendar]].
- **Assessment content:** only what has been taught and practiced. *"You should have seen similar content or formats beforehand"*, though *"the context in which the knowledge or skills are applied may be novel"* (Guide p.9).
- A CS task set next to an attainment is direct practice for it.
- Mid-term report grades in S1 are due Thu 2026-10-29.
- **Seniors get a reduced assessment load** in the week before the 1 Nov and 1 Dec university deadlines (Guide p.13).

## Curving and grade boundaries

Attainments are **not recorded as raw marks** (Guide pp.11–12):

- They are curved against **CIE past-paper data**, "allowing for where students are on their learning trajectory."
- Curves are **more generous in G10, stricter in G11** and later in the year.
- Grades "reflect your learning trajectory, not what you would score on a CIE exam today."
- Past-paper grade thresholds are "the best starting point" for setting boundaries.
- **Moderation is required** where an attainment runs across several classes taught by different teachers. The Head of Department checks large between-class differences and may adjust curves or boundaries.
- *"You will always be told how grade boundaries and curves are decided."*

An attainment "should make a reliable **Forecast Grade**" (Guide p.11).

## Grade conversion (Guide p.11)

| CIE scale | A-Level grade | BNDS GPA |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | A* | 4.3 |
| 80–89 | A | 4.0 |
| 70–79 | B | 3.5 |
| 60–69 | C | 3.0 |
| 50–59 | D | 2.5 |
| 40–49 | E | 2.0 |
| below 40 | U | 0 |

**Failing is below 50%** (Guide p.15). **At or below 70%** triggers the **Academic Concern Steps**: your advisor and the A-Level Director are informed and support measures follow (Guide p.13).

## Retake policy (Guide §IV, pp.15–16)

You get one chance to retake a failed course, in the holiday after the semester, with 2–3 teacher meetings for support. The work is "just as challenging" and similar in amount to the original. The grading policy is explained before finals and cannot be changed once agreed. You must sign an academic-honesty declaration; plagiarism means immediate failure with no resubmission.

A passed retake earns the equivalent of a D, not the grade you actually achieved. A failed retake leaves the original grade standing. Under force majeure, more than 50% completion plus written, parent-signed evidence can pass; without evidence, you fail.

Graduating seniors get only **two weeks** for a retake, because their Summer Holiday starts early.
