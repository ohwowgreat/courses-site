## What you will be able to do

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Bring the final drawing to a full tonal range with edges resolved, under assessment conditions
2. Use texture only where it earns its place
3. Write a reflection that names what you did rather than how you felt about the result

## Overview

This is a working session with a deadline. You return to the same station and the same drawing, and use one double to bring it from decided-everywhere to finished — full tonal range, texture where it earns its place, edges resolved. The reflection is part of the submission: A1 is a drawing *and* the evidence that you can see what you did.

## How it runs

- **Set-up (5 min)** — stations rebuilt from last week's photographs; drawings out; criteria on the board: **observation · tone · composition**. From here it is your eyes and your paper — no phones, no looking at a neighbor's drawing.
- **Studio (60 min)** — finish the drawing. A suggested arc, on the board as a checklist, not a script: push the full tonal range first (darkest darks in, before caution sets in) → texture passes where the surface demands it → edges and cast shadows → step back at arm's length twice, at 20 and 50 minutes. Your teacher circulates and gives prompts only ("where is your darkest dark?") — no hands on drawings today.
- **Reflection (15 min)** — one page in your sketchbook: "I chose this viewpoint because …" / "The light comes from …, so the darkest tones are …" / "The texture I am most happy with is … because …" / "The hardest part was …" / "Next time I will …" Use full sentences, at least four of the five frames.
- **Close (10 min)** — submit your drawing and reflection together. **HW1 briefed**: over the break, take ≥20 photographs of *one* subject — varied viewpoint, varied light, no editing — due Wed 10-14; it opens Unit 2.
