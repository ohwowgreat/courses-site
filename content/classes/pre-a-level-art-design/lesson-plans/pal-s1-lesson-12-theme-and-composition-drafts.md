## Overview

This lesson turns your experiments into a work. You decide what your collage is about, then you plan how it will look. You write your **main idea** in one sentence, then make two different composition drafts of that idea. You circle one draft and write why it argues the idea more strongly. Your **CS3** experiment set is collected this session. The circled draft leads into your final collage, made in class over W14 and W15, which is your **A3** deliverable, due Wed 2026-12-09.

## The ideas

### Meaning

A collage is not just the things inside it. It says something. Composition is how those things are arranged so they say it.

**Romare Bearden** made collage about memory and neighborhood. In works like *The Block* and *Three Folk Musicians*, cut paper stands in for a whole world.

A **vanitas** is a type of still life from before photography. It heaps books, globes, and jewels together to say one thing: none of this lasts. The objects are juxtaposed into a sentence. The example dates from 1662. Composition gives that sentence its grammar.

### Your main idea

Your **main idea** is one sentence, written in your sketchbook before you compose anything: "My collage is about … ". Concrete beats abstract. "My grandmother's kitchen" works better than "memory".

If you need an easier starting point, use the **silhouette self-portrait**: your profile filled with what fills your head.

### Composition drafts

Make two dry-fit compositions of the same main idea, on separate boards. Make them deliberately different: one crowded, one sparse, or one symmetrical, one tilted. Do not glue anything today. Photograph both.

Then circle one draft and write the reason it argues your idea more strongly.

## Review

Check you can:

- State in one sentence what your collage is about, not what is in it.
- Explain how **Romare Bearden** used collage for memory and neighborhood.
- Explain how a **vanitas** juxtaposes objects into a meaning.
- Write a clear **main idea** for your own collage.
- Make two different composition drafts of the same main idea.
- Circle one draft and give the reason it argues the idea more strongly.
