## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. **Select** your strongest work, which means judging your own work honestly.
2. Sequence a semester so it reads as development rather than as a pile.
3. Choose, for each final piece, the supporting material that best shows how you got there.
4. Produce one organized file that meets basic image-quality standards.
5. Say what your portfolio **argues** about your development, not only what is in it.

## The task

You have four finished pieces (drawing, photographic series, collage, poster or print), a semester of process work, and forty-plus sketchbook pages. The task is to turn that into **one organized portfolio that argues something you can see**.

What goes in:

- **Your 4 to 5 strongest works.**
- For each one, **supporting material you choose**: process photographs, drafts, contact sheets, thumbnails, experiments.
- Everything to the image-quality rules in the portfolio spec, in one file, in a deliberate order.

The double on Wed 2027-01-06 is for assembling and submitting it. The semester ends the way every unit in it ended: **by choosing.**

## Instructions

1. **Select** the 4 to 5 works. If you have exactly four finals, the fifth slot is yours to use for a piece of process work or an experiment you want seen.
2. For each, choose supporting material. One or two well-chosen items beat everything you have.
3. **Photograph or scan** anything physical properly. A badly lit photograph of a good collage is a badly lit collage as far as a portfolio is concerned.
4. Assemble one file, in order, following the spec's checklist.
5. Write the **one thing your portfolio argues**: a sentence about your development that the sequence supports.
6. Submit at the close, with your sketchbook.

**Order is an argument.** Chronological is the obvious choice and it is a legitimate one, because it shows development. But it is not the only one, and if you choose another order you should be able to say why.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before**. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This is the semester's last teaching session, so there is no later class.

## Rubric

Five criteria, equally weighted.

| | **Selection** | **Sequence** | **Supporting evidence** | **Presentation quality** | **The argument** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | The genuinely strongest work, chosen with honest judgment, including one piece a weaker student would have hidden | The order makes development visible. Each piece sets up the next | Each final is supported by exactly the material that shows how it was reached. Nothing padding | Clean file, good images, consistent, easy to move through | States what the semester shows about you, and the portfolio proves it |
| **Confident** | Strong work, well judged, with one questionable inclusion | A clear, deliberate order | Good supporting material throughout, with some over-inclusion | Well presented, minor inconsistencies | A clear claim the portfolio mostly supports |
| **Competent** | Reasonable selection, defaulting to the four finals | Chronological, without much thought about it | Some support for some pieces | Complete and legible, with variable image quality | A general statement about improving |
| **Adequate** | Selection includes work that does not belong | Order appears accidental | Supporting material is thin or unrelated | Poor images, inconsistent layout, hard to navigate | No argument, just contents |
| **Limited** | Incomplete selection | No order | No supporting material | The file is difficult to open or read | Nothing stated |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a curved grade, not a raw score, with the more generous G10 curve.

## Why there is no exam

A studio course has nothing to sit: there is no paper and no content to recall under timed conditions. The 20% is met by **the portfolio you submit on the last teaching session, reviewed during the exam window.** You do not sit anything, and there is no PAL session during the window. It is a submission, not a sitting.

## After submission

- Reviewed during the window, Mon 2027-01-11 to Mon 2027-01-18.
- Returned **Wed 2027-01-20** with grades, comments and one-to-one feedback, alongside A5.
- Together with A5 this produces the semester's final grades.
- **Keep the file.** In G11 the same act of selection and sequencing becomes the Component 1 portfolio, marked by Cambridge.

## How to prepare

| Where to look | What to use it for |
|---|---|
| The portfolio spec | The 4 to 5 works rule, the image-quality rules, and the checklist. Read it over the break |
| All four attainment reflections | You have already written about each piece. Mine them for the argument |
| Your sketchbook | Where the supporting material lives. This is why the rejects and drafts were kept |
| A2's selection and sequencing work | The same skill at semester scale. You have done this once already, with six to eight photographs |
