## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Judge your own work honestly and choose your strongest pieces.
2. Order a semester so it reads as development.
3. Choose the process material that shows how you got there.
4. Make one clean file that other people can open and read.
5. Say what your portfolio argues about you, not only what is in it.

## Description

You have four finished works, a semester of process material, and forty or more sketchbook pages. Turn that into one organised portfolio that argues something.

What goes in:

- Your 4 to 5 strongest works.
- For each one, supporting material you choose: process photos, drafts, contact sheets, thumbnails, experiments.
- Everything photographed or scanned properly, in one file, in an order you decided.

The double on Wednesday 6 January is for assembling and submitting. The semester ends the way every unit in it ended, by choosing.

There is no exam. The End of Term category expects something summative, sat in the exam window. A studio course has no paper to sit and nothing useful to test by memory. So the 20% is this portfolio, handed in on the last teaching session and reviewed during the window. It is a submission, not a sitting.

## Guidelines

1. Choose 4 to 5 works. If you have exactly four finals, use the fifth slot for a piece of process work or an experiment you want seen.
2. For each work, choose supporting material. One or two well-chosen items beat everything you own.
3. Photograph or scan anything physical properly. A badly lit photo of a good collage is a badly lit collage as far as the portfolio is concerned.
4. Assemble one file, in order.
5. Write the one thing your portfolio argues. A sentence about your development that the order supports.
6. Hand it in at the end of class, with your sketchbook.

Order is an argument. Chronological is the obvious choice and a fair one, because it shows development. It is not the only choice. If you order it another way, be able to say why.

If you are absent, tell me at least 24 hours before. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This is the last teaching session, so there is no later class.

## Rubric

Five criteria, equal weight.

| | **Selection** | **Sequence** | **Evidence** | **Presentation** | **Argument** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Your genuinely strongest work, judged honestly, including one piece a nervous student would have hidden | The order makes development visible. Each piece sets up the next | Each work is supported by exactly the material that shows how you reached it. No padding | Clean file, good images, consistent, easy to move through | Says what the semester shows about you, and the portfolio proves it |
| **Confident** | Strong work, well judged, one questionable choice | A clear, deliberate order | Good supporting material, some over-inclusion | Well presented, small inconsistencies | A clear claim the portfolio mostly supports |
| **Competent** | Reasonable, defaulting to the four finals | Chronological, without much thought | Some support for some works | Complete and readable, image quality varies | A general statement about improving |
| **Adequate** | Includes work that does not belong | The order looks accidental | Thin or unrelated | Poor images, inconsistent layout | No argument, just contents |
| **Limited** | Incomplete | No order | None | Hard to open or read | Nothing stated |

Your mark becomes a curved grade under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]], on the more generous Grade 10 curve.

Keep the file. In Grade 11 the same act of choosing and ordering becomes the Component 1 portfolio, marked by Cambridge.
