## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Draw an object by looking at it, including the parts that are hard.
2. Use the full range of tone, from your lightest light to your darkest dark.
3. Use texture where the surface needs it, and nowhere else.
4. Finish your edges and your shadows.
5. Write about what you decided, not about how you feel.

## Description

You finish the drawing you started in Week 4. Same table, same objects, same drawing. You have one double to take it from decided to finished.

Then you write one page in your sketchbook about the drawing.

You hand in both at the end of class. The drawing shows what you can see. The writing shows that you know what you did. I mark both.

## Guidelines

Work in this order:

1. Put your darkest darks in first. Most students stay too light for too long.
2. Add texture, where the surface asks for it.
3. Finish the edges and the cast shadows.
4. Stand back twice, at about 20 minutes and about 50 minutes. Look from two metres away.

Rules for the day:

- No phones.
- Work from the object. Do not look at your neighbour's drawing.
- I will walk around and ask questions. I will not draw on your paper.
- Unfinished at the end is still handed in at the end. The double is the time there is.

Your reflection is one page, in full sentences. Use at least four of these five:

- I chose this viewpoint because...
- The light comes from..., so the darkest tones are...
- The texture I am happiest with is... because...
- The hardest part was...
- Next time I will...

If you are absent, tell me at least 24 hours before the class. Illness needs a doctor's note. If you tell nobody in advance the mark may be 0, even if you were genuinely ill (AL Student Guide p.8).

## Rubric

Four criteria, equal weight. Each one is marked on its own, so strong tone does not cover weak composition.

| | **Observation** | **Tone** | **Composition** | **Reflection** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | You drew what was there, including the hard parts. The proportions convince | Full range, light to dark. The mid-tones carry the form. Texture only where it belongs | You can explain the viewpoint. The drawing sits on the page on purpose | Names your decisions and your reasons. Finds a real weakness |
| **Confident** | Mostly from looking. A few parts came from memory | Wide range, darks present, edges finished | The viewpoint works and the placement is considered | Clear decisions and reasons. The weakness is real but general |
| **Competent** | From looking, but the easy parts are stronger than the hard ones | Middle greys only. The darks are not dark, so the form flattens | Sound, but the object sits where it landed | Says what you did. Reasons are thin |
| **Adequate** | Half looking, half what you assumed the object looks like | A narrow band of tone. Light and shadow barely separate | The drawing floats, or crowds one edge | Talks about feelings, not decisions |
| **Limited** | Little sign of looking at the object | Flat, or almost no tone | No decision visible | One or two sentences, unconnected to the work |

Your mark becomes a curved grade under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]], not a raw score. The Grade 10 curve is more generous than Grade 11. The grade says where you are on the way somewhere.

Keep the drawing. With your first-day garlic drawing it is the before-and-after pair for the mid-term conversation and for parents' evening. A1 feeds the mid-term grades due Thu 2026-10-29.
