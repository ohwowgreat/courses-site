## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. Draw what you can actually see, including the parts that are difficult.
2. Use the **full range of tone**, from your lightest light to your darkest dark.
3. Use texture only where the surface asks for it, not everywhere.
4. Resolve your edges and your cast shadows.
5. Write a reflection that says **what you did**, not how you felt about the result.

## The task

You finish the observational drawing you have been building since W4. Same station, same objects, same drawing. One double to take it from decided-everywhere to finished.

Then you write one page of reflection in your sketchbook.

Both are submitted together at the close. **A1 is a drawing and the evidence that you can see what you did.** The reflection is part of the assessment, not an afterthought.

## Instructions

**Working order** (a checklist, not a script):

1. Push the **full tonal range first**. Get your darkest darks in before caution sets in.
2. Texture passes, where the surface demands them.
3. Edges and cast shadows.
4. Step back at arm's length **twice**: at about 20 minutes and about 50 minutes.

**The rules for the day.** From the start of the double it is your eyes and your paper. No phones. No looking at your neighbour's drawing. Your teacher will circulate and ask questions ("where is your darkest dark?") but will not put hands on your drawing today.

**The reflection.** One page, full sentences, at least four of these five:

- "I chose this viewpoint because …"
- "The light comes from …, so the darkest tones are …"
- "The texture I am most happy with is … because …"
- "The hardest part was …"
- "Next time I will …"

**Unfinished at the end is submitted at the end.** The double is the time there is.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before** the class. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without telling anyone in advance the mark may be 0, even if you were genuinely ill (AL Student Guide p.8).

## Rubric

Four criteria, equally weighted. Each is judged on its own, so a strong tonal range does not cover a weak composition.

| | **Observation** | **Tone** | **Composition** | **Reflection** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Drawn from looking throughout, including the difficult parts. Proportions and relationships are convincing without being measured | Full range in use, from lightest light to darkest dark, with the mid-tones carrying the form. Texture appears only where the surface asks for it | The viewpoint is a decision you can explain. The drawing sits on the page deliberately, and the negative space works | Names specific decisions and their reasons. Identifies a real weakness precisely |
| **Confident** | Mostly drawn from looking. A few passages fall back on what you expected to see | Wide range, darkest darks present, edges resolved. Texture mostly earns its place | The viewpoint works and the placement is considered | Names decisions clearly, with reasons. The weakness named is real but general |
| **Competent** | Drawn from looking, with the easy parts stronger than the difficult ones | Mid-range throughout. The darkest darks are not fully dark, so the form flattens a little | The composition is sound but not chosen: the object sits where it happened to land | Says what was done. Reasons are thin |
| **Adequate** | Some observation, some copying of what you assumed the object looks like | A narrow band of tone. Little separation between light and shadow | The drawing floats, or crowds one edge | Describes feelings about the result more than decisions in the work |
| **Limited** | Little evidence of looking at the actual object | Tone is applied flatly or hardly at all | No compositional decision is visible | One or two sentences, unconnected to the work |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a **curved grade**, not a raw score. G10 curves are more generous than G11: the grade reflects where you are on the way somewhere, not what this drawing would score at A Level.
