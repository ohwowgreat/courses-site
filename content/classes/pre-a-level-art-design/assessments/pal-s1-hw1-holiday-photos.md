## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Photograph one subject many times instead of many subjects once.
2. Change your viewpoint on purpose.
3. Notice how light changes the same object.
4. Collect enough material to choose from later.

## Description

Over the National Day break, photograph **one subject at least twenty times**.

One subject means one thing: a chair, a tree outside your window, your grandmother's hands, a staircase. Not "my holiday". The variation is the assignment. If you photograph twenty different things, you have not done the task.

Change two things across the twenty photos: **where you stand** and **what the light is doing**. Shoot in the morning and at night. Shoot from the floor and from above. Get close enough that the subject fills the frame, then stand far enough back that it almost disappears.

Do not edit them. No filters.

## Guidelines

- At least 20 photos. More is better.
- One subject only.
- Different viewpoints: high, low, close, far.
- Different light: morning, afternoon, night, indoors, outdoors.
- No editing and no filters.
- Bring them on Wednesday 14 October. We build the first photography lesson on your photos, so an empty phone means an empty lesson for you.

## Rubric

| | **Quantity** | **Viewpoint** | **Light** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | 20 or more, one subject | You moved. High, low, near, far, all present | The same subject looks different because the light changed |
| **Solid** | 20, one subject | Some variety, several frames from the same place | Two or three lighting situations |
| **Not yet** | Under 20, or several subjects | Every photo taken standing in the same spot | One lighting situation |

This is marked for looking, not for luck. A blurry photograph taken from a new angle is worth more here than a sharp one taken the easy way.
