## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Build a finished poster from a thumbnail you planned.
2. Order what the eye sees first, second and third. This is called hierarchy.
3. Set type so it is easy to read and suits the message.
4. Deliver one message to one audience in three seconds.
5. Write about the hierarchy decisions you made.

## Description

One double, one poster, made from the thumbnail you chose last week. The thinking already happened at CS4. These 90 minutes are making.

The standard is the test from the unit:

**Pin it up. Walk back three metres. Count three seconds.**

If the message does not arrive in that time, good craft will not save it. That is the whole assessment in one line.

## Guidelines

1. Work from your chosen thumbnail. This is not the day to start a new design.
2. Build the hierarchy first. Decide what the eye hits first, second and third.
3. Set the type. Readable at three metres beats decorative at arm's length.
4. Finish the surface, by hand or on screen.
5. Run the three-second test yourself before you hand it in. Pin it up, walk back, look.
6. Write the reflection. One page, naming your hierarchy decisions and your reasons.
7. Hand in the poster and the reflection together. Thumbnails and type studies into the sketchbook.

If you are absent, tell me at least 24 hours before. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This is the last session before the winter break, so a missed A4 is hard to reschedule.

## Rubric

Four criteria, equal weight, each marked on its own.

| | **Hierarchy** | **Typography** | **Message** | **Audience** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | The eye arrives where you intended, in the order you intended. Size, weight, position and space all work together | The typeface suits the message and reads at three metres. Spacing and alignment are controlled | One message, clear in three seconds, and worth saying | A specific audience is visible in the words, the image and the tone |
| **Confident** | Clear first, second and third read. One element competes | Readable and well set, with a typeface that suits the message | One clear message, inside the time | A clear audience, addressed consistently |
| **Competent** | There is a focal point, but the second and third reads are muddled | Readable. The typeface is neutral rather than chosen | Understandable, but slower than three seconds | An audience is implied but generic |
| **Adequate** | Elements compete. The eye does not know where to start | Too small, too tight, or fighting the background | The message needs explaining | No particular audience |
| **Limited** | No hierarchy. Everything the same weight | Hard to read at any distance | No message you can name | No audience considered |

Your mark becomes a curved grade under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]], on the more generous Grade 10 curve.

## If the class chose printmaking

Unit 4 runs either graphic design or printmaking, in the same slot, decided by the class. The poster is the default and I will confirm your route in class. If the class takes the [[classes/pre-a-level-art-design/lesson-plans/pal-s1-u4-printmaking-variant|printmaking variant]], A4 is the final linocut print instead, on the same date, with the same notice and the same weight.

| | **Carving control** | **Ink and registration** | **Two-tone legibility** | **Reflection** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Marks cut with intent. Line weight changes for a reason, and the cutting carries the image | Even ink, clean registration across the edition. The print repeats | The two tones separate the image cleanly and read at distance | Names decisions in the carving and printing, and what the proof taught you |
| **Confident** | Controlled cutting, a few places where the tool led | Good coverage, registration accurate with small drift | The image reads clearly in two tones | Clear reasons, drawn from the proof |
| **Competent** | Competent. Line weight is uniform | Ink adequate, registration visibly off | The image reads, with some tonal confusion | Says what you did |
| **Adequate** | Uneven or tentative cutting | Patchy ink, registration well out | The two tones fight each other | Describes the process without decisions |
| **Limited** | Little control of the tool | Incomplete or unreadable print | No tonal separation | Almost no writing |

Everything else on this page applies either way. CS4 changes with the route too: a proof with annotation instead of thumbnails with a poster analysis.

Keep the poster or print in good condition. It comes back as portfolio material in January. Over the break, keep the sketchbook alive, two to three pages a week. [[classes/pre-a-level-art-design/assessments/pal-s1-a5-sketchbook|A5]] collects it on Wed 2027-01-06.
