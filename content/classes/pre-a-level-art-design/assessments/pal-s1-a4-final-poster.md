## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. Execute a decided thumbnail as a finished poster, in whichever medium you chose.
2. Build a **hierarchy**: make the eye arrive in the order you intend.
3. Set type so it is legible and so the type choice suits the message.
4. Deliver **one message to one audience** within three seconds and three meters.
5. Write a reflection naming the hierarchy decisions you made.

## The task

One double, one poster, made from a thumbnail you decided last week. The design thinking has already happened, so these 90 minutes are execution with judgment.

The standard is the unit's own test:

**Pin it up. Walk back three meters. Count three seconds. If the message does not land in that window, no amount of craft saves it.** That is the whole assessment in one sentence.

## Instructions

1. Work from your **chosen thumbnail**. This is not the session to redesign.
2. Build the hierarchy first: what the eye hits first, second, third.
3. Set the type. Legibility at three meters beats decoration at arm's length.
4. Finish the surface, handmade or digital.
5. **Run the three-second test yourself** before you submit. Pin it up, walk back, look.
6. Write the reflection: one page, naming your hierarchy decisions and why you made them.
7. Submit poster and reflection together. Thumbnails and type studies into the sketchbook.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before**. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This is the **last session before the winter break**, so a missed A4 is difficult to reschedule.

## Rubric

Four criteria, equally weighted, each judged on its own.

| | **Hierarchy** | **Typography** | **Message** | **Audience** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | The eye arrives exactly where you intended, in the order you intended. Scale, weight, position and space all working together | Type choice suits the message and is legible at three meters. Spacing and alignment are controlled | One message, unmistakable in three seconds, and worth saying | A specific audience is visible in every choice: the language, the image, the tone |
| **Confident** | Clear first, second and third read. One element competes slightly | Legible and well set, with a type choice that suits the message | One clear message, landing inside the window | A clear audience, addressed consistently |
| **Competent** | A focal point exists, but the second and third reads are muddled | Legible. The type choice is neutral rather than chosen | The message is understandable, though it takes longer than three seconds | An audience is implied but generic |
| **Adequate** | Elements compete. The eye does not know where to start | Legibility problems: too small, too tight, or fighting the background | The message needs explaining | No particular audience is addressed |
| **Limited** | No hierarchy: everything is the same weight | Type is hard to read at any distance | No identifiable message | No audience considered |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a curved grade, not a raw score, with the more generous G10 curve.

## If your class takes the printmaking route

Unit 4 runs **either** Graphic Design **or** Printmaking, in the same slot. The poster is the default; your teacher will confirm the route in class. If your class takes the [[classes/pre-a-level-art-design/lesson-plans/pal-s1-u4-printmaking-variant|printmaking variant]], then **A4 is the final linocut print** instead, on the same date, with the same notice and the same weight. Its criteria:

| | **Carving control** | **Ink and registration** | **Two-tone legibility** | **Reflection** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Marks are cut with intent: line weight varies for a reason, and the cutting itself carries the image | Even ink, clean registration across the edition. The print quality is repeatable | The two tones separate the image cleanly and read at distance | Names specific decisions in the carving and printing, and what the proof taught you |
| **Confident** | Controlled cutting, with a few passages where the tool led rather than followed | Good ink coverage, registration accurate with minor drift | The image reads clearly in two tones | Clear reasons, evidenced from the proof |
| **Competent** | Competent cutting. Line weight is fairly uniform | Ink is adequate; registration is visible but off | The image reads, with some tonal confusion | Says what was done |
| **Adequate** | Cutting is uneven or tentative | Patchy ink, registration noticeably out | The two tones fight each other | Describes the process without decisions |
| **Limited** | Little control of the tool | The print is incomplete or illegible | No tonal separation | Minimal writing |

Everything else on this page applies either way. **CS4 changes with the route too**: proof plus annotation instead of thumbnails plus poster analysis.

## After the sit

- Back within **five working days**, with written feedback.
- The poster (or print) returns as **portfolio material at W19**, so keep it in good condition.
- **Over the break:** keep the sketchbook alive, 2 to 3 pages a week. **A5 collects it on Wed 2027-01-06.** Read the portfolio spec before that session.
