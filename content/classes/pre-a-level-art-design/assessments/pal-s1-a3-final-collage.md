## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Put two images together so the pair says something neither says alone.
2. Layer on purpose, so depth and order carry meaning.
3. Finish a work under assessment conditions.
4. Take off anything that is only decoration.
5. Write about decisions a reader can see in the work.

## Description

You finish the collage. The last layer goes down and the work starts speaking for itself.

Before you hand it in, use the test from Lesson 10 one more time:

**Cover one piece with your hand. Does the collage lose part of its sentence?**

If a piece is only there because it looks nice, take it off.

Your collage should be summed up in one sentence. You wrote that sentence in Lesson 12. That sentence is what makes meaning something I can mark, instead of a matter of taste.

## Guidelines

1. Finish the top layer, then the edges, then any words.
2. Run the covering test on every piece. Anything that survives only because it is pretty comes off.
3. Write the reflection. One page, full sentences, pointing at things a reader can see.
4. Hand in the collage and the reflection together at the end of class.
5. Put your drafts, dry-fit photographs and leftover scraps into your sketchbook.

If you are absent, tell me at least 24 hours before. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8).

## Rubric

Four criteria, equal weight, each marked on its own.

| | **Juxtaposition** | **Layering** | **Composition** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Pairs make something neither image held alone. The joins are chosen, and surprising in a way you can defend | Layers build depth and a reading order. What is buried and what is on top are decisions | The whole surface is composed. Size changes do work. Nothing landed by accident | One idea, carried by the work. The reflection adds reasons instead of supplying the meaning |
| **Confident** | Pairs work together, one or two only sit side by side | Layering is deliberate and mostly controlled | Composed, with a considered focal point and edges | The idea is clear in the work. The writing explains it well |
| **Competent** | Some pairs say something, others are just next to each other | Layers stack rather than interact | Sound, but not yet using size or the whole surface | An idea is there, but you need the writing to find it |
| **Adequate** | Images placed together with no relationship | Little layering, or layering that hides | Everything spread evenly, no emphasis | The writing claims a meaning the work does not carry |
| **Limited** | A group of cut-out images | One layer, or accidental overlap | No decision visible | No idea in the work or the writing |

Your mark becomes a curved grade under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]], on the more generous Grade 10 curve.
