## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. Put two images together so that the **pairing** says something neither says alone.
2. Layer deliberately, so depth and order carry meaning.
3. Finish the top layer, the edges and any words under assessment conditions.
4. **Remove anything that is only decoration**, and know it by testing it.
5. Write a reflection that points at decisions visible in the work.

## The task

You finish the collage. The last layer goes down and the work starts speaking for itself.

The test from Lesson 10, asked one final time before you submit:

**Cover any one element with your hand. Does the collage lose part of its sentence?**

If a piece is only decoration, it comes off.

A3 is a made-in-class attainment. **The mark rewards decisions you can point to**, in the work and in the reflection. Meaning is judged on both: the collage has to carry it, and the reflection has to give the reasons.

## Instructions

1. Finish the **top layer**, then the **edges**, then any **words**.
2. Run the covering test on every element. Anything that survives only because it looks nice comes off.
3. Write the reflection: one page, full sentences, pointing at things a reader can see in the work.
4. Submit collage and reflection together at the close.
5. Put your drafts, dry-fit photographs and leftover scraps into your sketchbook. They are process evidence for SB2 and A5.

**One main idea.** Your collage should be able to be summed up in one sentence. You wrote that sentence in Lesson 12. It is what makes "meaning" markable rather than a matter of taste.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before**. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8).

## Rubric

Four criteria, equally weighted, each judged on its own.

| | **Juxtaposition** | **Layering** | **Composition** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Pairings produce something neither image held alone. The joins are chosen, and surprising in a way you can justify | Layers create real depth and a reading order. What is buried and what is on top are decisions | The whole surface is composed. Scale shifts do work. Nothing is where it landed by accident | One idea, carried by the work itself. The reflection adds reasons rather than supplying the meaning |
| **Confident** | Pairings clearly do work together, with one or two that only sit next to each other | Layering is deliberate and mostly controlled | Composed, with a considered focal point and edges | The idea is clear in the work. Reflection explains it well |
| **Competent** | Some pairings say something; others are simply adjacent | Layers exist but mostly stack rather than interact | Sound composition, not yet using scale or the whole surface | An idea is present but you need the reflection to find it |
| **Adequate** | Images are placed together without a relationship between them | Little layering, or layering that hides rather than builds | Elements are distributed evenly with no emphasis | The reflection asserts a meaning the work does not carry |
| **Limited** | A group of cut-out images | Single layer, or accidental overlap | No compositional decision visible | No idea identifiable in work or writing |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a curved grade, not a raw score, with the more generous G10 curve.

## After the sit

- U4 opens next week with a route decision: **Graphic Design (the poster) or Printmaking (linocut)**. A4 follows whichever route the class takes; which route runs will be confirmed in class.
