## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Show ideas that developed instead of ideas that arrived finished.
2. Show experiments, including the ones that failed.
3. Show things you looked at, recorded, and then used.
4. Show writing you did while you worked, not after.
5. Keep a book somebody else can read.

## Description

The sketchbook is the one assessment in this course you cannot make in a session. It asks a single question: across a whole semester, is there evidence of somebody thinking in a book?

You hand in the book. That is the task.

What should be in it, built up since Week 1:

- Work from every session, dated.
- Tonal scales, texture studies and negative space work from Unit 1.
- Contact sheets and cut photographs from Unit 2.
- Collage drafts, dry-fit photos and scraps from Unit 3.
- Thumbnails and type studies from Unit 4.
- The reflection you wrote after each attainment.
- Whatever you did over the National Day and winter breaks, dated.

You have seen this standard twice already, at SB1 and SB2. A5 is the same book, marked completely instead of sampled.

## Guidelines

1. Bring the book on Wednesday 6 January.
2. Number your pages if they are not numbered. Check that everything is dated.
3. Do not remove your failures. A book with no failed experiments shows less, not more.
4. Do not add three weeks of work the night before. I can see the difference.
5. Hand it in at the end of class, with the portfolio.

Before you hand it in, read your own book from front to back. You will find your gaps faster than I will, and noticing them is itself a page of reflection.

If you are absent, tell me at least 24 hours before. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This is the last teaching session of the semester, so there is no later class to give it to.

## Rubric

Five criteria, weighted.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Ideas and creativity | 25% |
| Experimentation | 25% |
| Visual research | 20% |
| Critical reflection | 15% |
| Presentation | 15% |

| | **Ideas** | **Experimentation** | **Visual research** | **Reflection** | **Presentation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Ideas develop across pages. A thought is followed, tested, changed. A personal direction is appearing | Wide, real experimenting, failures kept and annotated. The experiments feed the finished work | Sustained looking, recorded and then used. Sources noted, something taken from each | Written as you went. Names decisions and what changed because of them | Navigable, dated, complete. The order tells the story of the semester |
| **Confident** | Ideas develop, some jumps unexplained | Regular experimenting across media, mostly connected to the finished work | Consistent research, mostly used | Regular reflection with clear reasons | Well kept, dated, easy to follow |
| **Competent** | Ideas are there but arrive rather than develop | Some experimenting, in the media the unit asked for | Collected, only sometimes used | Reflection after the big pieces, thin in between | Complete, with some undated stretches |
| **Adequate** | Little development. Finished attempts side by side | Thin, or one medium only | Copied images with nothing taken from them | Occasional writing that describes rather than reflects | Gaps, undated pages, hard to follow |
| **Limited** | No development visible | Almost none | Little research | Almost no writing | Substantially incomplete |

Your mark becomes a curved grade under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]], on the more generous Grade 10 curve.

The spot-checks are not small versions of A5. They look at upkeep only. A5 is the only time the full criteria are used.

Keep the book. Semester 2 continues in it. In Grade 11 the same habit becomes the recording layer of a real Cambridge portfolio.
