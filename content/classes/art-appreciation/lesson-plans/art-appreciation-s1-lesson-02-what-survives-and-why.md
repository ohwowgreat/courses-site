## Overview

This lesson asks what survives from the deep past, and why. You look at cave images made by people who left no writing, and learn to argue from the wall itself. You produce a one-page panel reading on Thu 2026-09-10, built from two paired images; it rehearses the A1 justification, the same move at greater length. A1 is announced on Fri 2026-09-04 and is due Fri 2026-09-18. LB1 is posted from the seminar on Fri 2026-09-11.

## The ideas

### What survives, and why

Start with the object in front of you and work backward. Name what had to happen, physically, for a mark made 30,000 years ago to reach you at all. Most things from that far back are gone; the few that survive did so for reasons you can name.

### The wall is the evidence

These are people who left no writing, so **the wall is the evidence**. You argue from what is on the wall, not from anthropology or guesswork. The two main sites are the caves at **Lascaux** and **Chauvet**. Account for the choices made: the choice of animal, the choice of wall, and the choice of depth in the cave. When you ask why someone made a mark, argue one reason and back it from the wall.

### Reading by proximity

**Reading by proximity** is Aby Warburg's method: you read two images by how they are placed together, and say what the pairing argues, not what each one shows on its own. You apply it to a cave wall the way Warburg applied it to a panel.

### Changing your position

In seminar, the skill is to change your position when someone else is right, out loud, and to say that is what happened. This is what LB1 watches.

- **Wed 09-09.** Why make a mark: argue one reason, evidenced from the wall. Chauvet reading due.
- **Thu 09-10.** Read a cave wall the way Warburg read a panel, by proximity. Write the one-page panel reading from two paired images. This is the deliverable.
- **Fri 09-11.** Seminar on what the first images wanted, thirty minutes. LB1 is posted from it.

## Assessment

- A1 is announced Fri 2026-09-04, due Fri 2026-09-18.
- **LB1**, Fri 2026-09-11, posted from the seminar. Graded on whether you took a position and moved it. This is a behavior, so it is what is watched in the seminar.
- **One-page panel reading**, Thu 2026-09-10. Direct rehearsal for the A1 justification, the same move at greater length.

## Review

Check you can:

- Explain what had to happen for a mark made 30,000 years ago to reach you.
- Argue one reason for making a mark, using the wall as evidence.
- Read two images by proximity and say what the pairing argues, not what each one shows alone.
- Change your position in a seminar when someone is right, and say that is what happened.
