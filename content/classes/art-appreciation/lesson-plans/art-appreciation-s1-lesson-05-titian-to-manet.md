## Overview

This lesson looks at three paintings and asks how one picture answers another. You study Gentileschi, Titian, and Manet, and you learn to build a real comparison rather than a list. You write **A2**, your Unit 2 comparative essay, in class on Tue 2026-10-13: one claim about two pictures, evidenced from the pictures. A2 carries your mid-term grades, which are due Thu 2026-10-29.

## The ideas

### Comparison, not a list

A **comparison** argues one claim about two pictures. It is not an inventory of every difference. A list is not a comparison. Differences only count when they carry meaning, so you choose the differences that support your claim and test the claim against what you can see.

### Evidence from the picture, not the reception

Argue from the pictures themselves. What a work provoked when it was shown, its **reception**, is background. Your evidence is the image in front of you, not the story of how people reacted to it.

### Gentileschi, *Judith Slaying Holofernes*

Describe the violence in the painting before you describe the painter. Then ask the question of the **witness**: who does the picture put you next to? Where a painting places you decides how you read the action.

### Titian, *Venus of Urbino*

Ask what the picture asks you to accept as natural. A painting can present a pose, a gaze, or a setting as ordinary, and naming that assumption is part of reading it.

### Manet, *Olympia*

Look at *Olympia* first with no context. Locate the exact **refusals** Manet makes of Titian, one by one: the specific choices where Manet declines to do what the *Venus of Urbino* does, and what each choice costs. This pairing is the heart of A2.

- Explain why a list is not a comparison.
