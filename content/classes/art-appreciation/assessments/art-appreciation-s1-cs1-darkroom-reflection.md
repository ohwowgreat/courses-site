# Art Appreciation · S1 CS1: Darkroom Reflection

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Fri 2026-09-04, written in the room |
| Where | In class, collected at the door |
| Length | One paragraph |
| Rehearses | A1, and the Unit 4 argument about what an image is |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Name a physical decision you made, in words another person could act on
2. Tell a decision apart from a feeling about the result
3. Describe a photographic image as something light did to a surface

## Description

After the darkroom session you write about your print while it is still wet. Not later, not at home. The wet print is in front of you and the decisions are still in your hands.

One criterion is marked: **you name a physical decision rather than a feeling about the result.**

"I placed the object off-center so its shadow crossed the edge" is a decision. Someone else could do it. "I like how it came out" is a feeling. Nobody can act on it.

The idea underneath this, that a photograph is a thing light did to a surface, comes back in Unit 4 when we argue about reproduction and the poor image. This paragraph is the course's first deposit on that December argument.

## Guidelines

- One paragraph. Four to six sentences is enough.
- Start with what you did, not with how you feel about it.
- Name at least two decisions: what you placed, where, how long you exposed it.
- Use the words for the things in front of you: paper, light, exposure, shadow, edge.
- If a sentence would still be true about somebody else's print, cut it.
- Hand it in at the door. Nothing is taken home.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Two or more physical decisions named precisely, each with what it did to the print. Nothing in the paragraph is a verdict. |
| **Secure** | One decision named clearly and its effect described. Perhaps one stray evaluative sentence. |
| **Developing** | A decision is mentioned but stays vague ("I moved it a bit"), or the effect is asserted rather than described. |
| **Not yet** | The paragraph is about liking or not liking the result. No physical decision is recoverable from it. |

The distinction is the whole task. A print you dislike, described precisely, scores well. A print you love, described vaguely, does not.
