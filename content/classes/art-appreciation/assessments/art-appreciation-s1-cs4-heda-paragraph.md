# Art Appreciation · S1 CS4: The Heda Paragraph

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Fri 2026-11-13, written in class |
| What | One paragraph on the Heda still life |
| Rule | Every adjective is earned |
| Rehearses | A3 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Write description in which the picture forces every word you use
2. Tell an earned adjective from a decorative one
3. Cut your own writing without losing the argument

## Description

One paragraph on the Heda still life in which **every adjective is earned**.

An adjective is earned when the picture forces it. "The lemon's peel hangs in one unbroken spiral" earns its words: you can point at the spiral. "The beautiful, masterful still life" earns none: nothing in the picture made you write either word.

Papers swap. Each reader cuts one unearned adjective from someone else's paragraph. You get yours back with a cut in it.

This is direct practice for A3, which is marked on arguing from the pictures.

## Guidelines

- One paragraph, written in the room, no notes from home.
- Before you write, look for sixty seconds without writing anything.
- Every adjective must be attachable to something you could point at.
- Ban list, and it is short: beautiful, masterful, stunning, amazing, interesting.
- When you swap, cut exactly one adjective. Not three. Choosing which one is the exercise.
- Read your returned paragraph and decide whether the cut was fair. You may argue it.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Every adjective is anchored to something visible. The paragraph would survive a reader hunting for a loose word. At least one observation is one nobody else made. |
| **Secure** | Mostly earned. One or two adjectives float free, and the partner's cut is easy to accept. |
| **Developing** | Several evaluative words carrying no evidence. The picture is praised rather than described. |
| **Not yet** | The paragraph is a verdict on the painting. Removing the adjectives would leave nothing behind. |

Precision, not enthusiasm. The test is whether a reader could find what you saw.
