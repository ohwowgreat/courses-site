# Art Appreciation · S1 CS3: Storyboard Debrief

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Fri 2026-10-09, first session back from the break |
| What | Ten silent minutes with your own storyboard and the criteria |
| Deliverable | A short posting recording what you found |
| Rehearses | The January retrospective |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Read your own work against criteria instead of against memory
2. Name the exact frame where your sequence stopped being legible
3. Say why it failed, in terms of framing rather than effort

## Description

Your storyboard comes back. Before anything is discussed, you spend **ten silent minutes** with it and the criteria, looking for one thing: **the frame where the sequence stopped being legible.**

The posting records what you found. It is marked on honest diagnosis, not on how good the storyboard was. Naming your own weakest frame accurately is the skill, and it is the same self-reading the retrospective asks for in January.

The silence is the part that does the work. It is not shortened.

## Guidelines

- Work alone for the full ten minutes. Do not compare with a neighbour yet.
- Name one frame by its number. Not "the middle bit".
- Say what a reader could not get from it, and what in the framing caused that.
- One sentence on what you would change. One, not a list.
- Honesty scores. "Frame 6 works" when your partner stalled at 6 does not.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | One frame named exactly, the failure described in terms of framing, and a single specific change proposed. The diagnosis matches what the partner actually experienced. |
| **Secure** | A frame is named and the problem is broadly right, though the cause is described loosely. |
| **Developing** | The posting is general ("the middle was confusing") or blames effort and time rather than a decision in the frame. |
| **Not yet** | No frame identified, or the posting defends the storyboard instead of reading it. |

A storyboard that failed, diagnosed precisely, scores higher here than one that worked, described vaguely.
