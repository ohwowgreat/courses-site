## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. Locate a **specific refusal** Manet makes of Titian, and say what that refusal costs.
2. Build a comparison from differences that **carry meaning**, not from an inventory of what is different.
3. Argue **one claim**, evidenced from the pictures rather than from their reception.
4. Write about paintings without decorating the sentences.

## The task

An in-class essay on two paintings, closed book.

You make **one claim** about Titian against Manet and you argue it from what is in the pictures.

The trap, and it is the whole reason this assessment exists:

**A list is not a comparison.**

Everybody can produce a list of differences: the gaze, the maid, the cat, the shoes, the flowers, the hand. A list is inventory. A comparison identifies which difference matters and argues what it does. One difference, followed properly, beats six differences noted.

The second trap is arguing from **reception**. What critics said in 1865 is interesting and it is not evidence about the picture. You read on *Olympia*'s reception as homework precisely so you know the difference between the painting and the noise around it.

## Instructions

1. **Choose your difference before you start writing.** Not the most obvious one, the most productive one.
2. **State your claim in your first paragraph.** A claim is arguable: somebody could disagree with it. "Manet's Olympia is different from Titian's Venus" is not a claim.
3. **Evidence every step from the picture.** Point at what is actually there: where the eyes go, what the hand does, how the space is built, what the paint itself does.
4. **Say what the refusal costs.** Manet gives something up by refusing Titian. Naming the cost is what separates a good essay from a competent one.
5. **Do not narrate the paintings.** Assume your reader can see them.

**Closed conditions.** No notes and no reading brought into the room. What you know is what you carry.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before**. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). This one also carries mid-term grades, so an absence has knock-on consequences.

## Rubric

Four criteria, equally weighted.

| | **The claim** | **Evidence from the pictures** | **Comparison as argument** | **Precision of language** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | One arguable claim, stated early, held throughout, and worth making. It identifies a refusal and names what the refusal costs | Every step evidenced from what is in the picture, at the level of where the eye goes and what the paint does | One difference followed all the way. The comparison produces something neither picture would have shown alone | Every adjective is earned. Nothing decorative. The prose does the analysis rather than dressing it |
| **Convincing** | A clear arguable claim, held, with the cost of the refusal implied rather than named | Consistently evidenced from the pictures, with one or two assertions unsupported | A dominant difference is followed, with minor detours into inventory | Controlled and mostly precise |
| **Competent** | A claim is present but broad, or drifts by the end | Some evidence from the pictures, some from what was taught | Two or three differences noted, one developed | Clear, with some vague or ornamental phrasing |
| **Partial** | A topic rather than a claim | Assertion with occasional gestures at the pictures | A list of differences, undeveloped | Loose, with unearned adjectives doing the work of argument |
| **Undeveloped** | No identifiable claim | Argues from reception or from memory of the lesson, not from the pictures | Description of two paintings in sequence | Imprecise throughout |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a curved grade rather than a raw score.
