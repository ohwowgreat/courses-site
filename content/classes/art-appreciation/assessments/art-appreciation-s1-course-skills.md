# Art Appreciation · S1 Course Skills

[[classes/art-appreciation/art-appreciation|Art Appreciation]] · [[classes/art-appreciation/assessments/art-appreciation-s1-assessments|S1 Assessment Register]] · the five Course Skills items and the seminar postings

The four majors and the retrospective each have their own page with a full brief. The five Course Skills items are smaller. This page lists them all in one place: what each is, when it happens, and what it is marked on. Each one rehearses a move a major will ask for. So none of them should feel new when it arrives.

## How these count

**Course Skills is 20% of your grade**, from the five items below. Each is marked on one named skill. You do it in the room or you get its own brief, with **72 hours' notice**. Notice the pattern: CS1 practices seeing an image as a made thing. CS2 and CS3 practice reading and making sequence. CS4 practices earned writing. CS5 practices precise description of change. Those are the five muscles the majors and the retrospective use.

## The five items

### CS1 · Darkroom reflection

**Fri 2026-09-04, written in the room, collected at the door.** After the darkroom session, you write about the print while it is still wet. Marked on one criterion: **naming a physical decision rather than a feeling about the result.** "I placed the object off-center so its shadow crossed the edge" is a decision. "I like how it came out" is a feeling. The idea underneath, the image as a thing light did to a surface, returns in Unit 4. So this reflection is the course's first deposit on its December argument.

### CS2 · Ten-frame storyboard

**Tue 2026-09-29, made in class, from the week's work.** Ten frames a stranger can read in the right order with **no captions and no dialogue**. The swap is the test: a partner reads yours cold, and where they stall is where the sequence fails. It feeds A2, because both are about reading what a picture decided. The sequence you make here is the one the break homework extends.

### CS3 · Storyboard debrief

**Fri 2026-10-09, first session back from the break.** Your storyboard comes back. You spend ten silent minutes with the criteria, finding **the frame where the sequence stopped being legible.** The posting records what you found. Marked on honest diagnosis, not on how good the storyboard was. Naming your own weakest frame accurately is the skill. It is the same self-reading the retrospective asks for in January.

### CS4 · The Heda paragraph

**Fri 2026-11-13, written in class.** One paragraph on the Heda still life in which **every adjective is earned**. An adjective is earned when the picture forces it: "the lemon's peel hangs in one unbroken spiral" earns its words, "the beautiful, masterful still life" earns none. Papers swap, and each reader cuts one unearned adjective from someone else's. This is direct practice for A3, which is marked on arguing from the pictures.

### CS5 · The poor-image lab

**Wed 2026-12-09, in class.** You degrade an image on purpose, by compression, re-photographing, or screenshotting the screenshot. Then you write fifteen minutes on **exactly what changed, not that it got worse.** "Worse" is a verdict. "The reds flattened, the text became unreadable" is an observation. It is the only session all term where you make something worse on purpose, and it is direct practice for A4.

## The seminar postings

The nine **LB** postings are Learning Behaviour, 10% of the grade. They are not tasks to prepare. Each records one seminar or session on the dates in the [[classes/art-appreciation/assessments/art-appreciation-s1-assessments|register]]. The criterion is the same every time: **taking a position and moving it, out loud, when someone else is right.** Changing your mind in public because of evidence is the graded behavior. Performing confidence is not. Two notes worth knowing: LB4 is posted for whoever is present while G12 is at Seniors' Day, with no penalty for being away. LB9 lands after the grades deadline, so it counts toward Semester 2 and is announced as such.
