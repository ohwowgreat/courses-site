## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show that you can:

1. Put two images next to each other **for a reason that belongs to that pair** and to no other pair.
2. Make an argument that is **legible from the layout before a word is read**.
3. Describe an object by **what it does**, not by what it reminds you of.
4. Write a justification that gives the reasons the wall cannot say out loud.

## The task

Your group builds a board on the wall from the print pool: images spanning Chauvet, Lascaux, Neolithic figures and ritual objects, deliberately over-supplied so that selection is a real decision rather than a scramble.

Then each of you writes your own justification of it.

The standard, stated once and worth memorizing:

**A board without reasons is a mood board.**

Every adjacency has to carry a reason. If the reason you give for putting A next to B would work just as well for A next to C, it is not yet a reason.

## Instructions

1. **Select** from the print pool. You cannot use everything, and what you leave out is part of the argument.
2. **Arrange** on the wall with pins. No glue: the arrangement has to stay changeable until you are satisfied.
3. **Test each adjacency.** For every pair, ask: could this reason be moved to a different pair? If yes, the pair is decorative.
4. **Step back and read your own wall.** Somebody who arrives knowing nothing should be able to see what you are claiming before you explain it.
5. **Write your justification individually.** What the board argues, what each adjacency does, and what the arrangement leaves out.

**The grade is half collective.** The board earns a shared mark; your justification earns your own. Understand how that works before you start, or you will spend the last session arguing about it instead of hanging.

**If you are absent.** Tell your teacher **at least 24 hours before**. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact the mark may be 0, even for genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8).

## Rubric

Four criteria. The first three are the board (shared); the fourth is yours.

| | **Adjacency reasoning** | **Layout legibility** | **Description** | **Written justification** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Every pairing carries a reason that could not be transferred to a different pair. At least one adjacency is genuinely surprising and survives scrutiny | The argument is visible on arrival. Distance, grouping and gaps all doing work | Objects are described by what they do: what they are for, what they make happen, who used them | Gives the reasons the wall cannot state, names what was excluded and why, and does not simply narrate the board |
| **Convincing** | Reasons are specific to their pairs, with one or two that could be moved | The argument reads clearly with a moment's looking | Mostly functional description, with occasional drift into association | Clear reasons throughout, including at least one exclusion |
| **Competent** | Pairings are related, and the reasons are true but general | An order is visible; the argument needs a sentence of help | Description mixes what objects do with what they look like | States what the board argues and gives reasons for most of it |
| **Partial** | Images are grouped by subject or by looking similar | The board reads as a collection | Description is mostly visual resemblance | Narrates the board rather than justifying it |
| **Undeveloped** | No reasoning offered for the arrangement | No argument discernible | Objects named, not described | Little or no justification |

Under the [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS framework]] this becomes a curved grade rather than a raw score.

## After the sit

The course's review protocol, used on every assessment:

1. **The criteria**, in front of you.
2. **Ten minutes of silence** with your own work and the criteria before anything is discussed.
3. **One named strength.**
4. **One move**: the single next thing.

Marking is back within five working days.

**Leave the boards up.** Unit 2 opens against them, and the December and January comparisons work much better if the first board is still on the wall.
