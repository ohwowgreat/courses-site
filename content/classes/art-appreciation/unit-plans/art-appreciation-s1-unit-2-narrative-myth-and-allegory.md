# Unit 2: Narrative, Myth and Allegory

[[classes/art-appreciation/art-appreciation|Art Appreciation]] · [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-semester-1-plan|Semester 1 Plan]] · Unit 2 of 5 · [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-s1-unit-1-origins-and-ancestral-memory|← Unit 1]] | [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-s1-unit-3-food-the-table-and-ethics|Unit 3 →]]

## What this unit does

This unit is about how images carry story without words. The first half builds your vocabulary: **narrative**, where a still image puts time; **myth**, shared shorthand that needs an audience who already knows the story; **allegory**, the image that means something elsewhere; and the **cycle**. You then turn this vocabulary into a made thing, the ten-frame storyboard that carries its story on framing alone.

The second half turns on a pair of paintings. You read Titian's *Venus of Urbino*, asking what it asks you to accept as natural, against Manet's *Olympia*, where you locate the exact refusals. Gentileschi's *Judith Slaying Holofernes* is the close-looking hinge on the problem of the witness.

The **critique flow**, describe, account, judge, is taught from cold at the top of Lesson 04. The storyboard is direct rehearsal for the [[classes/art-appreciation/assessments/art-appreciation-s1-a2-titian-manet-comparison|A2]] comparison. Both are about reading what a picture decided.
