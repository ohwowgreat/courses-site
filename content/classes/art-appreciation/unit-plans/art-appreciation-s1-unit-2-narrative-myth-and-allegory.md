# Unit 2: Narrative, Myth and Allegory

[[classes/art-appreciation/art-appreciation|Art Appreciation]] · [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-semester-1-plan|Semester 1 Plan]] · Unit 2 of 5 · [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-s1-unit-1-origins-and-ancestral-memory|← Unit 1]] | [[classes/art-appreciation/unit-plans/art-appreciation-s1-unit-3-food-the-table-and-ethics|Unit 3 →]]

## What this unit does

This unit is about how images carry a story without words. The first half builds your vocabulary — narrative (where a still image puts time), myth (shared shorthand that needs an audience who already knows the reference), allegory (the image that means something elsewhere), and cycle — and turns it into a made thing: a ten-frame storyboard that carries its story on framing alone.

The second half centres on a pair of works: Titian's *Venus of Urbino* (what it asks you to accept as natural) against Manet's *Olympia* (locating the exact refusals), with Gentileschi's *Judith Slaying Holofernes* as a close-looking study of the problem of the witness. You learn the critique flow — describe, account, judge — at the top of Lesson 04. The storyboard is direct rehearsal for the A2 comparison; both are about reading what a picture decided.
