# A Level Art & Design · Department Handbook

This is the Art & Design handbook you are given for the 9479 course. It runs to 208 pages and was written and designed by Doğan Arslanoğlu.

It covers the visual elements, the principles of design, the three components, the assessment objectives with checklists, the four disciplines, theme selection, artist studies, the Personal Study, and about 87 pages of portfolio samples.

## The handbook uses old-syllabus numbers

> [!important] Use the syllabus numbers, not the handbook's
> The handbook was written for an older version of the 9479 specification. Many of its numbers are out of date. Do not act on the numbers below. Use the current 2028-30 syllabus instead.

| Feature | Handbook says | Current syllabus |
|---|---|---|
| C1 name | "Component 1: Coursework" | Component 1: Portfolio (coursework is still an OK informal name) |
| C1 mark | single mark out of 100 | 60/60/75-mark grids |
| C2 supervised test | 15 hours | use the current syllabus |
| C2 submission | three sheets of A2, photographed and mounted | all-digital submission |
| C1 submission | five sheets of A2 | use the current syllabus |
| C3 submission | eight sheets of A2 | use the current syllabus |
| C3 written analysis | 1000–1500 words | 2000–2500 words ← use this |
| C3 mark | out of 100, "weighted to 200" | use the current syllabus |
| AOs | AO1 Record, AO2 Explore, AO3 Develop, AO4 Present | new per-component splits |

Every hard number you could act on, such as sheet counts, hours, word counts and mark totals, comes from the previous specification. The [[classes/a-level-art-design/a-level-art-design|course page]] also warns you never to take numbers from the older support documents. This handbook is one of them. Its prose, structure and teaching content are fine. It is the numbers that are wrong.

## Where the handbook contradicts itself

1. **Personal Study word count.** Pages 36 and 104 say 1000–1500 words. Pages 114–115 say an essay of 2000–3000 words that "should not exceed 3000 words." Use the current syllabus figure: 2000–2500 words. Pages 114–118 were adapted from a different exam board and do not describe this course, so ignore their rules, including the claim on p.111 that a missing bibliography means you "would automatically fail the whole unit."

2. **Tint.** Page 18 has it right: **tone** means adding darker shades, and **tint** means adding white. The p.19 glossary wrongly says "tint, another word for shade." Use the page 18 definitions.

3. **Assessment Objective names.** The handbook uses three different names for the assessment objectives. Use the set on page 39: **Record**, **Explore**, **Develop**, **Present**.

## What is in it

### Visual Elements (pp.7–23)

Six elements, each with a definition and a full-page plate: **Line** (Fred Sandback), **Shape** (Escher, *Sky and Water II*), **Space** (Doug Wheeler), **Tone** (Wolfgang Mattheuer), **Color** (pp.18–21, with a CMYK note and six example plates), **Texture**.

Form is not covered in this chapter. The fullest vocabulary for the elements is at p.81, in the Artist Studies chapter, which adds Form and gives adjective banks for all six. For example, Texture: "flat, raised, smooth, rough, coarse, shiny, glossy, reflective, scratched, matt, satin, frosted, visible, smooth, blended, impasto, washes, glazes, stippled, splattered, bold, timid, lively, layered."

### Principles of Design (pp.24–30)

- **p.25 "How to Read Images"**: the four-stage method, **Description**, **Formal Analysis**, **Interpretation**, **Judgement**, over a grid of 15 paired opposites for visual communication: balanced/unstable, gestural/technical, sharp/diffuse, symmetry/asymmetry, active/still, distorted/realistic, solitary/juxtaposed, flatness/depth, transparent/opaque, regular/irregular, subtle/amplified, vivid/neutral, unified/fragmented, ordered/loose, light/dark.
- **p.26 "17 Key Principles of Design"**: Alignment, Hierarchy, Contrast, Repetition, Proximity, Balance, Color, Space, Emphasis, Proportion, Rhythm, Pattern, Movement, Variety, Unity, Lines, Shapes.
- **pp.27–30**: worked demonstrations of eight: Emphasis (focal point), Hierarchy, Scale/Proportion (size, ratio, divisions), Contrast (in shape, color, scale, layout), Repetition, Unity & Variety, Balance (symmetrical, asymmetrical).

### The four disciplines (pp.48–57)

**Fine Art** (painting, drawing, sculpture, photography, printmaking, mixed media, assemblage) · **Graphic Communication** (illustration, printmaking, packaging, branding, advertising, signage, typography) · **Three-Dimensional Design** (sculpture, ceramics, product, architecture, interior, environmental, set design, jewellery) · **Textiles and Fashion** (fashion design/illustration, costume, constructed textiles, screen printing, batik, digital-printed textiles, surface pattern).

You may focus on one discipline or combine several. Two distinctions:

- **Fine Art** responds to "a theme" only. The other three respond to "a theme or brief."
- **Three-Dimensional Design** also requires you to consider "the setting in which the final outcome will be situated, e.g. interior/exterior, urban/rural" (p.55).

### Themes (pp.66–72)

Guidance on choosing a theme. It is not a list of past Cambridge themes; the handbook does not contain one. It gives nine qualities of an appropriate theme (personal relevance, breadth and depth, creativity and originality, flexibility, researchability, visual and conceptual interest, educational value, relevance to the assessment objectives, engagement with broader issues), then a seven-step selection guide, then the assignment: complete a theme selection form for at least four possible themes, each with related artists and possible artworks, then choose one and justify it. Submit it by the end of the week. This is Component 1 work.

### Assessment Objectives (pp.38–47)

The four AOs are on p.39 with bullet guidance, then a two-page overview and checklist for each. The checklists are usable: AO1's runs seven questions, from "collected images to show your inspiration and stimuli?" to "clearly linked all of your work to your starting point?"

Take the assessment objectives and all rubrics from the 2028-30 syllabus, not from this handbook. The handbook's AO checklists (pp.41–47) are still useful as scaffolding, as long as the objectives they hang from come from the syllabus.

### Personal Study (pp.104–118)

Three study types (p.105), which you can combine: **Technical** (media, surfaces, tools, techniques), **Stylistic** (signs, symbols, metaphor, meaning, figurative and formal elements), **Contextual** (art and society, social/historical/cultural contexts, theories of art).

Keep the integration rule (p.105) in mind: "the study will form the starting point of your practical coursework. The two must link together and inform each other i.e. they are not separate elements but dependent on each other. Your success depends on this point."

Use 2000–2500 words for this study, and ignore the word counts and rules on pp.114–118.

### Portfolio samples (pp.119–205)

About 87 pages of sample work, captioned "Final Outcome" or "SAMPLE 1 / Component 1."
