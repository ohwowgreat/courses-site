# A Level Art & Design · Assessment Criteria

Cambridge's assessment objectives and marking grid for **9479 for examination in 2028, 2029 and 2030**, the specification you sit.

## The four assessment objectives

Cambridge's wording, syllabus p.12:

| AO | Name | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **AO1** | Record | Record ideas, observations and insights relevant to intentions, reflecting critically on work and progress. |
| **AO2** | Explore | Explore and select appropriate resources, media, techniques and processes, reviewing and refining ideas as work develops. |
| **AO3** | Develop | Develop ideas through investigations informed by contextual and other sources, making purposeful connections and demonstrating analytical and critical understanding. |
| **AO4** | Present | Present a personal and coherent response that realises intentions and applies visual language to communicate ideas. |

The AO names are **Record, Explore, Develop, Present**.

## Weighting

**As a percentage of the qualification** (syllabus p.13):

| AO | AS Level % | A Level % |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 Record | 25 | 25 |
| AO2 Explore | 25 | 25 |
| AO3 Develop | 29 | 31 |
| AO4 Present | 21 | 19 |

**As a percentage of each component** (syllabus p.13):

| AO | Component 1 | Component 2 | Component 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 Record | 25 | 25 | 27 |
| AO2 Explore | 25 | 25 | 27 |
| AO3 Develop | 33 | 25 | 33 |
| AO4 Present | 17 | 25 | 13 |

## Marks per component

| Component | Name | Marks | Weighted to | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Portfolio | 60 | 90 | 60% of AS, 30% of A Level |
| **2** | Externally Set Assignment (15-hour supervised test) | 60 | 60 | 40% of AS, 20% of A Level |
| **3** | Personal Investigation (practical + 2000 to 2500 words) | 75 | 150 | 50% of A Level |

All three components are **externally assessed by Cambridge**. Component 1 is internally set with no question paper, and Cambridge assesses the portfolio as a whole, awarding one mark out of 60 (syllabus p.26).

## Component 1: marks per AO

| AO | Marks |
|---|---|
| AO1 Record | 15 |
| AO2 Explore | 15 |
| AO3 Develop | **20** |
| AO4 Present | **10** |
| **Total** | **60** |

Component 1 has a greater focus on AO3 Develop and a smaller focus on AO4 Present.

## Component 1: what you must evidence

Cambridge's elaboration of each AO for the portfolio (syllabus pp.14 to 16), by criterion and the evidence it asks for.

### AO1 Record

- **Selected relevant subject matter from a variety of sources.** Recording from a range of primary and secondary sources such as places, people and objects, to visually investigate a chosen theme, media or area of study. This should include research into relevant artists, designers and craftspeople, cultural investigation, and museum, gallery, workshop or studio visits.
- **Recorded ideas, insights and observations using different methods.** Observational study using the most effective processes available: drawing, sketching, painting, samples, prints, modelling, maquettes, photography. Cambridge asks specifically whether different angles, viewpoints, close-up study, surface quality, colour, tone and form have been investigated.
- **Communicated intentions through critical reflection.** The selected sources and observational studies are relevant to intention, with clear personal engagement visible in the selections.

### AO2 Explore

- **Selected appropriate media, techniques and processes.** Evidence of reviewing and refining media use to select the most relevant to take forward, with the chosen media reflecting your intentions.
- **Developed an understanding of appropriate skills and visual elements.** Understanding of the inherent properties of media and the visual elements, developing technical skill and control. Cambridge states explicitly that this does **not** mean technical skill alone.
- **Experimented with media, techniques and processes, reviewing and refining as work develops.** Different ways of applying techniques, showing a willingness to take creative risks. Either a selection of media explored for their different properties, or one medium used in different ways to explore its full potential.

### AO3 Develop

- **Formed purposeful and personal connections.** Connections forged between research, recording and exploration to create your own developed ideas, concepts and work.
- **Crafted an investigation that demonstrates analytical and critical understanding.** Evidence of how ideas and imagery moved forward over the duration of the study, with effective decision making throughout.
- **Explored visual elements to communicate ideas.** Alternative compositions, angles, viewpoints, perspectives, forms and designs considered within your own developed imagery.
- **Reflected critically on own strengths and weaknesses.** Awareness of successes, strengths and weaknesses in techniques, ideas and imagery, sufficient to outline modifications.

### AO4 Present

- **Realises intentions.** Evidence across the portfolio that intentions were realised, and that work was thoughtfully and appropriately selected and presented so ideas and progress read clearly.
- **Considers application of visual language to communicate ideas.** Understanding of visual elements such as colour, tone, line, form, texture and composition to create a personal visual language, explored throughout the portfolio and used thoughtfully.

## Component 1: the marking grid

Cambridge's five levels, syllabus pp.31 to 33. Each AO is marked on its own level, so a portfolio can sit at different levels on different objectives.

| Level | AO1 Record (15) | AO2 Explore (15) | AO3 Develop (20) | AO4 Present (10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | **13–15.** Perceptive selection of relevant subject matter from a variety of sources · highly effective methods of recording ideas, insights and observations · clear intention communicated consistently through critical reflection | **13–15.** Perceptive selection of media, techniques and processes · highly effective and consistent understanding of appropriate skills to explore visual elements · highly effective experimentation to review and refine ideas as work develops | **17–20.** Perceptive and mature connections between contextual and other sources · focused investigation demonstrating highly effective analytical and critical understanding · accomplished exploration of visual elements · insightful reflection on own strengths and weaknesses | **9–10.** Accomplished portfolio that fully realises intentions · perceptive application of visual language to communicate ideas |
| **4** | **10–12.** Confident selection from a variety of sources · effective methods of recording · clear intention communicated through critical reflection | **10–12.** Confident selection of appropriate media, techniques and processes · effective and consistent understanding of appropriate skills · effective experimentation to review and refine | **13–16.** Confident connections · focused investigation demonstrating confident analytical and critical understanding · effective exploration of visual elements · effective reflection on own strengths and weaknesses | **7–8.** Confident portfolio that effectively realises intentions · confident application of visual language |
| **3** | **7–9.** Competent selection from different sources · competent methods of recording · competent communication of intention through critical reflection | **7–9.** Competent selection of media · competent understanding of appropriate skills · relevant experimentation to review and refine | **9–12.** Competent connections · thorough investigation demonstrating competent analytical and critical understanding · competent exploration of visual elements · competent reflection on own strengths and weaknesses | **5–6.** Competent portfolio that realises intentions · relevant application of visual language |
| **2** | **4–6.** Sufficient selection from different sources · adequate methods of recording · some communication of intention through critical reflection | **4–6.** Sufficient selection of media · adequate understanding of appropriate skills · adequate experimentation to review and refine | **5–8.** Some evidence of connections · adequate investigation demonstrating some analytical and critical understanding · adequate exploration of visual elements · adequate reflection on own strengths and weaknesses | **3–4.** Adequate portfolio that partly realises intentions · some application of visual language |
| **1** | **1–3.** Limited selection from sources · limited methods of recording · basic ability to communicate intention through critical reflection | **1–3.** Limited selection of media · basic understanding of appropriate skills · limited experimentation to review and refine | **1–4.** Limited evidence of connections · limited investigation demonstrating basic analytical and critical understanding · limited exploration of visual elements · basic reflection on own strengths and weaknesses | **1–2.** Basic portfolio that does not fully realise intentions · limited application of visual language |
| **0** | 0. No creditable response | 0. No creditable response | 0. No creditable response | 0. No creditable response |

Reading down any column, the level words run: **perceptive · confident · competent · sufficient or adequate · limited or basic.** The same ladder runs through all three components.

## How this maps onto the semester

Component 1 is marked **as a whole portfolio, once, by Cambridge**. The attainments in [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-assessments|the register]] are BNDS milestones sampling that portfolio as it is built, so each one is marked against the AO columns it draws on rather than against all four:

| Attainment | AO columns used | Marks available |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Recording | AO1 | 15 |
| A2 Exploration and artist study | AO2 + AO3 | 35 |
| A3 Development | AO3 | 20 |
| A4 Final portfolio | AO1 to AO4 | 60 |

Under [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|the BNDS framework]] these marks are then curved rather than reported raw, so a milestone mark is a Forecast Grade signal, not a Cambridge score.
