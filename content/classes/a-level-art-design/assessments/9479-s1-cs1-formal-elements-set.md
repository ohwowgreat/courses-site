## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Produce a study set covering line, tone and form, texture and color
2. Compose a recording page that argues something rather than displaying exercises
3. Choose and sequence your own work instead of showing all of it

## Description

Two things are due together. The **study set** from the week: line, tone and form, texture, color. And **Recording Page 1**, composed from that set.

The page is the harder half. A recording page is not a display case. It has to argue something: this is the direction, and here is the evidence for it. That means selection and sequence matter more than coverage. A page holding your four best studies in a considered order beats a page holding all nine.

## Guidelines

- Work in the sketchbook, not on loose sheets.
- Cover all four elements in the study set. Do not skip color because it is slower.
- For Recording Page 1, choose from the set. You are not obliged to use everything.
- Decide the order before you glue anything down. What does the eye meet first, and why that?
- Annotate briefly: what each study was testing, in your own words.
- Leave room. A crowded page cannot argue.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | All four elements studied with evident control. The page selects, sequences and annotates so a reader follows the thinking without you there. Something has been left out on purpose. |
| **Secure** | The set is complete and the page is composed, though the sequence is more habit than argument, or the annotation restates what is visible. |
| **Developing** | Studies present but uneven, or the page shows everything produced with no selection. Annotation is labels rather than reasoning. |
| **Not yet** | Elements missing, or the page is a collection of exercises with no order. |

The examiner reads the page as evidence of how you think. Coverage alone does not show that.
