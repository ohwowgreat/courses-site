# A Level Art & Design · S1 CS6: Stage 3 Page and the Selection Note

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Due | Fri 2026-10-23, written annotation |
| What | The Stage 3 page, plus the media selection note |
| If the trip takes Friday | It collects Monday |
| Feeds | A2 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Lay out a page that reads as reasoning rather than as a record
2. Name a chosen medium in assessment-objective language
3. Say what a medium does to your particular theme

## Description

The **Stage 3 page**, reading in this order: reasoning, experiments, decision, trial, refinement. A reader should see the argument move.

With it, the **media selection note**, naming your chosen media **in AO language**: not "I like charcoal" but what this medium does to this theme. Charcoal that smudges into a dusk you keep failing to paint is a reason. Charcoal you enjoy is not.

## Guidelines

- Five parts, in order: reasoning, experiments, decision, trial, refinement.
- The decision must come after the experiments on the page, not before.
- The trial is one piece of evidence that the decision works.
- Selection note: three or four sentences.
- Use the assessment vocabulary deliberately. It is how the examiner reads you.
- Say what the medium does to the theme, not what you enjoy about it.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | The page reads as an argument in five moves, and a reader can see why the decision follows from the experiments. The note ties medium to theme in AO language. |
| **Secure** | All five parts present in order; the link from experiment to decision is stated but thin. |
| **Developing** | The page records what happened rather than reasoning about it, or the note gives preference rather than justification. |
| **Not yet** | Parts missing or out of order, or no selection made. |
