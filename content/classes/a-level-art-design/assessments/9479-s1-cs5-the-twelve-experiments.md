# A Level Art & Design · S1 CS5: The Twelve Experiments

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Due | Fri 2026-10-16, in the sketchbook |
| What | Twelve media experiments, each labelled |
| Requirement | At least one must have failed, and say so |
| Feeds | A2 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Test a medium with a question rather than trying it out
2. Record what an experiment showed, including when it showed the idea does not work
3. Take a risk that could visibly fail

## Description

All **twelve media experiments** documented, each labelled with **the question it tested** and **what it showed**.

An experiment has a question. "What happens if I dilute the ink and drag it with card?" is a question. "Ink experiment" is not.

**At least one experiment must have failed**, and its annotation says so and says why. This is not a formality. A safe dozen is the one way to lose marks here: twelve experiments that all worked means twelve experiments that risked nothing.

## Guidelines

- Twelve. Count them.
- Each one gets two lines: the question, and what it showed.
- Vary the variable deliberately. Twelve versions of one idea is one experiment.
- Keep the failures in the book. Do not tidy them out.
- Name the failure honestly: what you expected, what happened, why you think it happened.
- Work on this theme, not in the abstract. The experiments must be usable later.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Twelve genuinely different experiments, each with a real question and a recorded result. At least one failure analysed rather than apologised for. The set visibly narrows toward a choice. |
| **Secure** | Twelve present and labelled, most with real questions. A failure is recorded but thinly explained. |
| **Developing** | Fewer than twelve, or several are variations of one idea, or labels describe the material rather than the question. |
| **Not yet** | Undocumented experiments, or no failures anywhere, which usually means nothing was risked. |
