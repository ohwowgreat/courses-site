## Overview

This week Unit 3 turns from your own experiments to an artist who solves the problem you are working on. You choose and analyze Artist 1, write their meaning and context, study their method by copying, then answer them with your own personal response. You produce the Artist 1 pages, which are graded as **[[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s2-assessments|CS5]]** on Fri 2027-04-16. ## The ideas

### Choosing Artist 1

You choose **Artist 1** for the problem they solve, not because you like them. Look for an artist whose work solves a problem live in your own investigation: a composition, a process, or a way with the material. Then analyze how the work is made and why it holds, using specialist language.

### Meaning and context

Place the artist: what the work meant, when it was made, and for whom. This is the **first material of your research paper**. Write it properly from the start so it gathers into the written element, not as a detached bibliography added later.

### Method study

**Copy to understand, not to own.** Copy a passage of the work to learn how it was made, then note what the copying taught you that looking alone could not.

### Personal response

**Respond, do not imitate.** Take the artist's method and turn it on your own theme, working from your own sources. A copy of their work with your subject placed on top is not a response.

### The influence page

Lay out the artist page so the influence is visible in your own work, not just described in words.

## Practice

Try each question before you open its answer. The artist is a tool. Choose tools for the job.

**1.** State the choosing rule, and what counts as a problem an artist can solve.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Chosen for the problem they solve in your investigation, not because you like them. A problem is concrete: a composition you cannot resolve, a process you need, a way with the material your theme demands. Liking is welcome. It is just not the reason.

</details>

**2.** Why does Tuesday's meaning-and-context writing count twice?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

It is the artist study's context, and it is the first material of your research paper. Written properly now, it gathers into the written element from the start, instead of becoming a detached bibliography bolted on in October. This is what integrated means in practice.

</details>

**3.** The method study rule is copy to understand, not to own. What does its note record?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

What the copying taught that looking could not. You copy a passage to feel how it was made: the order of marks, the resistance of the material, the decisions hidden in the surface. The note names those. The copy itself is evidence of study, not a page of your development.

</details>

**4.** What is the test of Thursday's personal response?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Respond, do not imitate. A copy with your subject on top is not a response. The response takes the method and turns it on your theme from your own sources, so the influence is visible in work that could only be yours.

</details>

**5.** CS5 is marked on two things: the influence shows in your own work, and the context writes well enough to carry into the paper. Before Friday, check both on your own pages. Then the homework: read one source on Artist 1 toward the paper, and shortlist Artist 2 as a different angle on the same problem.
