## Overview

This lesson is the exam proper. You realize the final piece you planned at A3 inside the **15-hour supervised test**, under exam conditions. You add nothing new and invent nothing under supervision. The days before the test are for refinement and rehearsal only, because content closed at A3. Between sittings your work is stored securely. The test is externally marked by Cambridge. Assembly of the submission package follows in [[classes/a-level-art-design/lesson-plans/9479-s4-lesson-08-assembly-and-a4-the-submission-package|Lesson 08]].

## The ideas

### Nothing invented under supervision

**Nothing invented under supervision** means the outcome you planned and marked at [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s4-assessments|A3]] is realized as it stands: no additions, no new ideas made up during the test. The plan is both your safety and your mark. This is why the elastic days before the test go to refinement and rehearsal, never to new content.

### Secure storage between sittings

Your work is stored securely and documented between sittings. There is **no access, no additions, no exceptions**. The integrity of the test depends on this.

### The test floats

The **15-hour supervised test** floats: it fits only the mock window or a school exam window, never the daily timetable. This is why the dates below are projected. On one reading the whole unit (test, Unit 5, [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s4-assessments|A4]], [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s4-assessments|CS7]]) pulls forward one week.

## Day by day

- **Elastic window week.** The four teaching days before the test go to refinement and rehearsal of your plan. This is not new work.
- **The 15-hour supervised test.** You realize your plan under exam conditions. On one reading the test runs Mon 2028-04-10 to Fri 04-14, five sittings of 3 hours. On the other it runs around Qingming (Tue 04-04), four sittings of 3h45, with the whole tail moved one week earlier. There is no homework: the test is the work, and you rest between sittings. These dates are projected and may shift when the school publishes the calendar; the exact schedule will be confirmed in class.

## Review

Check you can:

- Realize your planned final piece inside the **15-hour supervised test** under exam conditions.
- Explain why you invent nothing under supervision, because content closed at A3.
- Use the elastic days before the test for refinement and rehearsal only.
- State the storage rule between sittings: secure, documented, no access, no additions, no exceptions.

## Practice

Read these before the elastic window opens. The test rewards preparation it can no longer change.

**1.** What are the elastic days for, and what are they never for?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Refinement and rehearsal of the plan. Never new content, because content closed at A3. A new idea arriving now is a temptation wearing the costume of inspiration, and the plan already marked is both the safety and the mark.

</details>

**2.** State the test's one rule.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Nothing is invented under supervision. The fifteen hours realize the plan: the composition, palette, and materials decided and marked weeks ago. Every hour spent deciding inside the test is an hour taken from building.

</details>

**3.** What happens to the work between sittings?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Secure, documented storage. No access, no additions, no exceptions. The integrity of the test depends on it, and the rule protects you: work that could have been touched cannot prove it was not.

</details>

**4.** Why is there no homework during the test block?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because the test is the work, and rest between sittings is preparation. Fifteen hours of sustained building rewards a fresh hand and a clear head more than any late-night addition could.

</details>

**5.** The rehearsal that costs nothing: walk the plan hour by hour, aloud or on paper. What does sitting one block in, what does sitting three resolve, where are the risky passages and which sitting meets them? A plan you can narrate is a plan you can execute.
