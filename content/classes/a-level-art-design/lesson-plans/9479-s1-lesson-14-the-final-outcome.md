## Overview

In this lesson you build the final outcome in one continuous run and resolve it to a unified surface. You work from underdrawing through to surface across three build days, take a hard look at the piece halfway through, then spend two days resolving it. You write [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-course-skills|CS10]], the mid-production review reflection, which is collected on Fri 2026-12-18 and is direct practice for [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-a4-final-portfolio|A4]]. The piece is declared finished in writing on Tue 2026-12-22, the last session before the break. The final dates may shift when the school publishes the calendar for this period.

## 1 · Learn

### The final outcome

**The final outcome** is the real piece. You build it in one sustained run, from underdrawing to finished surface, following the plan you made in Lesson 13. That plan governs every session. If you need to change course, that is a conference, not a drift: raise the change and agree it, do not wander off the plan. The focus for this lesson is **AO4 Present**.

### The build run

The build run is three continuous days on one project:

- Day 1: underdrawing and block-in.
- Day 2: mid-stages, with the main relationships established.
- Day 3: production and detail.

Each day you start with a re-entry look, build, then annotate against your plan.

### The mid-production review

**The mid-production review** is a hard look at the piece and the plan side by side, taken while there is still time to change things. A teacher pass and a peer pass apply a protocol, and the changes you need are listed and ranked. You write **CS10**, the review reflection: what changes, in what order, and why. This is direct practice for A4. You then go straight back to work and execute the top-ranked changes while the review is minutes old.

### Resolving the outcome

The resolve run is two days. First you resolve the focal areas and unify the whole surface. Then you make final touches and stop. You declare the piece finished in writing.

## 2 · Do

- **Mon 12-14.** Build day 1: underdrawing and block-in. Sketchbook work continues through W16.
- **Tue 12-15.** Build day 2: mid-stages, with the main relationships established.
- **Thu 12-17.** Build day 3: production and detail, with conferences concentrated here.
- **Fri 12-18 (double).** P3: the mid-production review. You compare the piece and the plan, apply the protocol, and list and rank the changes. You write and hand in CS10. P4: you execute the top-ranked changes right away.
- **Mon 12-21.** Resolve day 1: read the criteria, note the Thu 2027-01-07 date, then work on the focal areas and surface unity.
- **Tue 12-22.** Resolve day 2: final touches, then stop. Declare the piece finished in writing. This is the last session before the break, and there is no homework over the break.

## Assessment

**CS10** (mid-production review reflection) is collected Fri 2026-12-18. It is direct practice for A4. See the [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-assessments|full register]].

## 3 · Check

Check you can:

- Build the final outcome in one sustained run, from underdrawing to surface.
- Take the mid-production review seriously and act on the midpoint hard look while change is still possible.
- Write CS10, saying what changes, in what order, and why.
- Resolve the focal areas and unify the whole surface.
- Declare the piece finished in writing.

## Practice

Try each question before you open its answer. The semester's longest run deserves a clear head each morning.

**1.** Name what each of the three build days does.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Day one, underdrawing and block-in. Day two, the main relationships established. Day three, production and detail. Structure before refinement, always: detail on day one is procrastination wearing a brush.

</details>

**2.** The plan governs every session, and deviation is a conference, not a drift. What is the difference?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

A conference is a change you decide out loud, with the plan on the table, and then annotate. A drift is a change you discover later. Both alter the piece. Only one of them is development you can evidence.

</details>

**3.** What does CS10 actually collect on Friday?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The review reflection: what changes, in what order, and why. The review puts the piece and the plan side by side while there is still time to act. Ranked changes are the point: three days remain, and they go to the top of the list, not to everything.

</details>

**4.** Recite the resolve run.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The Friday double's second period executes the top-ranked review changes while the review is minutes old. Monday, focal areas and surface unity. Tuesday, final touches and stop, with the piece declared finished in writing. The order matters: the ranked changes first, then the whole surface brought to one level, then the courage to stop.

</details>

**5.** The daily ritual for every session: a re-entry look before touching the piece, and an exit annotation against the plan. One line each evening answering one question: what did today's work do that the plan asked for? Those honest lines are the build run's whole story.
