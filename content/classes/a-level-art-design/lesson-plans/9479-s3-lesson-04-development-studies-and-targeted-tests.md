## Overview

This week you push the candidate compositions until development decides things. You make development studies that resolve real questions, then run targeted tests on what is still unresolved and change the composition on the evidence. You end with a structured critique that judges the candidates against your intention. The deliverable is the targeted tests, graded as **[[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s3-assessments|CS4]]** on Fri 2027-09-24, plus the critique. What survives the critique is refined toward the evaluation and the Stage 6 page in [[classes/a-level-art-design/lesson-plans/9479-s3-lesson-05-evaluation-and-the-national-day-break|Lesson 05]].

## 1 · Learn

### Development studies

**Development studies** are studies that decide things. Each one answers a real question the composition poses, not a general exercise.

### Targeted tests

**Targeted tests** run only on what is still unresolved: a color relationship, a passage that is not reading, a material that may not hold. A targeted test changes the composition because of evidence, meaning the test result and not a preference. A test that changes nothing was decoration.

### Tested, rejected, decided

Development must read as **tested, rejected, decided** on the Stage 6 page, not as a gallery of effort. The page should show what you tried, what you dropped, and what you chose.

### Judging against the intention

The critique judges the candidates **against the intention, not the effort**. This is the standard the whole investigation is marked by: whether the work meets what you set out to do, not how much work went in.

## 2 · Do

- **Mon 09-20.** Studio: development studies. Each study answers a question the composition actually poses.
- **Tue 09-21 / Wed 09-22.** Targeted tests over a two-day run, on only what is unresolved.
- **Thu 09-23.** Studio: act on the tests. Change the work because of the test result, not a preference.
- **Fri 09-24.** Structured critique reading the candidates against the intention. CS4 marks the targeted tests. Homework (≤30 min): write up one test, what it asked, what it showed, and what you changed.

## 3 · Check

Check you can:

- Make **development studies** that resolve real questions the composition poses.
- Run **targeted tests** on only what is unresolved, and change the composition because of evidence.
- Show development as **tested, rejected, decided**, not as a gallery of effort.
- Judge the candidates **against the intention, not the effort**.

## Practice

Try each question before you open its answer. Development that decides things is the week's standard.

**1.** What makes a development study one that decides things?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

It answers a question the composition actually poses. A study with no question attached is practice, and practice is not development. Before each study, say the question. After it, say the answer.

</details>

**2.** Give three examples of what a targeted test runs on, and the boundary that keeps it targeted.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

A color relationship that is not settled, a passage that is not reading, a material that may not hold. The boundary: only what is unresolved. A test on something already working is a fresh round of exploration wearing a lab coat.

</details>

**3.** What must happen on Thursday for the tests to have counted?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The composition changes because of evidence: the test result, not a preference. A test that changes nothing was decoration. The change, annotated with the result that caused it, is the AO3 evidence.

</details>

**4.** How must the development read on the Stage 6 page?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Tested, rejected, decided. Not a gallery of effort. Each item on the page is there because it tested something, was rejected for a reason, or decided a direction. Friday's critique applies the same standard: candidates judged against the intention, not the work that went in.

</details>

**5.** The homework is one test written up in three lines: what it asked, what it showed, what you changed. If the third line is empty, either the test failed to test or the result was ignored. Both are worth knowing before CS4.
