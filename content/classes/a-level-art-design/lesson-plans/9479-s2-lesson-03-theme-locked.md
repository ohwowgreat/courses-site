## Overview

This week your theme stops being a proposal and becomes the thing you will make. On Monday you lock it in writing, signed. From there you build on that theme: a statement of intent in your own words, a research plan with real reading and dates, and your first primary-source shoot. You produce **[[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s2-assessments|CS2]]**, the statement of intent plus annotated references, due Fri 2027-03-12, and Unit 1 closes. This work feeds directly into **[[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s2-assessments|A1]]**, the recording milestone next week. These dates are set for 2027 and may shift when the school publishes the calendar for that period.

## The ideas

### Locking the theme

The **theme lock** is the point where your theme is fixed in writing and signed against the exemplar criteria. It is the unit's real gate. After Monday, the investigation runs on this theme all the way to submission in December. There are **no restarts**. From here the work is to make the theme well, not to keep looking for a better idea.

### Statement of intent

A **statement of intent** is a short account of your investigation in your own words: what you will look at, why, and how you expect to work. It must be yours, not borrowed exemplar language. The examiner reads it as *your* intent.

### Research plan

A **research plan** lists your reading, sources, and dates: what you will read, which primary sources you will shoot and when, and how the research paper starts gathering alongside the visual work. The research is **integrated from the start, not bolted on** at the end.

### Primary-source shoot and annotation

A **primary-source shoot** is your own first-hand photography of real sources: light, angle, series. Nothing found, nothing borrowed. This is reference you will draw from for months, so shoot far more than you think you need. When you **annotate** the images, you name what each image *shows*, not what it makes you feel.

## Day by day

- **Mon 03-08.** You sign the locked theme against the exemplar criteria. This is the gate: the investigation now runs on this theme to submission in December, with no restarts.
- **Tue 03-09.** You finalize your statement of intent, writing the investigation in your own words.
- **Wed 03-10.** You write your research plan: the reading, the primary sources you will shoot and when, and how the research paper gathers alongside the visual work.
- **Thu 03-11.** You run your first primary-source shoot from first-hand sources. Shoot far more than you think you need.
- **Fri 03-12.** You select and annotate your shoot, naming what each image shows. **CS2** is due, and Unit 1 closes. Homework: extend your primary-source shooting, first-hand and dated, ahead of next week's Stage 1 recording pages.

## Review

Check you can:

- Explain what the **theme lock** means and why there are no restarts after it.
- Write a **statement of intent** in your own words.
- Build a **research plan** with real reading, sources, and dates.
- Run a **primary-source shoot** from first-hand sources.
- Annotate images by naming what each one shows, not what it makes you feel.

## Practice

Try each question before you open its answer. After Monday there are no restarts.

**1.** What does the lock mean, practically, from Tuesday morning onward?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The investigation runs on this theme all the way to submission in December. The work now is to make it well, not to keep looking for a better idea. The lock is signed against the exemplar criteria, and it carries no mark because it is a gate, not a grade.

</details>

**2.** Why must the statement of intent be in your own words?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because the examiner reads it as your intent. Borrowed exemplar language describes someone else's investigation politely. Yours has to say what you will look at, why, and how you expect to work, in sentences only you could have written.

</details>

**3.** Name the three things the research plan pins down.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The reading, the shoots with their dates, and how the research paper begins gathering alongside the visual work. Integrated from the start, not bolted on at the end. A paper that starts in October reads like it started in October.

</details>

**4.** State the rules for the first primary-source shoot and its annotation.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Light, angle, series, from first-hand sources, and shoot far more than you think you need: this is the reference you will draw from for months. Nothing found, nothing borrowed. Then annotate each select for what it shows, not what it makes you feel.

</details>

**5.** Over the weekend, extend the shooting: first-hand, dated, thirty minutes at most per day. A1, the recording milestone, sits Fri 2027-03-19. The foundations you lay this week are what it reviews.
