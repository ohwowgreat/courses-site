## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Choose an artist because they solve your problem, not because you like them
2. Analyze the work, then place its meaning in context
3. Copy the method to learn it, then answer the artist in your own work so the influence is visible, not just claimed

## Goal

Choose your first artist because they solve your problem, not because you like them. The week runs the full artist study arc: analyze, understand in context, copy the method to learn it, then answer the artist with your own work. Influence must be visible in the work, not only claimed in the writing.

## Key content: artist pools, by theme

Choose from the pool for your committed theme. To swap to an artist outside the pool, make a one-to-one case.

- **Reflections and Surfaces** — Richard Estes (urban glass), Janet Fish (glass, light, still-life color), M. C. Escher (reflecting-sphere drawings).
- **Worn and Weathered** — Antoni Tàpies (matter and surface), Aaron Siskind (photographed peeling walls), Anselm Kiefer (material and memory).
- **Hands at Work** — Käthe Kollwitz (working hands and figures), Albrecht Dürer (praying-hands studies), Yoshitoshi or a contemporary craft documentary photographer as a non-European anchor.
- **Structures of the Everyday** — Charles Sheeler (industrial structure), Lyonel Feininger (architecture into geometry), Rachel Whiteread (structure into 3D casting).

## How it runs

- **Mon 10-26** — read the criteria; pools presented, two works per artist; choose and begin your analysis (composition, tone, mark, color); write your choice rationale: which problem this artist solves.
- **Tue 10-27** — meaning and context: what the work meant then, what it means for you now; writing frame for the ESL register; in peer reading, strike every sentence that could describe any artist.
- **Wed 10-28** — method study: copy a passage of the artist's method in the artist's logic; annotate what the copying taught you.
- **Thu 10-29** — personal response: work your own source through what the artist taught; exit: name the artist's fingerprint in your piece.
- **Fri 10-30** — the influence note: point at the evidence; assemble your artist pages; **CS7 collected.**
