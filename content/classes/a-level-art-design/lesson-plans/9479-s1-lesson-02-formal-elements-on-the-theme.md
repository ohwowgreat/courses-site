## Overview

This lesson works the **formal elements** directly on your own theme, not in the abstract: line, tone and form, texture, and color. Across the week you make a study for each element and then build Recording Page 1, so your theme is seen through five instruments and your first portfolio page argues something. You also set up your digital portfolio and file the study set into it. The study set and Recording Page 1 form [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-course-skills|CS1]], collected Mon 2026-09-14, which is practice for [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-a1-recording-milestone|A1]]. This lesson ends Unit 1.

## 1 · Learn

### Line

**Contour line** is a line that follows the outer edge of an object. **Structural line** is a line that describes how the object is built underneath. You make two A4 line studies of the same object, each with a different instrument, then annotate which line told the truth about the object.

### Tone and form

The **nine-step tonal ladder** is a scale of tone from light to dark in nine steps. You make a tonal study in charcoal from a theme source, with no outlines: tone alone builds the form. Then you push the study further: larger, with a darker range, further than feels safe. Going too far is useful, so annotate the failure and what it taught you.

### Texture

The **mark inventory** is a set of six marks, one per tool, that you can call on to stand for a surface. In **surface matching** you render three textures from theme sources, each with the mark that fits it, so the mark and the photographed surface can be matched to each other.

### Color

**Limited-palette mixing** is mixing toward an observed color from only a few starting colors. You make three color studies, each with its swatch, the mixing route you took, and the color shown in context.

### Recording Page 1

**Recording Page 1** is a portfolio page that argues something rather than displaying exercises. You compose it from the week's studies, choosing what to include first, then setting it out. You then set up the digital portfolio from the template and file the study set into it.

## 2 · Do

- **Mon 09-07.** Contour line versus structural line: two A4 line studies of the same object, each with a different instrument, annotated for which line told the truth.
- **Tue 09-08.** The nine-step tonal ladder, then one tonal study from a theme source in charcoal, with no outlines.
- **Thu 09-10.** Push the tonal study: larger, wider tonal range, further than feels safe; annotate the failure and its lesson.
- **Fri 09-11 (double).** Texture: build the mark inventory, then render three theme textures, each with the mark that fits, for peers to match to the photographed surface. Color: limited-palette mixing toward observed color, with three color studies.
- **Mon 09-14.** Read the exemplar's opening page as an argument, then compose Recording Page 1 from the week's studies, selection first, and set up the digital portfolio from the template. CS1 collected.

Each night, add one further study from the theme to your sketchbook, alternating the element; ten minutes each, dated.

## Assessment

**CS1** (formal elements study set + Recording Page 1, sketchbook) collects Mon 2026-09-14 — see the [[classes/a-level-art-design/assessments/9479-s1-assessments|full register]]. It is marked for evidence of observation and honest annotation, not polish, and returned inside five working days.

## 3 · Check

Check you can:

- Work each formal element, line, tone and form, texture, and color, directly on your own theme.
- Tell contour line from structural line and say which described an object better.
- Build form with a nine-step tonal ladder in charcoal, using tone without outlines.
- Match a texture to a mark from your mark inventory through surface matching.
- Reach an observed color by limited-palette mixing and record its swatch, mixing route, and context.
- Compose Recording Page 1 so it argues something, and file your study set in the digital portfolio.

## Practice

Try each question before you open its answer. Everything here is CS1 material.

**1.** Contour line and structural line: what does each do, and how do you know which one told the truth?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Contour line follows the seen edge. Structural line builds how the object is constructed. The one that told the truth is the one a stranger could rebuild the object from. Your annotation should say which, and why.

</details>

**2.** The tonal rule on the board: no outlines. Why?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because the world does not show outlines. An edge in tone is where one value meets another. Draw the outline and you flatten the form you were supposed to find. The nine-step ladder is there so your darks actually reach dark.

</details>

**3.** Wednesday's instruction is to push the tonal study further than feels safe, and failure is the point. What do you do with the failure?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Annotate it and its lesson. A pushed study that broke, with one honest line about where and why, is worth more to the portfolio than a safe study that proves nothing. This is what marked-for-observation-not-polish means.

</details>

**4.** Recording Page 1 must argue something, not display work. What does that look like in practice?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Selection before layout: choose the studies that make one point about your theme, sequence them so a stranger follows the point, and let the annotation say what was learned. Coverage is not an argument. Eight exercises pasted down is a display.

</details>

**5.** The nightly habit starts now: one further study per night from the theme, alternating element, ten minutes, dated. Keep the dates honest. A dated sketchbook is the portfolio's spine, and it cannot be faked in the last week.
