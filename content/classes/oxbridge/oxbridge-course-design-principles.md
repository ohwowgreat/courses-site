# Oxbridge Course Design Principles

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · course apparatus

Six rules. They govern the shape of each session, the wording of each question, and what counts as a session going well. They are written for this course, and several apply to any ESL seminar room.

## 1. The question does the work

The teacher does not deliver a position. The question draws one out.

> A session is well designed when the students argue and the teacher mostly redirects. If the teacher talks for more than a third of the session, the question was too closed.

That last sentence is the test. It is about question design. A teacher talking too much is a symptom.

## 2. Scaffold from what students already own

**Never open with a philosopher.** Open with something you can already answer from experience: a map, a thermometer, a feeling of cold, a video game boundary, a photo on a phone. The philosopher arrives later, and only to **name** what you have already been arguing.

This is the most important sequencing rule in the course, and every session shows it:

- Berger goes on the board in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|W01]] only after students have generated the idea themselves.
- Barthes arrives at the *end* of [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06]], to name what students built out of maps and video-game boundaries.
- Spinoza's reframe closes [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w08-can-the-body-be-trusted|W08]] rather than opening it.

## 3. Questions should be plain on the surface and catalytic underneath

The register to aim for:

- "What does the world look like?"
- "Where does the body end?"
- "Who agreed that this is real?"

Avoid academic scaffolding **in the question itself**. No sub-questions, no anticipated responses, no explanation attached. The complication belongs in the follow-up, spoken live, not printed on the slide.

This is why [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-question-bank|the question bank]] is written as single lines.

## 4. Close with a defensible position, not a summary

Every session ends by asking each student to state a position they will defend under pressure. The closing question is **carried out of the room, not resolved in it**.

## 5. Complicate from inside the student's own commitment

The Socratic move is not to bring in a counterexample from outside. It is to find the consequence you have not yet noticed inside your own claim, and to ask about that.

A worked example:

> "You said the body cannot lie. What about phantom limb pain?" is weaker than "You said the body cannot lie. Does that mean a person who feels pain in a missing leg is feeling something true?"

Both use the same case. The second makes your own claim do the work.

## 6. Register

Students here read English as a second language. Sentences should be short. Vocabulary should be plain. **Difficulty should live in the idea, never in the syntax.**

> A hard concept in easy words is the target. An easy concept in hard words is failure.

This rule is why the course can run Foucault, Bergson, and Emmelhainz with Grade 11 ESL students without dilution: the concepts are not simplified, the sentences are.

## How the principles show up elsewhere

- The five-part session shape in [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template|the template]] is principle 1 and 4 made structural: two minutes of silent writing so every student enters with something of their own, and a commit movement where **neutrality is the one banned position**.
- [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-interview-preparation|Interview Preparation]] is principle 5 turned into a drill. The push line is the same move, performed by a student instead of the teacher.
- The four qualities the course names, commitment, pressure, precision, movement, are principle 4 stated as feedback. Nothing is graded ([[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|Assessment — none]]), so they are said aloud in the commit movement rather than written on anything: "that is a survey, not a position" is a sentence you can act on right away.
