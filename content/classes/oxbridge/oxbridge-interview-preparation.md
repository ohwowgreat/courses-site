# Oxbridge Interview Preparation

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · course apparatus

## What the interview actually tests

**Not knowledge.** This course is built on one claim:

> The Oxbridge interview tests whether a candidate can think while being watched, revise a position without collapsing, and stay with a difficult question rather than resolving it prematurely.

The whole seminar prepares you for that. Three moves are practiced explicitly.

## The three moves

**1. Take a position quickly.** If you hedge, you lose the interview in the first minute. State a claim, then qualify it. **Never qualify first.**

**2. Absorb the push without abandoning the claim.** When the interviewer objects, do not surrender and do not just repeat yourself. Use one of two sentences:

> "That case is harder for my position, and here is why I think it still holds"
>
> "That case actually defeats my position, so let me narrow it."

**3. Follow the consequence.** The interviewer is usually asking where your claim leads, not whether it is true. **Volunteer the consequence before being asked.**

## The push line format

Every question in [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-question-bank|the question bank]] has two parts: a plain opening question, and a **push line** that replicates the interviewer's follow-up move. Practice them in pairs, with one student as interviewer.

> **Opening.** Is a photograph evidence?
>
> **Push.** You said it depends on whether it was edited. Every photograph involves choices about framing, exposure, and moment. So is every photograph edited, and does your distinction survive that?

## Mock interview format

**Twelve minutes per student. One question, no preparation time.**

The interviewer's job is to **push three times, not to be fair.**

Debrief for three minutes afterward on one thing only:

> At which push did the candidate stop thinking and start defending?

## Where the moves get drilled inside the course

The three moves are not confined to mock interviews. Each has a home session:

- **Take a position quickly:** Movement 3 of every session. One sentence, one reason, no abstentions. [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template|The template]] bans neutrality precisely to train this.
- **Absorb the push:** [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07]] runs it as a paired drill with the three named pushes (the consequence, the hard case, the definition), and debriefs on the same question the mock does. With no essays in the course, W07 is entirely this drill, which makes it the most direct interview rehearsal in the fourteen weeks.
- **Follow the consequence:** [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|principle 5]]. All term the teacher asks about the consequence inside your own claim, so you hear the move roughly forty times before you have to perform it.
- **Movement under argument is praised, not penalized.** [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|W05]] makes changing position under pressure an explicit skill: anyone who moves columns must name the quotation that moved them.

## Scheduling

Mock interviews run in the week following [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]], if the timetable allows. This week is not one of the fourteen.

A full round is about 15 minutes per student: twelve minutes plus a three-minute debrief. One 45-minute session covers three students, so a larger cohort needs several sessions or a block outside the timetable. The number of sessions depends on cohort size and will be confirmed in class.

## The connection to the closing session

W14 aims at the same capacity the interview is testing:

> The best outcome is a room that can hold the paradox without either collapsing into relativism or fleeing back to naive realism. That capacity, holding a genuinely open question without needing to close it, is precisely what an Oxbridge interviewer is testing for.
