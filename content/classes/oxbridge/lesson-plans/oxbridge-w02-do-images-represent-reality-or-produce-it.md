# Oxbridge W02: Do Images Represent Reality or Produce It?

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-1-the-image|Unit 1: The Image]] · Week 2 of 14 · [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|← W01]] | [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w03-can-measurement-reach-the-thing-itself|W03 →]]

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Core question | Is the quality of an image a technical fact or a political one? |
| Duration | 45 minutes, [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template\|standard template]] |
| Reading | [[entities/hito-steyerl\|Steyerl]], "In Defense of the Poor Image", e-flux |
| Prepare | The single sentence in Steyerl you found **most arguable**, ready to say which and why |
| Bring | Two versions of one image: high resolution, and badly compressed / screen-recorded / re-uploaded |
| Take away | A thermometer measures temperature. Does it show you the temperature, or change it? |

## Opening prompt (5 minutes)

Both versions of the image on screen.

> Which of these is more real?

Two minutes of silent writing.

## Movement 1 · elicit (10 minutes)

Which one did you choose, and what does "more real" mean in your answer?

Where did each of these images come from, and **who paid for the difference**? Does a high resolution image cost something a poor image does not?

## Movement 2 · complicate (15 minutes)

Steyerl's central reversal:

> Resolution is not only a technical property. It is a marker of value, of institutional access, and of who is permitted to circulate. **The poor image is degraded because it moved. The high resolution image is pristine because it was kept.**

Questions:

- If an image loses quality every time it is shared, what does high quality tell us about how much it was shared?
- Steyerl says the poor image is about defiance and appropriation as much as about conformism and exploitation. Can something be both?
- Does an image represent an event, or does it produce the event **as a public thing**?

Follow-ups: Is a screenshot of a painting a painting? If an event is not photographed, did it happen publicly?

## Movement 3 · commit (10 minutes)

Do images show us the world, or make the world showable? One sentence, one reason.

If you said in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|W01]] that perception can be trained toward neutrality, say now what a neutral image would look like **and who would be able to afford it**.

## Closing question (5 minutes)

> A thermometer measures temperature. Does it show you the temperature, or change it?

This question carries into [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w03-can-measurement-reach-the-thing-itself|W03]].
