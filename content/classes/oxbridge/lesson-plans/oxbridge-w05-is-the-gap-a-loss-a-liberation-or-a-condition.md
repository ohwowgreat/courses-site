# Oxbridge W05: Is the Gap a Loss, a Liberation, or a Condition?

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2: The Gap]] · Week 5 of 14 · [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w04-is-there-a-reality-behind-appearances|← W04]] | [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w06-does-reality-have-a-history|W06 →]]

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Core question | Given that no representation reaches the thing itself, how should we live? |
| Duration | 45 minutes, [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template\|standard template]] |
| Reading | **None.** Four quotations, delivered live |
| Prepared | Arrive with a position and a case, from W04's closing question |

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Take one of the three readings of the gap and defend it against the other two
2. Work from four quotations delivered live, without a text to prepare against
3. Move from an epistemological claim to a claim about how to live, and notice they are different moves

## Opening prompt (5 minutes)

The question is already on the board from last session. Write your position and one reason. Two minutes, silent.

## Movement 1 · elicit (10 minutes)

You give your position in one sentence. The room is sorted into three columns on the board: loss · liberation · condition.

| Column | The claim | Push |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss** | "There is a real world and we are locked out of it" | Locked out implies you know what is on the other side. **How?** |
| **Liberation** | "If there is no fixed reality, we get to make one" | Make one **out of what**? |
| **Condition** | "It is just what having a mind is like" | If it is simply the condition, why does it feel like a loss to so many people? |

## Movement 2 · complicate (15 minutes)

Four quotations, introduced one at a time. Each names a position you may already occupy.

### [[entities/jean-paul-sartre|Sartre]]

> Man is condemned to be free.

If there is no essence, no given nature, no original to return to, is the gap even a loss? Can you lose something that never existed? Sartre calls it a condemnation, not a gift. Why the harsh word, if freedom is good?

### [[entities/albert-camus|Camus]]

> The absurd is born of the confrontation between the human need and the unreasonable silence of the world.

Camus locates the absurd **in the meeting** — not in us and not in the world. Does that change anything? Is revolt a position you could actually hold, or only a mood?

### Heidegger

We are always already thrown into a world we did not choose, with tools and frameworks that were there before us.

If the framework is what makes understanding **possible** rather than what blocks it, does the word "gap" still fit? **Is a lens an obstacle to sight?**

### Camus again

> We must imagine Sisyphus happy.

Is that convincing, or is it a rhetorical trick? Is happiness inside the condition the same as accepting a loss?

## Movement 3 · commit (10 minutes)

Return to your column. Anyone who wants to move must say which quotation moved them and why.

> [!note] Changing your mind is the point
> Moving under argument is the skill being trained here. In this session, changing your mind is an achievement, not a lapse. It rehearses the second interview move (*absorb the push without abandoning the claim*) and **movement**, the fourth quality the course names.

## Closing question (5 minutes)

> Things considered real in the past are now considered false. Were we wrong then, or did reality change?
