# Oxbridge W09: Is the Human and Machine Line Essential or Contingent?

## Overview

This lesson asks whether the line between human and machine is essential or contingent. You start from a list of things you can do that a machine cannot, then test each item against what machines already do. You read Rees, weigh Bergson and Freud against him, and end by committing to one sentence: is the human and machine distinction essential or contingent. That commitment, and the question of whether human and machine memory differ in kind, feeds directly into [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w10-what-survives-forgetting|W10]].

## The ideas

### Essential or contingent

A property is **essential** if it is part of what a mind necessarily is. A property is **contingent** if it is only what technology currently happens to be. When you name something a machine cannot do, ask which kind of claim you are making. For each item, two tests help: what exactly would a machine have to do for you to withdraw the item, and has any item like it already been withdrawn in the last ten years. Common answers stretch under pressure. "Feel" needs a test for evidence of feeling in anything, including another person. "Understand rather than predict" needs a test that separates the two; if none exists, the distinction may do no work. "Have a body" points to being alive rather than to bodies, since robots have bodies. "Create something new" needs a definition of new, since much human creation is recombination.

### Rees

**[[entities/tobias-rees|Tobias Rees]]** argues that AI breaks concepts we take for granted. Three of his lines:

> AI defies many of the most fundamental, most taken-for-granted concepts that have defined the modern period.

> Deep learning systems have qualities that, up until recently, were considered possible only for living organisms in general and for humans in particular.

> AI can have agency, creativity, knowledge, language and understanding without either being alive or being human.

The pointed question: if agency and understanding can come apart from being alive, were they ever properties of life, or properties we assigned to life because we had no other examples?

### Bergson and Freud as counterweight

**[[entities/henri-bergson|Henri Bergson]]** holds that lived time is **duration**: time accumulated and indivisible, not a sequence of separate states. A system that can be reset to a previous state has no duration.

**[[entities/sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]]** holds that there is a part of you that you did not author and cannot access, which shapes what you do: the unconscious. A system whose weights can be inspected has no unconscious in that sense.

Ask whether these are differences in kind or only in current engineering. If a system accumulated distortion instead of storing records, would either objection survive? That question opens W10.

### The Snell move

The interior self, the sense of having an inner life, may not have been available to Homeric Greeks. It was constituted over centuries, partly by writing. If something that fundamental can be produced by a technology, AI may be producing something now that we cannot yet see.

## Day by day

- **This session.** Read Rees in Noema before class and answer three questions: what concept does Rees think AI breaks; is his claim about machines or about us; and where does his argument go too far. In class you list what a machine cannot do, test each item, weigh Rees against Bergson and Freud, and commit to one sentence on whether the human and machine distinction is essential or contingent. Essentialists must name the property. Contingentists must say whether any distinction could ever be essential on their view.

## Review

Check you can:

- Distinguish an **essential** property from a **contingent** one.
- Use **Rees** to say where the human and machine line has already moved.
- Explain **Bergson's** duration and why a system that can be reset has none.
- Explain **Freud's** unconscious and why inspectable weights differ from it.
- Explain the Snell move: how writing helped constitute the interior self.
- Argue whether repainted human memory and loadable machine memory differ in kind or in degree.
