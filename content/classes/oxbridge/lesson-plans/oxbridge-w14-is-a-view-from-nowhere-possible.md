# Oxbridge W14: Is a View from Nowhere Possible?

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-8-synthesis|Unit 8: Synthesis]] · Week 14 of 14 · [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w13-who-gets-to-show-us-the-world|← W13]]

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

1. Name a position you have changed your mind about over the term, and say what changed it
2. Answer a question directly, rather than carrying it out
3. Say what the throughline of the course was, in your own words

## Opening prompt (5 minutes)

> What does the world look like?

Two minutes, silent. A plain question that will produce fourteen weeks of material in one line each.

## The four questions

One question at a time, roughly ten minutes each.

### 1. Is an unframed view of anything even possible?

Every framework this term hid the edges that made it a framework rather than reality: the map, [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]], the body, machine cognition, the categories that decide who counts.

If science is also a chosen set of exclusions, what distinguishes a good frame from a bad one, if not closeness to an unframed reality nobody can access? This is the W04 question returning, and [[concepts/situated-knowledge|situated knowledge]] is a third option between neutral science and total relativism.

### 2. Does the human and machine line survive its own test?

We drew the line at memory: human memory is sedimented and repainted, machine memory is a clean record. If a system learned the way a body learns, through accumulation and distortion rather than storage, would the distinction hold — or were we only describing what AI happens to be right now? This is structurally identical to the Plato question from Week 4.

### 3. Whose agreement made this real?

Take any object in the room and trace it back to power, not just agreement. Then ask whether power and agreement are different things or the same thing described from two positions.

### 4. Is "everything is a framework" itself a framework?

The hardest move, and the reason this session exists. Apply the course's suspicion to the course itself.

- If every position is framed, is that claim framed? If it is, why should we accept it? If it is not, you have found your view from nowhere.
- Has this course given you a better view, or only a different one? What would tell you which?
- Is the suspicion useful, or does it end in a place where nothing can be said?

## Closing (5 minutes)

For the first time in fourteen weeks, you answer a question instead of carrying one out.

> Say one thing you now believe that you did not believe at the start of the course, and say what changed it.
