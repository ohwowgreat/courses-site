## Overview

This is the final session of the course. You look back over the whole term and answer four large questions directly, instead of carrying out a task. There is no reading and nothing to hand in. Come ready to name one position you have changed your mind about this term and to say what changed it. The session pulls the course's throughline into one line: is a **view from nowhere** possible, a view of reality that no particular framework shapes? The phrase echoes the philosopher **Thomas Nagel**.

## The ideas

### Is an unframed view of anything even possible?

Every framework you studied this term hid the edges that made it a framework rather than reality itself. The examples run through the whole course: the map, [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]], the body, machine cognition, and the categories that decide who counts. If even science is a chosen set of exclusions, what marks a good frame from a bad one, if not closeness to an unframed reality that no one can reach? This is the week 4 question returning. [[concepts/situated-knowledge|Situated knowledge]] is the third option if you are stuck between neutral science and total relativism: knowledge is always tied to a position, but that does not make all positions equal.

### Does the human and machine line survive its own test?

The course drew the line between human and machine at **memory**. Human memory is sedimented and repainted: it builds up and distorts over time. Machine memory is a clean record: it stores and returns. But if a system learned the way a body learns, through accumulation and distortion rather than storage, would the distinction hold, or were you only describing what AI happens to be right now? This has the same shape as the Plato question from week 4.

### Whose agreement made this real?

Take any object in the room and trace it back. Its reality rests on agreement. Push further and agreement becomes power. Then the real question: are power and agreement two different things, or the same thing described from two positions?

### Is "everything is a framework" itself a framework?

This is the hardest move and the reason the session exists. The course has trained a suspicion of frameworks. Now apply that suspicion to the course. If every position is framed, is that claim framed too? If it is, why accept it? If it is not, you have found your view from nowhere. Ask also whether the course gave you a better view or only a different one, and what would tell you which. Ask whether the suspicion is useful, or whether it ends in a place where nothing can be said.

## Day by day

- **The single session (45 minutes).** You open on the prompt "What does the world look like?" and sit with it silently for two minutes. Then you work through the four questions, about ten minutes each, with no scaffolding. You close by answering, not asking: say one thing you now believe that you did not believe at the start of the course, and say what changed it.

## Review

Check you can:

- Name a position you changed your mind about this term, and say what changed it.
- Explain what a **view from nowhere** would be and why the course doubts one is possible.
- Use **situated knowledge** as a third option between neutral science and total relativism.
- Say whether the human and machine line, drawn at **memory**, survives its own test.
- Apply the course's suspicion to the course itself and judge whether "everything is a framework" is itself a framework.
- State the throughline of the course in your own words.
