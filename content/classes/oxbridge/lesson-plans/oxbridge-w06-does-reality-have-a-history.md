## Overview

This lesson asks whether reality can have a history. You look at beliefs people once held as real and now call false, and you decide whether we were simply wrong then or whether the framework itself changed. You use maps and one medical case to test the difference. You leave with one claim of your own: a framework you use every day that claims not to be one. That claim is what you defend in W07.

## The ideas

### Superseded beliefs

People used to be certain about things we now consider false. These beliefs split into two kinds. Some were **factually wrong**: the belief got the world wrong. In others the **category or definition** itself has shifted, so the belief was never quite a fact. The line between the two is hard to draw. The people who held these beliefs were not stupid; they reasoned well from a different starting point.

**Barry Marshall**, 1984, is the test case. The claim "they just did not have the data" does not hold here: the data was available, but the framework rejected it. A framework does something to data it cannot accept.

### Maps as frameworks

**Mercator** beside **Gall-Peters** shows two map projections of the same world. Mercator is not a lie; it is useful for navigation. That is the point: a **framework** is a set of chosen sacrifices. Some of those sacrifices are visible to the user, and some are not. Every map distorts, but distortions do not all carry the same consequences.

**Taprobana** is an island that appeared on maps for centuries. Its name referred unstably to Sri Lanka, to Sumatra, and to nowhere. Sailors still sailed toward it. Ask what it would take to answer whether Taprobana was real.

In a **video game map**, the edge of the map is disguised as a feature of the world. The mountain is too high. The road is closed. The edge is disguised instead of shown as a plain wall because a visible wall reminds you that you are inside something.

### Frameworks that hide themselves

**Naturalization** is the move by which a framework hides its limits by making them look like the world. A biased history book is not the real problem. The problem is the history that claims not to be biased.

[[entities/roland-barthes|Barthes]] puts it this way: the author who disappears behind the text is the most ideological author of all. The same holds for any framework that presents itself as no framework at all.

The closing image is from Merleau-Ponty: the body is our general medium for having a world. If that is true, what happens when the medium is wrong?

## Day by day

- **This week.** One 45-minute session, no reading. You sort superseded beliefs into factually wrong versus category shifted, test frameworks against the two map projections, Taprobana, and the Barry Marshall case, and name one framework you use every day that claims not to be one. That claim is the one you defend in W07.

## Review

Check you can:

- Say whether a superseded belief means we were wrong then or that reality itself changed.
- Tell a factually wrong belief apart from one where the category or definition has shifted.
- Use a historical example, such as Barry Marshall or Taprobana, precisely rather than as illustration.
- Explain how a framework works as a set of chosen sacrifices, using Mercator and Gall-Peters.
- Explain naturalization: how a framework hides its limits by making them look like the world.
- Hold a position on whether reality is the kind of thing that can have a history.
