# Oxbridge W06: Does Reality Have a History?

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3: Frameworks and History]] · Week 6 of 14 · [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|← W05]] | [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07 →]]

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Core question | Things considered real in the past are now considered false. Does this mean we were wrong then, or that reality itself changed? |
| Duration | 45 minutes, [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template\|standard template]] |
| Reading | none |
| Bring | **Three artifacts** — Mercator beside Gall-Peters; an early modern map showing Taprobana; a one-paragraph account of Barry Marshall, 1984 |
| Carried through | The Merleau-Ponty quotation: the body is our general medium for having a world. If that is true, what happens when the medium is wrong? |

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Say whether a superseded belief means we were wrong then, or that reality itself changed
2. Use a historical example precisely rather than as illustration
3. Hold a position on whether reality is the kind of thing that can have a history

## Opening prompt (5 minutes)

> Name one thing people used to be certain about that we now consider false.

Two minutes, silent, one example each.

## Movement 1 · elicit (10 minutes)

Examples are collected and sorted into two columns: things that were *factually wrong*, and things where the *category itself* has shifted.

**Questions.**

- Were the people who believed these things stupid, or were they reasoning well from a different starting point?
- **Which of your examples will our grandchildren put on this list about us?**

| Point raised | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| "They just did not have the data" | **Barry Marshall.** The data was available; the framework rejected it. What does a framework do to data it cannot accept? |
| "Some of these are not facts, they are definitions" | Which ones, and why is the line hard to draw? |

## Movement 2 · complicate (15 minutes)

### The map

Mercator beside Gall-Peters.

**Questions.** Is Mercator a lie? What is it good at? Who benefits from what it is good at? **Is a projection that distorts nothing possible?**

| Point raised | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| "Not a lie, it is useful for navigation" | So a framework is **a set of chosen sacrifices** — which sacrifices are visible to the user, and which are not? |
| "Every map distorts, so it does not matter" | Does every distortion have the **same consequences**? |

### Taprobana

An island appeared on maps for centuries, referring unstably to Sri Lanka, to Sumatra, to nowhere. Sailors sailed toward it. **Question.** Was Taprobana real? What would it take to answer that?

### The video game map

**Question.** In a game, the edge of the map is disguised as a feature of the world. The mountain is too high. The road is closed. **Why disguise it instead of showing a wall?**

The answer: because a visible wall reminds you that you are inside something.

## Movement 3 · commit (10 minutes)

[[entities/roland-barthes|Barthes]]:

> The author who disappears behind the text is the most ideological author of all.

**The insight.** A biased history book is not the problem. The problem is the one that claims not to be. The framework hides its limits by making them look like the world.

**Question.** Name one framework you use every day that claims not to be one. Every student answers — examples include exam grades, the calendar, the news feed, "common sense", the school timetable, the map app. W07 puts these claims under pressure, so you leave with a claim of your own to defend.

## Closing question (5 minutes)

> The body is our general medium for having a world. If that is true, what happens when the medium is wrong?
>
> — Merleau-Ponty, *Phenomenology of Perception*, 1945
