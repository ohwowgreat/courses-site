# Oxbridge W11: Whose Agreement Makes a Thing Real?

## Overview

This lesson asks where the line falls between what is real no matter what anyone thinks and what is real only because people agree. You sort ten items into those two kinds, defend a hard case on one side of the line, and then name one thing you hold to be real that most people around you do not. Your job is to say who does the agreeing and what would make a thing count. This sets up whether we can still share one world (W12) and the idea that realness may rest on power (W13).

## The ideas

### Two kinds of real

Take ten items: gravity, money, a nation, Tuesday, a species, a debt, a disease, a genre, a race, a language. Some are real **regardless of agreement**. Gravity holds whether or not anyone agrees. Some are real **only because of agreement**. Tuesday is one: it exists because we all keep the same week.

A quick objection is that if a thing depends on agreement it is not really real. Test it: try to spend a currency nobody agrees on. Then ask which has more effect on your life this week, gravity or money. Money depends on agreement and still runs your week. So agreement-dependent things are real too, just in a different way. The task is to name that difference precisely, using the [[concepts/framework|Framework]].

### The hard cases

- **A species.** Is the boundary in nature, or in the way we classify?
- **A disease.** Was it there before it was named? Was addiction a disease in 1850?
- **A race.** This is the hardest item, because the sorting starts to break down.

### Who does the agreeing

Agreement raises a political question: does it need **everyone**, or **enough people**, or **the right people**? Some things are real by agreement and then stop being real; it is worth naming one and describing what its ending looked like.

If a hundred people agree and one refuses, is the one **wrong**, or simply **outside**? Whose disagreement counts as disagreement, and whose counts as error? Two easy answers do not hold. "Majority rules" fails when a persecuted minority is outnumbered but not wrong about its own status. "Experts decide" only pushes the question back: who certifies the experts, and by what agreement?

### From agreement to power

One answer is that this is not really agreement at all, it is **power**: the thing counts as real because those with power make it count. This is the entry to the idea that reality can be made, not only found, which the [[concepts/the-ontological-turn|The Ontological Turn]] names. W13 takes it up.

### The mechanism of realness

The final move is to name one thing you believe is real that most people around you do not, and to say what would make it count. This forces you to describe the **mechanism of realness**, how a thing comes to be real, rather than just asserting that some instance is real.

## Day by day

- **This week (45 minutes).** You sort the ten items into two columns, then work through the hard cases (species, disease, race). You take the political turn (everyone, enough people, or the right people; the lone refuser; whose disagreement counts), reach the idea that realness may rest on power, and close by naming one thing you hold real that others do not and what would make it count. No reading is set.

## Review

Check you can:

- Draw the line between what is real regardless of agreement and what is real only because of agreement.
- Say who does the agreeing in a case like money, borders, or weekends.
- Defend where you place a hard case, such as a species, a disease, or a race.
- Name the difference between agreement-dependent reality and agreement-independent reality precisely.
- Explain why "majority rules" and "experts decide" do not settle whose disagreement counts.
- State what **power** adds to the account of realness.
- Describe the mechanism of realness for one thing you hold real that others do not.
