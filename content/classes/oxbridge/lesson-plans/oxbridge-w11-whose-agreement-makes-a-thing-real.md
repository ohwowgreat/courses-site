# Oxbridge W11: Whose Agreement Makes a Thing Real?

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-6-collective-reality|Unit 6: Collective Reality]] · Week 11 of 14 · [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w10-what-survives-forgetting|← W10]] | [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w12-is-a-common-world-still-possible|W12 →]]

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Core question | Some things are real whether or not anyone agrees. Some things are real only because everyone agrees. Where is the line, and who draws it? |
| Duration | 45 minutes, [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-session-structure-template\|standard template]] |
| Reading | none |
| Bring | The ten-item list, on the board |
| Carried into next week | Can we still share one world, or only private ones? |

## What you will be able to do

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Draw the line between what is real regardless of agreement and what is real only because of it
2. Say who does the agreeing, on a case like money, borders or weekends
3. Defend the placement of a hard case on either side of that line

## Opening (5 minutes)

Ten items are on the board. You sort them into two columns, silently, for two minutes.

> gravity · money · a nation · Tuesday · a species · a debt · a disease · a genre · a race · a language

## Movement 1 (10 minutes)

Start with the easy ones, then work through the hard cases:

- **A species:** is the boundary in nature or in the classification?
- **A disease:** was it there before it was named? Was addiction a disease in 1850?
- **A race:** what happens to your sorting here, and why is it the hardest one?

## Movement 2 (15 minutes)

The political turn:

- Does agreement require **everyone**, or **enough people**, or **the right people**?
- Name something that was real by agreement and stopped being real. What did the ending look like?
- If a hundred people agree and one refuses, **is the one wrong, or outside?**
- Whose disagreement counts as disagreement, and whose counts as error?

## Movement 3 (10 minutes)

Name one thing you believe is real that most people around you do not, and say what would make it count. This forces you to describe the **mechanism** of realness rather than just assert an instance of it.

## Closing (5 minutes)

> Can we still share one world, or only private ones?
