## Overview

This lesson asks whether feeling is more reliable than any system, or whether the body is just another framework. You start from a simple case: you feel cold, a thermometer reads a number, and you ask which one can be wrong. You then test the claim against four harder cases and against one quotation from Merleau-Ponty. You do not hand in written work. What you produce is a stated position of your own, and one question you carry into Week 9: what can you do that a machine cannot?

## The ideas

### Is feeling more reliable than a system?

Start with the cold case. A thermometer says 22 degrees and you are shivering. Which one is wrong? One strong answer is that neither is wrong: the thermometer measures the room and your body measures you. That answer forces a further question: what is the body an instrument of? You cannot escape into the thermometer either, because you only reach its reading through a body that reads it.

A classical position holds that you cannot be wrong about a feeling, only about its cause. You can feel cold and be mistaken about why, but the feeling itself is not an error.

### The body as window or medium

**Merleau-Ponty** says the body is our general medium for having a world. On this view the body is not a clear window onto things. It is the medium that shapes what can appear at all. This changes the meaning of a wrong reading. If the medium shifts, you may not be wrong about the world so much as have a different world. A blind person, on this view, is not perceiving less of the world but a different world.

### Four cases that complicate trust

Each case closes off one easy answer.

- **Phantom limb.** A person feels pain in a leg that is not there. The pain is felt, so is it real, and is it accurate?
- **Chronic pain without injury.** The pain signal fires with nothing to report. The question is whether the body is lying or reporting honestly about its own state.
- **Synaesthesia.** Someone hears color. This asks whether they perceive more of the world or simply differently.
- **Adaptation.** Move from a cold room to a warm one and the same water feels hot, then neutral. So which reading was correct?

Two distinctions do the work here. First, calling one body **normal** and another a malfunction assumes an answer: ask what makes a configuration normal rather than simply common. Second, "trusted" only means something once you say trusted **to do what**. A hammer that is bad at cutting is not untrustworthy, because cutting is not its job.

### What can a body do?

**Spinoza** offers a reframe: we do not yet know what a body can do. Instead of asking what a body **is**, ask what a body **can do**. The open question is whether this dissolves the problem or dodges it. At the end you state one of two positions: the body is an instrument to be corrected, or the body is the condition of there being anything to correct.

## Day by day

- **This session.** Read the Merleau-Ponty quotation sent in advance. In class you describe being cold without the word "cold" and without a number, work through the cold case, the four complicating cases, and the Spinoza reframe, then state your position. Carry one question out of the room: what can you do that a machine cannot?

## Review

Check you can:

- Argue whether feeling is more reliable than any system, or whether the body is just another framework.
- Explain what **Merleau-Ponty** means by the body as the general medium for having a world.
- Use the phantom limb, chronic pain without injury, synaesthesia, and adaptation cases to test whether the body can be trusted.
- Distinguish a **normal** configuration from a merely common one.
- Explain the **Spinoza** reframe: asking what a body can do rather than what it is.
- Answer what you can do that a machine cannot.
