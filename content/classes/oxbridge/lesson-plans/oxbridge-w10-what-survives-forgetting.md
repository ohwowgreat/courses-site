## Overview

This lesson asks whether forgetting is a failure of memory or a function of it. You bring one memory from before you were ten and use it as evidence about how memory actually works. You produce a one-sentence answer to the core question by the end of the session. The closing question about money, borders, and weekends carries forward into W11.

## The ideas

### Is the memory the event?

When you recall something, you may not be describing the event at all. You may be describing **a photograph, a story you were told, or a previous time you remembered it**. Clarity is not proof: your clearest dreams are not your truest ones, so clarity does not track accuracy. A memory retold many times can blur until you cannot tell which part is yours.

### The painted sheet

**The painted sheet** is a model of memory as repainting, not retrieval. Remembering is not pulling a file from storage. It is painting over a surface, and the new paint covers the old. After twenty years of remembering, what you hold is the last painting, made from the previous painting, not the event. On this model, a memory you revisit often may be less reliable, because each recall repaints it. This raises a further distinction: remembering something is not the same as **remembering remembering it**.

### Bergson

[[entities/henri-bergson|Bergson]] splits memory in two. **Habit memory** is performed by the body: you do not recall how to ride a bicycle, you do it. **Pure memory** survives whole, and attention filters it rather than losing it. On this account we forget nothing. We simply cannot attend to most of it. If nothing is lost and only access fails, you can ask whether that makes forgetting less serious or more.

### Freud

[[entities/sigmund-freud|Freud]] treats forgetting as work, not accident. Things are pushed down, and they return in distorted forms: slips, dreams, repetitions. If forgetting is something you do rather than something that happens to you, you can ask whether you are responsible for what you forget.

### The machine version

Linking back to W09: if a machine forgot the way a body forgets, by accumulating distortion rather than deleting files, would the human and machine line still hold?

## Day by day

- The lesson runs in one 45-minute session. You bring and describe a memory from before you were ten, then note how you know it happened. The class works through the painted sheet, Bergson, and Freud, and you commit to a one-sentence answer to the core question. The closing question is: money is real, borders are real, weekends are real, who agreed?

## Review

Check you can:

- Argue whether forgetting is a failure of memory or a function of it.
- Use your own memory as evidence without turning it into anecdote.
- Explain the painted sheet model and why a revisited memory may be less reliable.
- Distinguish remembering something from remembering remembering it.
- Distinguish Bergson's habit memory from pure memory, and explain the claim that we forget nothing.
- Explain Freud's view that forgetting is work that returns as slips, dreams, and repetitions.
- Say what survives when the record does not.
