# Oxbridge Assessment — none

This course is not graded. There is no register of marks.

## What replaces marking

Nothing is measured, but there is still a standard for what a good contribution looks like, and it is named aloud in the room. A strong contribution shows:

1. **Commitment.** You take a position rather than survey positions.
2. **Pressure.** You name the strongest objection to your own case.
3. **Precision.** Your claim is narrow enough to be wrong.
4. **Movement.** The argument arrives somewhere it did not start.

You get these as spoken feedback in the commit movement — "that is a survey, not a position" is something you can act on immediately. They map onto the three interview moves: commitment is *take a position quickly*, pressure is *absorb the push*, and movement is what the mock debrief measures. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-interview-preparation|Interview Preparation]].

## What the course tests

The course tests your speaking: fourteen commit movements, in which neutrality is the one banned position.

## The hedging repair

[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07]] is a pure pressure workshop — paired push practice and a four-line spine built in the room — and the same work happens in every session's commit movement.

## Discussion questions

The following prompts run as discussion questions, all in [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-question-bank|the question bank]]:

- Is a low quality image worth less than a high quality one?
- Is the gap a loss, a liberation, or simply the condition of having a mind?
- Take one framework you rely on daily and show where it hides its own edges.
- Is forgetting a failure of memory or a function of it?
- Who decides what is visible, and does knowing the answer change anything?
- Is a view from nowhere possible?
