# Oxbridge Assessment — none

## Why the page exists at all

This course is not graded. There is no register and no marks. If you came here from one of the other four courses looking for one, this is the answer.

## What replaces marking

Nothing is measured. But the sessions still hold a standard for what a good contribution looks like, and it is named aloud in the room:

1. **Commitment.** Do you take a position rather than survey positions?
2. **Pressure.** Do you name the strongest objection to your own case?
3. **Precision.** Is the claim narrow enough to be wrong?
4. **Movement.** Does the argument arrive somewhere it did not start?

Ungraded, these four do their best work as **spoken feedback in the commit movement**. "That is a survey, not a position" is a sentence you can act on right away, which a grade is not. They map onto the three interview moves: commitment is take a position quickly, pressure is absorb the push, and movement is what the mock debrief measures. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-interview-preparation|Interview Preparation]].

## The claim that survives without the essays

The course tests its central claim in speaking, not writing: fourteen commit movements, in which neutrality is the one banned position.

## Where the hedging repair went

The writing existed to fix one failure mode: **hedging**. That repair now lives in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07]], a pure pressure workshop with paired push practice and a four-line spine built in the room, no essay attached. It also runs in every session's commit movement.

The old writing prompts are now discussion questions in [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-question-bank|the question bank]].
