## Unit question

**Do images represent the world, or do they produce it?**

## Why this opens the course

You already live inside images. You have opinions about resolution, screenshots, compression, filters, and what counts as a real photograph. This unit starts there. It needs no prior reading to enter, and it stays rigorous without simplification.

[[entities/john-berger|John Berger]] gives the first idea: seeing is not neutral. [[entities/hito-steyerl|Hito Steyerl]] turns that idea into a political and economic argument.

The unit also installs the **method** you will use for the rest of the course: begin from an intuition you already hold, discover it does not survive a single hard case, then let a thinker arrive to name what you have been arguing. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|Course Design Principles]].

## Sessions

- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|W01: Is perception ever neutral?]] — one unfamiliar image, seen twice: before and after you are told what it is. Berger arrives at the end.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w02-do-images-represent-reality-or-produce-it|W02: Do images represent reality or produce it?]] — the same image at two resolutions. Steyerl turns the vocabulary of compression into an argument about access.

## Key texts

- [[entities/john-berger|John Berger]], *Ways of Seeing*, chapter 1 (first six pages; the BBC episode is the alternative entry point)
- [[entities/hito-steyerl|Hito Steyerl]], "In Defense of the Poor Image", e-flux journal

See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-reading-list|the reading list]].

## Preparation

Both sessions set a reading. This is the only consecutive pair in the course that does. Nothing is written or collected; the course is [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|discussion only]]. Come to W02 having read Steyerl, and having picked the single sentence you found most arguable, ready to say which and why.

## What you should leave with

> A defensible position on whether image quality is a technical property or a political one, and the start of a suspicion that how something is shown does more work than what is shown.

## Across the two weeks

- **The science vocabulary turns.** Steyerl uses vocabulary you may know well, resolution and compression, and then turns it into an argument about access. The same pattern returns in [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2]].
- **The real claim is strong.** This is not just that people interpret images differently. It is that seeing is already interpretive before any interpreting begins.

## Bridge to Unit 2

> If the image is not a neutral window, the next question is whether anything is. That moves the argument from pictures to measurement, where you expect to find safety.

W02's closing question does the handoff: a thermometer measures temperature; does it show you the temperature, or change it? You are meant to hear a change of subject. [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2]] removes the safety.
