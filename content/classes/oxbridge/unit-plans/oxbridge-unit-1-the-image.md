# Oxbridge Unit 1: The Image

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-seminar-plan|Seminar Plan]] · Unit 1 of 8 · Weeks 1–2 · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2 →]]

## Unit question

**Do images represent the world, or do they produce it?**

## What this unit is about

You already live inside images. You have opinions about resolution, screenshots, compression, filters, and what counts as a real photograph. This unit starts there: it needs no prior reading to enter and stays rigorous once you are in.

[[entities/john-berger|John Berger]] gives the first idea: seeing is not neutral. [[entities/hito-steyerl|Hito Steyerl]] turns that into a political and economic argument.

This unit also introduces the method you will use for the rest of the course: start from an intuition you already hold, test it against a single hard case, and then let a thinker name what you have been arguing. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|Course Design Principles]].

## Sessions

- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w01-is-perception-ever-neutral|W01: Is perception ever neutral?]] — one unfamiliar image, seen twice: before and after being told what it is. Berger comes in only at the end.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w02-do-images-represent-reality-or-produce-it|W02: Do images represent reality or produce it?]] — the same image at two resolutions. Steyerl turns the vocabulary of compression into an argument about access.

## Key texts

- [[entities/john-berger|John Berger]], *Ways of Seeing*, chapter 1 (first six pages; the BBC episode is an alternative entry point)
- [[entities/hito-steyerl|Hito Steyerl]], "In Defense of the Poor Image", e-flux journal

See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-reading-list|the reading list]].

## Preparation

Both sessions set a reading — the only consecutive pair in the course that does. Nothing is written or collected; the course is [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|discussion only]]. Come to W02 having read Steyerl, and pick the single sentence you found most arguable — be ready to say which and why.

## What you should leave with

> [!note] By the end of this unit
> A defensible position on whether image quality is a technical property or a political one, and the beginning of a suspicion that the way something is shown is doing more work than what is shown.

## Bridge to Unit 2

> [!note] Looking ahead
> If the image is not a neutral window, the next question is whether anything is. That moves the argument from pictures to measurement, where you might expect to find safety.

W02 closes on one line — *a thermometer measures temperature; does it show you the temperature, or change it?* [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2]] removes the safety.
