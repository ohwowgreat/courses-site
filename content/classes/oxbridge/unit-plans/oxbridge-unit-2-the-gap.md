# Oxbridge Unit 2: The Gap

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-seminar-plan|Seminar Plan]] · Unit 2 of 8 · Weeks 3–5 · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-1-the-image|← Unit 1]] | [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3 →]]

## Unit question

**Can any representation reach the thing itself?**

## The arc

Three sessions turn the same problem a quarter turn each. This is the longest unit and the one that introduces the course's central term.

**Week 3, epistemology.** This week is about how well any observation reaches its object. A thermometer changes the temperature of what it measures. In the double slit experiment, observation changes what is observed. Language calls something warm, and warm means different things to different speakers. The gap looks wider in language than in science, but it is present in both. This is the starting point, not the end.

**Week 4, ontology.** The question shifts from how well we can know things to what kind of things there are to know. [[entities/plato]] arrives here, with [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]], a technique for depicting the world from one point that came to look like the world itself.

**Week 5, the existential turn.** By now the gap cannot be closed. The question becomes how to live with it. [[entities/jean-paul-sartre|Jean-Paul Sartre]], [[entities/albert-camus|Albert Camus]] and Heidegger appear as three positions you can adopt and defend, not as content to memorize.

## Sessions

- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w03-can-measurement-reach-the-thing-itself|W03: Can measurement reach the thing itself?]]: the thermometer, the double slit, then the same problem in language. No reading.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w04-is-there-a-reality-behind-appearances|W04: Is there a reality behind appearances?]]: the cave and the perspective construction. The ontological turn.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|W05: Is the gap a loss, a liberation, or a condition?]]: four quotations, three columns on the board, and permission to change columns.

## Preparation

Only W04 sets a reading: Plato, about two pages. W03 and W05 run on cases and quotations given live. The reading load is lightest exactly where the ideas are hardest.

What you carry between sessions is a position. W03 closes on the tree in the forest, and you are asked not to discuss it before W04. W04 asks you to **bring a case** to W05. Nothing is written or collected. The course is [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|discussion only]].

## What to watch in this unit

If you come from a science background, you may treat science as the exception, the frame that reaches truth while others do not. Build that case as strongly as you can in week 3. Then, in week 4, ask what makes a good frame better than a bad one if "closer to an unframed reality" is not available as a test.

Two points to hold, one per session:

- **W03:** the claim is not that measurement is unreliable. It is that measurement is an *interaction*, and an interaction has two sides. "Precision and disturbance are not opposites; the more precisely you interact, the more you interact." Do not let this collapse into "everything is subjective."
- **W05:** the existentialists are positions, not biography. You do not need Sartre's life story. You need to say "I hold the Sartre position and here is why the Camus objection does not defeat it." That is the interview skill in miniature.

## Links between sessions

- **W02 → W03.** The thermometer question closes the image unit and opens this one.
- **W03 → W04.** W03 closes on the tree falling in the forest, and you are asked not to discuss it. W04 opens by polling it. If you answered "the gap is about reality" in W03, you have already stepped into ontology, and W04 uses that split.

## Bridge to Unit 3

> If frameworks cannot be escaped, the next question is whether they change. That is a historical question.

W04's closing question sets this up, and W05's closing question asks it directly: things once considered real are now considered false. Were we wrong then, or did reality change? This leads into [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3]].
