# Oxbridge Unit 2: The Gap

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-seminar-plan|Seminar Plan]] · Unit 2 of 8 · Weeks 3–5 · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-1-the-image|← Unit 1]] | [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3 →]]

## Unit question

**Can any representation reach the thing itself?**

## The arc

Three sessions, each turning the same problem. This is the longest unit and the one that introduces the course's central term.

**Week 3, epistemology.** A thermometer changes the temperature of what it measures. Observation in the double slit experiment changes what is observed. Language calls something warm, and warm means different things to different speakers. You will likely conclude that the gap is wider in language than in science but present in both — that is the starting point, not the end.

**Week 4, ontology.** The question shifts from how well we can know things to what kind of things there are to know. [[entities/plato]] arrives here, alongside [[concepts/linear-perspective|linear perspective]] — a technique for depicting the world from a point that came to look like the world itself.

**Week 5, the existential turn.** Having established that the gap cannot be closed, you are asked how to live with it. [[entities/jean-paul-sartre|Jean-Paul Sartre]], [[entities/albert-camus|Albert Camus]] and Heidegger are used as three positions you can adopt and defend.

## Sessions

- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w03-can-measurement-reach-the-thing-itself|W03: Can measurement reach the thing itself?]] — the thermometer, the double slit, then the same problem moved into language. No reading.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w04-is-there-a-reality-behind-appearances|W04: Is there a reality behind appearances?]] — the cave and the perspective construction. The ontological turn.
- [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w05-is-the-gap-a-loss-a-liberation-or-a-condition|W05: Is the gap a loss, a liberation, or a condition?]] — four quotations, three columns on the board, and permission to change columns.

## Preparation

Only W04 sets a reading (Plato, ≈ two pages). W03 and W05 run entirely on cases and quotations delivered live, so the reading load is lightest where the ideas are hardest.

What you carry between sessions is a position. W03 closes on the tree in the forest, with instructions not to discuss it before W04. W04 asks you to **bring a case** to W05. Nothing is written or collected — the course is [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|discussion only]].

## What to expect

If you have a science background, you may treat science as the exception — the frame that gets closer to truth. Build that case as strongly as you can in Week 3. In Week 4 you will be asked what distinguishes a good frame from a bad one if closeness to an unframed reality is not available as a criterion.

- **W03:** the claim is not that measurement is unreliable — it is that measurement is an *interaction*, and an interaction has two sides. Precision and disturbance are not opposites; the more precisely you interact, the more you interact.
- **W05:** the existentialists are positions, not biography. The aim is to be able to say "I hold the Sartre position and here is why the Camus objection does not defeat it."

## Bridge to Unit 3

If frameworks cannot be escaped, the next question is whether they change — a historical question. W05 closes on it directly: *things considered real in the past are now considered false — were we wrong then, or did reality change?* This leads into [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3]].
