# Oxbridge Seminar Plan

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · fourteen weekly sessions · **Spring 2027, dates unassigned**

This page shows the whole course: what happens each week, what it feeds, and where the order matters.

## Status

The course runs for fourteen weekly sessions in Spring 2027. It has no fixed dates yet. Teaching would resume after the winter vacation, around **Mon 2027-02-22** ([[calendar|school academic calendar]]). Fourteen unbroken weeks reach about the week of **2027-05-24**. Qingming (around 2027-04-05) and May Day (around 05-01 to 05-05) push the close to about **2027-06-07**. These dates are provisional. They may shift when the school publishes the spring calendar. Sessions are numbered by week.

## The arc

The units are not a list of topics. Each unit closes by producing the question the next one opens with. The sessions hand off the same way. W02 ends on the thermometer, which is W03's opening prompt. W03 ends on the tree in the forest, which W04 polls in its first five minutes. **The handoffs are the course.** The order carries more than it looks like it would.

## Units

- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-1-the-image|Unit 1: The Image]]: W1–2. Berger and Steyerl. You already live inside images, so the course can start rigorous without starting hard.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-2-the-gap|Unit 2: The Gap]]: W3–5. Epistemology, then ontology, then the existential turn. The same problem turned a quarter turn each week.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|Unit 3: Frameworks and History]]: W6–7. Does reality have a history, then the midpoint pressure workshop.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-4-the-body-and-the-machine|Unit 4: The Body and the Machine]]: W8–9. Two sessions asking one structural question: is a boundary essential or contingent?
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-5-memory-and-forgetting|Unit 5: Memory and Forgetting]]: W10. The single-session unit, and the most personal one.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-6-collective-reality|Unit 6: Collective Reality]]: W11–12. Agreement, then the fragmentation of the shared world.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-7-power-and-visibility|Unit 7: Power and Visibility]]: W13. The unit that collects everything. The framework turns out to have an owner.
- [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-8-synthesis|Unit 8: Synthesis]]: W14. Four questions, no reading, no new material.

## Nothing is set, collected, or graded

**The course is discussion only.** There is no written work, no assessment, and no attainment codes. See [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|Assessment — none]].

Each week asks the same of you: **read the text if there is one, and arrive with a position.** Seven of the fourteen sessions set a reading. The rest run on cases you already have. The only thing that travels between sessions is the closing question, posted to the group chat with a quote attached.

Six of these questions began as essay prompts. All six now run as **discussion questions** in [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-question-bank|the question bank]]. Four criteria describe a strong contribution: **commitment**, **pressure**, **precision**, and **movement**. Name them aloud when you commit and move.

The course produces no artifact. Nothing outlives the room except the group chat.

## Where the sequence is load-bearing

Four points where the order carries weight:

1. **W01 → W02.** W02 calls back your W01 commitments by name. If you said perception can be trained toward neutrality, you "must now say what a neutral image would look like and who would be able to afford it."
2. **W02 → W03 → W04.** Two handoffs are disguised as topic changes: the thermometer, then the tree in the forest. You are meant to meet W03 as a new subject and find that it is not.
3. **W08 → W09.** W08 ends on one line: what a body can do and what a machine can do are the same kind of question. Without it, W09 reads as a change of subject.
4. **W04 → W09 → W14.** The same structural question comes three times: is the difference essential, or is it where we currently stand? W14's second question depends on your having met it twice already.
