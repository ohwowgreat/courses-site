# Oxbridge Unit 4: The Body and the Machine

[[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-seminar-plan|Seminar Plan]] · Unit 4 of 8 · Weeks 8–9 · [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-3-frameworks-and-history|← Unit 3]] | [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-5-memory-and-forgetting|Unit 5 →]]

## Unit question

**Where does the thinking thing end?**

## Why these two sessions belong together

Both sessions ask the same structural question: **is a boundary essential or contingent?** Week 8 asks whether the body is a reliable instrument or a framework like any other. Week 9 asks whether the line between human and machine is a real difference in kind or just a description of where technology currently stands.

One line holds the two weeks together:

> What a body can do and what a machine can do turn out to be the same kind of question. Both are about mapping capacities, not testing essences.

## Week 8: the body

[[entities/maurice-merleau-ponty|Maurice Merleau-Ponty]] gives the anchor: the body is our general medium for having a world. If that is true, what happens when the medium is wrong?

You work through four cases, one at a time: **phantom limb, chronic pain without injury, synaesthesia, thermal adaptation.** Each one breaks a different escape route. The session closes on Spinoza's reframe: stop asking what a body **is** and ask what a body **can do**.

## Week 9: the machine

[[entities/tobias-rees|Tobias Rees]] argues that AI defies concepts that have defined the modern period; that deep learning systems have qualities once thought possible only for living things; and that a system can have agency, creativity, knowledge, language and understanding without being alive or human.

[[entities/henri-bergson|Henri Bergson]] and [[entities/sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]] supply the counterweight: **duration** and **the unconscious** as things a system without a history cannot have. [[entities/donna-haraway|Donna Haraway]] and [[entities/benjamin-bratton|Benjamin Bratton]] extend the argument outward.

## Preparation

W08 sends you one quotation in advance. It is the only session in the course with no reading that still arrives with something prepared. W09 sends the Rees essay with three guiding questions. The third question, "where does his argument go too far?", invites you to disagree before you open the text.

Nothing is written or collected. The course is [[classes/oxbridge/assessments/oxbridge-writing-and-assessment|discussion only]].

## Teaching notes

- **W09 repeats the shape of W04.** Both ask whether a difference is essential or just where we currently stand. The same pairing returns a third time in [[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]]'s second question.
- **W08's opening prompt is the whole session in miniature:** describe being cold without using the word cold and without using a number. It is harder than it looks.
- In W09, both sides face a hard demand. The essentialists must name the property. The contingentists must say whether *any* distinction could ever be essential on their view, or whether they have made the word meaningless.
- **Carsten Höller's laboratory of doubt** is an optional visual anchor for W08.

## Bridge to Unit 5

> The strongest surviving distinction students usually offer is that human memory is sedimented and repainted while machine memory is a clean record. That claim deserves its own session.

W09 closes on exactly that question, which [[classes/oxbridge/unit-plans/oxbridge-unit-5-memory-and-forgetting|Unit 5]] takes up.
