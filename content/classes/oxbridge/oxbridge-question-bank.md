# Oxbridge Question Bank

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · course apparatus

Single-line questions covering the full course arc — "plain on the surface, catalytic underneath." Use them as openers, as closing questions, or as interview prompts. The single-line form is deliberate: the printed question stays plain, and the complication comes in the spoken follow-up.

## Image and perception

- Is perception ever neutral?
- What is a photograph evidence of?
- Is a low quality image worth less than a high quality one?
- Do images show us the world or make the world showable?
- Is a screenshot of a painting a painting?

## Measurement and the gap

- Can measurement reach the thing itself?
- Is the gap a problem with our instruments or a condition of observing?
- Which is wider, the gap between "cold" and the temperature, or between the reading and the temperature?
- What would it feel like to have no gap?

## Appearance and reality

- Is there a reality behind appearances?
- Where does your idea of a perfect circle come from?
- Does knowing you are in a cave change your relationship to it?
- Is the prisoner who leaves better off than the ones who stay?
- Is perspective a discovery or an invention?

## The gap as a way of living

- Is the gap a loss, a liberation, or just the condition?
- Can you lose something that never existed?
- Is revolt a position or a mood?
- Must we imagine Sisyphus happy?

## History and frameworks

- Does reality have a history?
- Were we wrong then, or did reality change?
- Is a map that distorts nothing possible?
- Was Taprobana real?
- Name a framework you use every day that claims not to be one.
- Why does a game disguise its edge as a mountain?

## The body

- Can the body be trusted?
- Can you be wrong about being cold?
- If the thermometer says 22 and you are shivering, which is wrong?
- Is a blind person perceiving less of the world, or a different world?
- What can a body do?

## Human and machine

- What can you do that a machine cannot?
- Is the line between people and machines essential or contingent?
- Can there be understanding without life?
- What would a machine have to do for you to withdraw your answer?
- Could a machine have a past?

## Memory and forgetting

- What survives forgetting?
- Is forgetting a failure of memory or a function of it?
- Is a memory you revisit often more reliable or less?
- Are you responsible for what you forget?
- What is the difference between remembering and remembering remembering?

## Collective reality

- Whose agreement makes a thing real?
- Does agreement require everyone, enough people, or the right people?
- If a hundred agree and one refuses, is the one wrong or outside?
- Is a common world still possible?
- What is the last thing everyone in this room definitely saw?

## Power and visibility

- Who gets to show us the world, and who has to be seen?
- Is being seen recognition or capture?
- Who can afford to be invisible?
- Is awareness of a framework enough, or does it change nothing?

## Synthesis

- Is a view from nowhere possible?
- What does the world look like?
- How does the world appear to us?
- Is "everything is a framework" itself a framework?
- What distinguishes a good frame from a bad one, if closeness to reality is unavailable?

## Interview format, with push lines

Practice in pairs. The push replicates the interviewer's follow-up move. See [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-interview-preparation|Interview Preparation]] for the three moves being drilled.

**Is a photograph evidence?**
> Push: you said it depends on editing. Every photograph involves framing, exposure, and a chosen moment. So is every photograph edited, and does your distinction survive?

**Can a machine understand?**
> Push: you said understanding requires experience. Describe a test that would tell you whether another person has experience. If you cannot, is your criterion doing any work?

**Is a common world still possible?**
> Push: you said shared experience rather than shared images. Does that scale beyond a room, and if not, have you described a shared world or a small one?

**Were we wrong then, or did reality change?**
> Push: you said we were simply wrong. What counts as a disease has changed. Was addiction not a disease in 1850, or did we fail to notice that it was?

**Is being seen recognition or capture?**
> Push: you said the answer is to avoid visibility. Who has that option, and who is punished for taking it?
