# Oxbridge Session Structure Template

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · course apparatus · **45 minutes**

Every session in the course follows this shape. All fourteen session plans use it, so once you know this page you can follow any of them.

## The five parts

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Silent opening | 5 | One question on the board. Students write for two minutes. **No answers taken yet** |
| Movement 1 · elicit | 10 | Positions on the table. Concrete, experiential, no theory. The goal is disagreement, not accuracy |
| Movement 2 · complicate | 15 | The case, image or example that breaks the easy answer. The philosopher may arrive here |
| Movement 3 · commit | 10 | One sentence and one reason from every student. Others may challenge |
| Closing | 5 | One question on the board, unanswered, carried into next week |

### Silent opening

You write for two minutes before anything is said. The reason is explicit: it "guarantees every student enters the session with something of their own, which matters enormously for quieter and less confident speakers." In an ESL room this is not a warm-up. It is the mechanism that makes the rest of the session possible.

### Movement 1 · elicit

Get positions on the table. The disagreement is named out loud when it appears: "So we have two camps in the room." The goal is disagreement, not accuracy, so a wrong answer stated clearly is more useful here than a hedged correct one.

### Movement 2 · complicate

The case, image or example that breaks the easy answer. The pressure comes **from inside student positions rather than against them** (see [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|principle 5]]). This is where a thinker may arrive, "and only to give a name to the position a student has already taken."

### Movement 3 · commit

Each student states a position in one sentence and gives one reason. Others may challenge.

> [!important] One rule
> No student is allowed to remain neutral. Neutrality is the one banned position in this room.

### Closing

One question is written on the board, unanswered, and carried into the next week. It is posted to the group chat afterward with a quote attached.

## After the session

A short recap message goes to the group chat. The register is casual. It gives two or three sentences naming what emerged, one quote for the next session, and the closing question restated.

This is the only routine outside the room, and it does real work. It is how the closing question stays live across seven days, and it is where the next session's text or quotation is seeded.

## Where sessions deviate

Three of the fourteen bend the template, each for a stated reason:

- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07]].** The movements become *what an argument is*, *live pressure in pairs*, and *build the spine*. Consolidation, no new material.
- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w12-is-a-common-world-still-possible|W12]].** Movement 2 is student-led. Each student presents a chosen angle and answers one question from the room before the next speaks.
- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]].** The template is dropped entirely for four questions at roughly ten minutes each, and there is no closing question. "For the first time in fourteen weeks, students answer one instead."
