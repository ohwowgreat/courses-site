# Oxbridge Session Structure Template

> [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge|Oxbridge]] · course apparatus · **45 minutes**

Every session in the course follows the same shape. Once you know this page, you can follow any session quickly.

## The five parts

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Silent opening | 5 | One question on the board. You write for two minutes. **No answers taken yet** |
| Movement 1 · elicit | 10 | Positions on the table. Concrete, experiential, no theory. The goal is disagreement, not accuracy |
| Movement 2 · complicate | 15 | The case, image or example that breaks the easy answer. The philosopher may arrive here |
| Movement 3 · commit | 10 | One sentence and one reason from every student. Others may challenge |
| Closing | 5 | One question on the board, unanswered, carried into next week |

### Silent opening

Two minutes of writing before anything is said. This guarantees you enter the session with something of your own, which matters especially for quieter and less confident speakers. In an ESL room this is not a warm-up; it is what makes the rest of the session possible.

### Movement 1 · elicit

Positions go on the table. When a disagreement appears, it is named out loud — "So we have two camps in the room." The goal is disagreement, not accuracy, so a wrong answer stated clearly is more useful here than a hedged correct one.

### Movement 2 · complicate

The case, image or example that breaks the easy answer. Pressure comes **from inside your positions rather than against them** (see [[classes/oxbridge/oxbridge-course-design-principles|principle 5]]). This is where a thinker may arrive, "and only to give a name to the position a student has already taken."

### Movement 3 · commit

Each student states a position in one sentence and gives one reason. Others may challenge.

> [!important] No neutral positions
> No student is allowed to remain neutral. Neutrality is the one banned position in this room.

### Closing

One question written on the board, unanswered, carried into the next week. It is posted to the group chat afterward with a quote attached.

## After the session

A short recap message goes to the group chat: two or three sentences naming what emerged, one quote for the next session, and the closing question restated. The register is casual.

This is the only routine outside the room. It keeps the closing question live across seven days, and it is where the next session's text or quotation is seeded.

## Where sessions deviate

Three of the fourteen sessions bend the template:

- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w07-argument-workshop|W07]]** — the movements become *what an argument is* / *live pressure in pairs* / *build the spine*. Consolidation, no new material.
- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w12-is-a-common-world-still-possible|W12]]** — Movement 2 is student-led; each student presents a chosen angle and answers one question from the room before the next speaks.
- **[[classes/oxbridge/lesson-plans/oxbridge-w14-is-a-view-from-nowhere-possible|W14]]** — the template is dropped entirely for four questions at roughly ten minutes each, and there is no closing question. For the first time in fourteen weeks, you answer one instead.
