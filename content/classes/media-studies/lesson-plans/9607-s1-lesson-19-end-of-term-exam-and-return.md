## Overview

This lesson closes Semester 1. You sit the full Component 2 paper in an exam window between Mon 2027-01-11 and Mon 01-18. Your script comes back on Tue 01-19 with question-level AO breakdowns. On Wed 01-20 you build a one-page Semester 2 target sheet ([[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|LB7]]): where the marks went, which Assessment Objective is your gap, and what Semester 2 does about it. The exam counts in the 20% End-of-Term category, and it feeds your Forecast Grade evidence. These dates are provisional and may shift when the school publishes the calendar; the exam office sets your slot.

## The ideas

### The exam paper

The **End of Term Exam** is the full Component 2 paper, two hours, sat in the exam venue under full conditions. Section A is on an unseen extract: 30 minutes viewing and notes, then 45 minutes writing. Section B is one question from the contexts menu: 45 minutes.

### Reading your marks

Your marked script shows a **question-level AO breakdown**: how many marks each question earned, split by Assessment Objective. Self-band first. With the script face-down, band your own work per criterion from memory of what you wrote, then compare with the mark. The difference between your self-band and the real mark is your **gap**. Name your gap in AO language, using the script as evidence: not "Section B went badly," but "AO1 contexts: my case-study facts were pre-2020."

### The target sheet

Your **Semester 2 target sheet** is one page with four rows:

- **gap**, in AO language
- **evidence**, from your script and semester data ([[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a1-timed-media-language-analysis|A1]] → [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a4-section-a-authentic-format|A4]] → [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-eot-component-2-paper|EoT]])
- **action**, specific and shaped for Semester 2
- **review date**, a named Semester 2 week that falls before the first Semester 2 attainment

Write it to be read in February by the person who wrote it in January.

**LB7** is your response to feedback and your Semester 2 goal.

### What Semester 2 holds

C1 goes to Cambridge in the spring. The film-industry case study opens Section B properly. Paper 2 sits in June. The theory-card deck and the case-study document carry across the break as the course's two living artifacts. (Aside: the coursebook numbers these units U6–U8, which does not match our calendar bands.)

## Day by day

- **Exam window Mon 01-11 → Mon 01-18.** You sit the full Component 2 paper, two hours, in the exam venue under full conditions. The exam office sets your slot.
- **Tue 01-19.** Your script comes back. Self-band each criterion from memory, then read the question-level AO breakdown and name your gap in writing, in AO language, with the script as evidence.
- **Wed 01-20 (double).** Build your one-page Semester 2 target sheet: gap, evidence, action, review date. Post LB7: your response to feedback and your Semester 2 goal. Then preview Semester 2. Grades and comments are due today.

## Review

Check you can:

- Sit the full two-hour Component 2 paper, applying your rehearsed minute budget.
- Read your question-level AO breakdown and state your gap without prompting.
- Produce a one-page Semester 2 target sheet with gap, evidence, action, and review date (LB7).
- Name one concrete thing from the Semester 2 preview you will meet prepared.

## Practice

Use the first three before the sitting, the last two after the return.

**1.** State the full paper from memory: sections, clocks, and what Section B runs on.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Two hours. Thirty minutes of viewing and notes on the unseen extract, four screenings, no notes on the first. Forty-five minutes for Section A. Forty-five for Section B, from the contexts menu, running on your own case studies. If any part of that needed looking up, say it aloud tomorrow and again the day after.

</details>

**2.** The night before: what travels to the venue, and what does not?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Pens, your minute budget in your head, and nothing else. The theory cards and the case-study document have done their work by then. Revision the night before is the loss-point cards and the frozen Q1 stem, not new material.

</details>

**3.** Why has the self-band ritual been running since October?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Calibration. Each time, you band your own script per criterion before seeing the mark, and the gap between the two numbers shrinks. A student whose self-band lands within one band of the marker knows what their work earns before being told. That is the skill the whole thread was building.

</details>

**4.** After the return: write your gap in AO language, with the script as evidence.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The model shape: "AO1 contexts: my case-study facts were pre-2020." Not "Section B went badly." An AO-language gap names the criterion, the evidence, and points at its own fix. It is the first row of your target sheet.

</details>

**5.** Build the target sheet to survive February: four rows, gap in AO language, evidence from the script and the semester's data, one specific Semester 2 action, and a review date that falls before the first Semester 2 attainment. Then keep the two living artifacts alive over the break: the theory-card deck and the case-study document both sit Paper 2 with you in June.
