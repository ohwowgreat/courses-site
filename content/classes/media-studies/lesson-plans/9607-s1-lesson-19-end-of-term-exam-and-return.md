## Overview

This lesson closes the semester with evidence. You sit the full two-hour Component 2 paper in the exam window. Your script comes back on Tue 01-19 with question-level AO breakdowns, so you can see where the marks went and which AO is your gap. On Wed 01-20 you turn that data into a one-page Semester 2 target sheet and post [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|LB7]]. The script counts in the 20% End-of-Term category, and the target sheet feeds your Semester 2 work and your Forecast Grade evidence.

## The ideas

### The exam paper

You sit **Component 2** in full: a two-hour paper under full exam conditions. **Section A** is on an unseen extract: 30 minutes viewing and notes, then 45 minutes writing. **Section B** is answered from the contexts menu: 45 minutes. The paper stays sealed until the sitting.

### Reading your marks

Your return starts with **self-banding**: you band your own script against each criterion from memory of what you wrote, before you look at the mark. Then you read the **question-level AO breakdown**, the marks your script earned per question. The distance between your self-band and your mark is your gap, and for most students that gap has been shrinking since October.

You then name your **gap** in AO language, using the script as evidence. Not "Section B went badly" but "AO1 contexts: my case-study facts were pre-2020."

### The target sheet

The **target sheet** is one page with four rows: **gap** (in AO language), **evidence** (the script plus your semester data, [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a1-timed-media-language-analysis|A1]] → [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a4-section-a-authentic-format|A4]] → [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-eot-component-2-paper|EoT]]), **action** (specific and shaped for Semester 2), and **review date** (a named Semester 2 week that falls before the first Semester 2 attainment). Write it to be read in February by the person who wrote it in January.

### Semester 2 preview

Semester 2 previewed honestly: C1 goes to Cambridge in the spring, the film-industry case study opens Section B properly, and Paper 2 sits in June. Note the two numbering systems: the coursebook uses "U6–U8", which is not the same as our calendar bands. The theory-card deck and the case-study document both carry across the break as the course's two living artifacts.

As an aside, the class re-runs the Lesson 01 wager, "I'm not impacted by media", and records the show of hands next to September's count.

## Day by day

These dates are provisional. They may shift when the school publishes the calendar for that period.

- **Exam window, Mon 01-11 → Mon 01-18.** You sit the full Component 2 paper, two hours, in the exam venue under full conditions. The exam office sets your exact slot within the window.
- **Tue 01-19.** Scripts return. You self-band from memory, then read your question-level AO breakdown, then write your gaps in AO language with the script as evidence.
- **Wed 01-20 (double).** You build your one-page Semester 2 target sheet, post LB7 (response to feedback and Semester 2 goal), and close the semester with the Lesson 01 wager re-run and the Semester 2 preview. No homework.

## Review

Check you can:

- Sit the full two-hour Component 2 paper using your rehearsed minute budget.
- Read your question-level AO breakdown and state your gap in AO language.
- Produce a one-page Semester 2 target sheet with gap, evidence, action, and review date (LB7).
- Name one concrete thing from the Semester 2 preview you will meet prepared.

## Practice

Use the first three before the sitting, the last two after the return.

**1.** State the full paper from memory: sections, clocks, and what Section B runs on.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Two hours. Thirty minutes of viewing and notes on the unseen extract, four screenings, no notes on the first. Forty-five minutes for Section A. Forty-five for Section B, from the contexts menu, running on your own case studies. If any part of that needed looking up, say it aloud tomorrow and again the day after.

</details>

**2.** The night before: what travels to the venue, and what does not?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Pens, your minute budget in your head, and nothing else. The theory cards and the case-study document have done their work by then. Revision the night before is the loss-point cards and the frozen Q1 stem, not new material.

</details>

**3.** Why has the self-band ritual been running since October?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Calibration. Each time, you band your own script per criterion before seeing the mark, and the gap between the two numbers shrinks. A student whose self-band lands within one band of the marker knows what their work earns before being told. That is the skill the whole thread was building.

</details>

**4.** After the return: write your gap in AO language, with the script as evidence.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The model shape: "AO1 contexts: my case-study facts were pre-2020." Not "Section B went badly." An AO-language gap names the criterion, the evidence, and points at its own fix. It is the first row of your target sheet.

</details>

**5.** Build the target sheet to survive February: four rows, gap in AO language, evidence from the script and the semester's data, one specific Semester 2 action, and a review date that falls before the first Semester 2 attainment. Then keep the two living artifacts alive over the break: the theory-card deck and the case-study document both sit Paper 2 with you in June.
