## Overview

This lesson brings the four technical elements together. First you lock your C1 plan to a shootable standard. Then, after the break, you run all four elements across one unseen extract in a single pass. You produce your **[[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS7]]**, a four-element analytical paragraph on an unseen extract, on Thu 2026-10-08. This is direct practice for [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a1-timed-media-language-analysis|A1]], four school days early. Your locked plan and this integrated reading both feed A1 and [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a2-blog-mid-point|A2]].

## 1 · Learn

### The planning lock

The **planning lock** is the point where your plan becomes a plan someone else could shoot without asking questions. Your plan passes only when it clears a ten-item **shootability checklist**: numbered shots, confirmed locations, cast attached to dates, equipment per session, a contingency day, assigned roles, costume and props, storyboard matching the shot list, a schedule that fits the break, and no outside dependencies. All ten must be green. In peer review, another group checks your suite against the checklist using the protocol **describe → question → suggest**. Costume and props on the plan must match your L05 design board, or one of the two is wrong.

Once locked, the plan is locked. Any change after the lock is a documented decision in the **production diary** (WS 6.4), not a silent swap. The diary's decisions and consequences columns are harvested later for your Creative Critical Reflection.

### The viewing protocol

The **[[concepts/the-viewing-protocol|viewing protocol]]** is a fixed reading sequence you use on every extract: framing → camera → light → mise-en-scène → sound → edit. Same order, every time, for two years. It exists because the most common failure is not ignorance. It is leaving a whole family of codes unvisited. The order forces you to visit each one.

### Writing a CS7 paragraph

A **CS7** paragraph is written in exam register and moves in a fixed line: technique named → correct terminology → meaning created → link to how a person, place, or event is represented. At least three of the four technical elements must appear.

### Common failure modes

Three failure modes recur in raw footage, the ones drilled in L03, L04, and L06: exposure lost on a bright background, audio recorded in an uncontrolled space, and a cut that will not match. When you screen your footage, name the shot you got right and the one that failed, and say why in the syllabus's words.

## 2 · Do

- **Wed 09-30 (double).** Peer-review each group's planning suite against the shootability checklist, apply fixes, then post and date your final plan on the blog before leaving. The production diary (WS 6.4) is issued. [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|HW1]] is set. - **Thu 10-08 (P1).** Apply the viewing protocol to unseen extracts. The teacher models one extract aloud in protocol order. You then read a second extract, played twice: watch first, take notes in order on the second pass. Write your CS7 paragraph; it is collected.

HW1 is debriefed with margin notes, annotated not graded. [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|LB1]] is posted, covering W5 and W6. A1 is the following Wednesday.

## 3 · Check

Check you can:

- Run all four technical elements across one unseen extract in a single pass, without a checklist.
- Write a CS7 paragraph that names a technique, its terminology, the meaning it creates, and the representation it constructs.
- Apply the viewing protocol in its fixed order: framing → camera → light → mise-en-scène → sound → edit.
- Produce a planning suite that passes the ten-item shootability checklist.
- Record changes to a locked plan as decisions in the production diary.
- Name the equipment your group has tested and will shoot on.
- Identify the three failure modes: exposure lost on a bright background, uncontrolled audio, and a cut that will not match.

## Practice

**1.** State the viewing protocol's order from memory.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Framing, camera, light, mise-en-scène, sound, edit. Same order, every extract, every time. The order is the cure for the commonest gap: whole code families left unvisited.

</details>

**2.** Put the four moves of a CS7 paragraph in order: the representation, the technique named, the effect on the viewer, the correct term.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Technique named, correct term, effect on the viewer, then the representation it constructs. Claim first, evidence second, effect third, and the representation last, because that is the one the mark scheme is actually looking for.

</details>

**3.** A script reads camera brilliantly and never mentions sound. What has it lost, and why?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Coverage. Section A names four elements and the examiner sees which family is missing. Sound is the one most often absent, which is why naming it well is the cheapest way to stand out.

</details>

**4.** State the planning lock's standard in one sentence, from memory.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

A plan someone else could shoot without asking questions. Numbered shots, confirmed locations, cast attached to dates, tested equipment, a contingency day. If a stranger would need to ask, the plan is not locked.

</details>

**5.** Take your own locked plan and find the shot you are least sure you can get. Write the contingency now, before the break, in one line.

**6.** Rehearse HW1 now. Run the protocol on any two-minute sequence and mark yourself for coverage only: which of the four elements did you leave out?
