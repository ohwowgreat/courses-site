## Overview

This week you build one artifact: the **Creative Critical Reflection (CCR)**. It carries about a fifth of the [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a3-c1-portfolio|A3]] marks, because it is where AO2 lives in the portfolio. Over four days you draft all four CCR questions, review them in [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS7]] against the C1 criteria on Wed 11-18, and post a complete redraft to the blog by Fri 11-20. The CCR then becomes portfolio material for A3, which is one week out.

## The ideas

### The rule: describe or reflect

One rule governs the whole week: cut every sentence that describes instead of reflects. A **description** says what you did. A **reflection** names a decision, a reason, or a change. The **describe-or-reflect test** tells them apart: a reflective sentence contains a decision, a reason, or a change; a descriptive sentence contains a sequence of events. Honest sentences read as reflection. Add sentences that flatter nobody, for example: "we chose the location because it was available, and it shows in the lighting."

### The format

Choose your CCR format by Friday. The menu is **director's commentary**, **voiceover presentation**, **podcast**, or **screencast**. "Presented creatively, using technology" is a requirement, not a suggestion.

### The four CCR questions

- **Q1: conventions and social groups.** How the product uses or challenges conventions, and how it represents. Your best material is the blog's own evidence and the stereotype audit from Lesson 08. A fixed stereotype is a reflection sentence waiting to happen.
- **Q2: audiences and distribution.** Who the product engages and how it would really circulate. Draw on the audience profile from post 3 and the screening response cards. "Young people aged 15–25" is banned as a sentence.
- **Q3: skills development.** From day-one footage to the rough cut: name what changed and what changed it.
- **Q4: technology integration.** Software, hardware, and online tools written as decisions, not inventory: "we used X so that Y."

### The production diaries

The **WS 6.4 production diaries**, locked in Lesson 05, are your evidence base. Every entry in the decisions and consequences columns is a ready-made reflection sentence: decided X because Y, consequence Z. Transcribe the strongest ones into your drafts.

### The C1 criteria

You review full drafts against the real **C1 criteria**. Read each draft, ask questions of it, then suggest changes, all in C1 criteria language. This is direct practice for A3.

## Day by day

- **Mon 11-16.** Draft Q1 against the blog evidence and the Lesson 08 stereotype audit. Add one honest sentence. Homework: finish the redraft moves agreed in class (30-min cap).
- **Tue 11-17.** Draft Q2 against the audience profile from post 3 and the screening response cards. Open the WS 6.4 production diaries beside your drafts and transcribe the three best decision/consequence entries. Add a second honest sentence.
- **Wed 11-18 (double).** Draft Q3 and Q4. Then run CS7: review full drafts against the real C1 criteria, teacher and peers. Each draft leaves with a written action list in C1 criteria language.
- **Fri 11-20.** Sort ten sample sentences, five descriptive and five reflective, then name the tell. Run the describe-or-reflect test on your own draft, cutting or converting the describers. Pitch your chosen format in fours, 60 seconds each, then vote and finalize the format. Post your complete, dated redraft to the blog before you leave. This session runs sentence surgery; whether Foreign Culture Day affects the day will be confirmed in class.

## Review

Check you can:

- Draft Q1 and Q2 answers that name real conventions used and real audience decisions, including ones that failed.
- Draft Q3 and Q4 answers that evidence learning through named mistakes and changes.
- Apply the C1 criteria to a peer's draft and spot description masquerading as reflection.
- Use the describe-or-reflect test so every sentence names a decision, a reason, or a change.

## Practice

Run these against your own draft. The production diary from the planning lock is your evidence base.

**1.** State the describe-or-reflect tell from memory, then sort this sentence: "We filmed the opening on Tuesday and edited it on Wednesday."

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

A reflective sentence contains a decision, a reason, or a change. A descriptive one contains a sequence of events. The sample is description. One conversion: "We reshot the opening on Tuesday because the first take hid the villain too well, and the recut on Wednesday moved the reveal three seconds earlier."

</details>

**2.** Name what each of the four CCR questions asks, in your own words.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Q1: how your product uses or challenges conventions, and how it represents social groups. Q2: who it engages and how it would really circulate. Q3: how your skills developed, evidenced by what changed. Q4: how you integrated technology, as decisions rather than inventory.

</details>

**3.** Why does "we used Premiere, a tripod, and a phone" fail Q4, and what shape fixes it?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

It is an inventory. The fixed shape is "we used X so that Y": the tool tied to the decision it enabled. "We recorded room tone on the phone so that the dialogue cuts stopped jumping" is Q4 material. The list alone is not.

</details>

**4.** Your CCR must spread across more than one application or format. Why is this rule worth marks?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Both of Cambridge's published C1 exemplars answered all four questions in a single digital tool, and both moderators criticized it. One portfolio sat at the top of Level 5 everywhere except the CCR, for exactly this. The syllabus permits a different format per question. This is the cheapest credit in Component 1.

</details>

**5.** The sentence "our audience is young people aged 15 to 25" is banned from Q2. What replaces it?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Evidence. Your post 3 audience profile, the screening response cards, what actual viewers understood and where their attention dropped. Q2 wants who the product really engages and how it would really circulate, not a demographic guess.

</details>

**6.** Harvest your production diary: transcribe the three best decisions/consequences entries into reflection sentences. Then add one honest sentence that flatters nobody. Honesty is what examiners read as reflection.
