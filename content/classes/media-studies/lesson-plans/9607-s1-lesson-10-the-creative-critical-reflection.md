## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Draft Q1 and Q2 answers that name real conventions used and real audience decisions, including ones that failed
2. Draft Q3 and Q4 answers where learning is evidenced by named mistakes and changes
3. Apply the C1 criteria to a peer's draft and identify description masquerading as reflection
4. Post a redraft where sampled sentences pass the describe-or-reflect test

## Goal

One week, one artifact: the creative critical reflection carries a fifth of the A3 marks, because it is where AO2 lives in the portfolio. One rule governs all four days and is also the success criterion: **cut every sentence that describes instead of reflects.** A description says what you did; a reflection names a decision, a reason, or a change. The production diaries you were issued at the L05 lock are your evidence base — this week they pay out.

## How it runs

### D1 — Mon 11-16: Q1, against the evidence

- **The artifact (10 min)** — the four CCR questions in student language, EN/ZH; the format menu (director's commentary, voiceover presentation, podcast, screencast), chosen by Friday. "Presented creatively, using technology" is a requirement, not a suggestion.
- **Q1 drafted (25 min)** — *conventions and social groups*: how the product uses or challenges conventions, and how it represents — drafted against your blog's own evidence and last week's stereotype audit. The L08 audit findings are Q1's best material.
- **Close (10 min)** — add **one honest sentence** that flatters nobody ("we chose the location because it was available, and it shows in the lighting"). Honesty is what examiners read as reflection.

### D2 — Tue 11-17: Q2, and the diaries pay out

- **Q2 drafted (25 min)** — *audiences and distribution*: who the product engages and how it would really circulate. Use the audience profile from post 3 and the screening response cards as evidence; "young people aged 15–25" is banned as a sentence.
- **Diary harvest (15 min)** — open your **WS 6.4 production diaries** beside your drafts: every entry in the decisions/consequences columns is a ready-made reflection sentence — *decided X because Y; consequence Z*. Transcribe the three best into your drafts.
- **Close (5 min)** — add the second honest sentence.

### D3 — Wed 11-18 (double): Q3, Q4, and CS7

- **Q3 + Q4 drafted (40 min)** — *skills development* (from day-one footage to the rough cut — name what changed and what changed it) and *technology integration* (software, hardware, online — as decisions, not inventory: "we used X *so that* Y").
- **CS7 (40 min)** — full drafts reviewed against the C1 criteria, teacher and peers, describe → question → suggest; every draft leaves with a written action list in C1 criteria language.
- **Close (10 min)** — surgery previewed: one sample sentence converted live from describe to reflect.

### D4 — Fri 11-20: sentence surgery

Whether this session runs on Foreign Culture Day will be confirmed in class.

- **The sort (15 min)** — the surgery sheet: ten sample sentences, five descriptive, five reflective; sort them, then name the tell — reflective sentences contain a decision, a reason, or a change; descriptive ones contain a sequence of events.
- **The surgery (25 min)** — run the test on your own draft, sentence by sentence: describers cut or converted. Finalize your format.
- **Post before leaving (5 min)** — post the complete redraft to the blog, dated. A3 is one week out; the CCR is now portfolio material, not homework.
