## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

1. Track a narrative as equilibrium, disruption and new equilibrium on a text not used in teaching
2. Map characters by function rather than identity and name where Propp strains
3. Post a planning suite (storyboard or flat plan, shot or shoot list, schedule, roles) that passes the shootability checklist in peer review
4. Return HW1 on time and name one cut or sound choice in your own footage using the new vocabulary (LB2)

## Goal

On the Wednesday double — the last before the break — you lock your C1 plan to a shootable standard: a plan someone else could shoot without asking questions. HW1 goes out the same day because A1's notice window falls inside the holiday and cannot wait. The Friday session after the break is the debrief: annotated, not graded, and your first introduction to the sound and editing vocabulary the shoots have just made real.

## How it runs

### D1 — Mon 09-28: Todorov

- **Input via WS 3.2 (30 min)** — **I-do:** Todorov's arc (equilibrium → disruption → recognition → repair → new equilibrium) modelled on one short narrative; **We-do:** the class tracks it on a second text together; **You-do:** you map a story you know. Note the caveat: the model fails on non-linear narratives, and saying so earns marks.
- **Application (10 min)** — turn the arc on your own C1 plan: where is the disturbance in your opening?
- **Close (5 min)** — theory card: Todorov.

### D2 — Tue 09-29: Propp, and the blogs brought current

- **Retrieval (5 min)** — the five stages, no notes.
- **Input (15 min)** — Propp: characters classified **by what they do, not who they are** — hero, villain, donor, helper, princess, dispatcher, false hero — with the caveat attached (folk tales, 1928; apply with judgment). Binary oppositions via a worked *Lupin* table: narratives run on conflicts of value, not just people.

- **Mapping (15 min)** — in pairs, map one known story's cast onto functions, then your own C1 opening: who is your donor? do you even have one?
- **LB1 + catch-up (10 min)** — **LB1 posted** (blog punctuality, posts 1–5); ten minutes reserved, in the room, to bring lagging blogs current. Theory card: Propp.

### D3 — Wed 09-30 (double): the planning lock

- **The standard (10 min)** — the ten-item shootability checklist: numbered shots, confirmed locations, cast attached to dates, equipment per session, contingency day, assigned roles, costume/props, storyboard↔shot-list match, schedule fits the break, no outside dependencies.
- **Peer review (45 min)** — another group reviews your planning suite (WS 6.8 storyboard or 6.7 flat plan, shot list, WS 6.3 schedule, WS 6.1 roles, WS 6.2 casting) against the checklist, protocol **describe → question → suggest** — no "it's fine," every item checked or challenged.
- **The lock (20 min)** — apply fixes, then post and date your final plans **on the blog before leaving**. Locked means locked: changes after today are documented decisions in the production diary, not silent swaps. **WS 6.4 production diary issued** — you use its decisions/consequences columns later (L10) for the CCR.
- **Announcements (15 min)** — **HW1 set and sized aloud:** an A1-format analysis of a self-chosen 2–3 minute sequence (teacher-viewable platform, not a taught text), due Fri 10-09. Break production window: 3–4 hours of shooting, diary entries as you go. **Equipment confirmed on the way out** — school camera sign-out collected, or own kit named; the "equipment per session" item must point at a kit your group has actually tested.

### D4 — Fri 10-09 (post-break): the return

- **Footage first (20 min)** — sound and editing vocabulary (the WS 2.2/2.3 terms) taught against your own break footage where it exists: find a J-cut you accidentally made; name the ambient sound you forgot to control.
- **HW1 debrief (20 min)** — annotated, not graded: the margin notes are A1 feedback five days early. Common gaps collected on the board.
- **LB2 (5 min)** — posted: HW1 returned on time, glossary current. A1 is Wednesday.
