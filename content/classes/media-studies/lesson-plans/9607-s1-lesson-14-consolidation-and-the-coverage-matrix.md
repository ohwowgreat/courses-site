## Overview

This lesson audits the semester's knowledge before the exam tests it. You review your [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a4-section-a-authentic-format|A4]] script against the mark scheme, rehearse Section B arguments aloud, and build a coverage matrix that crosses every case study against the four Key Concepts. You complete the coverage matrix on Wed 12-23 and leave with your weakest cell named in writing. That cell is your [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|HW2]] target: one revision page per case study plus one Section B essay plan, due Mon 2027-01-04. This work feeds the End of Term Exam.

## The ideas

### Reviewing your A4 script

Read your returned A4 script against the five-criteria rubric and band yourself on each criterion before you see your mark. Then compare your self-band with the mark and its per-criterion AO splits. The **AO gap** is the distance between your self-band and the actual mark: name it in one sentence. Log one target from the splits and connect it in writing to your Lesson 11 target. You now have two data points, so the Forecast Grade conversation has evidence.

### The Section B argument

A Section B argument has four moves: **claim → evidence → counter → response**. This is not an essay plan. It is an argument you could win out loud. You rehearse it by speaking it: one person argues a case-study question, one counters from the same case study, one referees for evidence. Capture the argued case as a skeleton plan only after you have argued it, because a plan that survived a counter holds up better than one written cold.

### The coverage matrix

The **coverage matrix** crosses every case study against the four Key Concepts. The rows are your case studies. The columns are **Language**, **Representation**, **Industry**, and **Audience**. You claim a cell by producing the evidence from your documents, and each cell is scored honestly: evidenced, thin, or empty. An honestly called "empty" scores too, because the point is the audit, not the bluff. Your weakest cell is your HW2 target.

## Practice

Run these across the break. They are the audit the exam will run on you anyway.

**1.** Name the four moves of a Section B argument, and say why the third one is there.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Claim, evidence, counter, response. The counter is there because an argument that has never met one goes stale on the page. A plan that survived a real counter aloud writes itself under time. That is why the talking comes before the writing.

</details>

**2.** Describe the coverage matrix: its rows, its columns, its three cell scores, and why an honest "empty" scores.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Rows are your case studies. Columns are **Language, Representation, Industry, Audience**. Each cell is scored evidenced, thin, or empty. An honest empty scores because the matrix rewards the audit, not the bluff: the named empty cell is exactly what HW2 and the break exist to fill.

</details>

**3.** Why do you band your own A4 script before you see the mark?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Calibration. The conversation that matters is the gap between your band and the marker's, per criterion. With the L11 target you now have two data points on yourself. The gaps shrink when you track them, and the Forecast Grade conversation runs on that evidence.

</details>

**4.** What is the End of Term Exam, in format terms?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

The full two-hour Component 2: A4's format plus Section B. Thirty minutes of viewing and notes, then the two written sections. Nothing in it is something you have not now done.

</details>

**5.** Build your own mini-matrix now. List your case studies as rows, the four Key Concepts as columns, and score every cell honestly: evidenced, thin, or empty. Write your weakest cell at the top of your HW2 plan. That cell is what your Section B essay plan should aim at.

**6.** Rehearse one argument aloud before you write anything. Argue one Section B question to someone from your case study. Have them counter from the same case study. Only then write the skeleton plan. Plans written cold go stale. Plans that survived a counter do not.
