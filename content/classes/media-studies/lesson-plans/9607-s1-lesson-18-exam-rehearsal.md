## Overview

This is the last teaching week, and it rehearses the exam until nothing about it is a surprise. You walk through the whole of Paper 2, drill five-minute Section B plans, and write [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS13]]: a full timed Section B on your own case studies, on Wed 2027-01-06. You peer-mark a classmate's script against the mark scheme, then see it moderated aloud. By Friday you name where you lose marks and how to stop it. [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|HW2]] is due Mon 2027-01-04, and your only homework after this is revision from your marked CS13 and the matrix. Everything here feeds the End of Term Exam.

## 1 · Learn

### The paper structure

**Paper 2** runs to a fixed clock: 30 minutes viewing and notes (four screenings, with no notes on the first and notes crossed out), then 45 minutes Section A, then 45 minutes Section B. You have met these parts in [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a4-section-a-authentic-format|A4]] and [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS12]]; this is the assembled whole. Learn your own per-section minute budget: paragraph time, plan time, and check time inside each 45-minute block.

**Frozen Q1 stem** is Question 1's wording, which is identical in every sitting. Know it cold before the exam.

### Planning a Section B answer

A **Section B plan** is a five-minute outline built from claim, evidence, counter, and response, plus the case-study facts you will cite. Done round by round, the plans grow denser and calmer. A good plan lets someone else write your essay from it; if a plan leaves gaps, those are the same gaps the examiner would see.

### Marking to the scheme

Peer-marking uses the **official five-criteria rubric**: you mark a classmate's script with the band words, best-fit per criterion, and comments in the WS 4.6 comment-bank style. Reading the scheme as a marker is the point, so you know how your own script will be read. The teacher then moderates a sample aloud to show where marks drifted.

A **pairing sentence** (WS 4.5) states a technique and its content in one sentence, linking what is used to what it does.

### The AO economy

The **AO economy** is where the 25 marks sit: **AO1** is worth 15 marks (concepts, contexts, terminology) and **AO2** is worth 10 marks (analysis, examples), scored across five criteria on a best-fit basis. Map this against your CS13 result to see which criteria you feed and which you starve.

**Loss points** are the two places you are most likely to drop marks, each paired with a counter-move. For example: "I list techniques without effects" is fixed by giving every technique a "so that" clause. Write both on a card for your revision deck.

## 2 · Do

- **Mon 01-04.** HW2 is due. Walk through all of Paper 2 using a sealed past paper, including the rubric's clock and the frozen Q1 stem. Do two rounds of five-minute Section B plans from the CS13 bank. Write your own minute budget for each section.
- **Wed 01-06 (double).** Sit CS13: a full Section B, timed at 45 minutes, on your own case studies, with one question chosen on the day. Then peer-mark a classmate's script against the five-criteria rubric, and watch the teacher moderate three scripts aloud.
- **Thu 01-07.** Do round three of the five-minute plan. Swap plans and test whether you could write a stranger's essay from theirs. Revisit where the peer-marking drifted and which criterion caused it. Finish with WS 4.5 pairing sentences at speed.
- **Fri 01-08 (last teaching day).** Map the AO economy against the class's CS13 results. Leave with two named loss points and a counter-move for each, written on a revision card. Note the exam logistics: the window opens Monday, your slot comes from the exam office, screening facilities are booked, and you bring only pens.

## 3 · Check

Check you can:

- State the full paper structure and your per-section minute budget from memory.
- Write a complete Section B answer in 45 minutes using your own case studies.
- Mark a peer's script against the five-criteria rubric and justify the band you award.
- Name your two most likely loss points and the counter-move for each.

## Practice

The week's goal is to make the exam boring. These make it boring faster.

**1.** State the paper's clock from memory.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Thirty minutes of viewing and notes: four screenings, no notes on the first. Then forty-five minutes for Section A and forty-five for Section B. If you had to look that up, run this question again tomorrow.

</details>

**2.** Where do Section A's 25 marks actually sit?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

AO1 carries 15, across concepts, contexts, and terminology. AO2 carries 10, across analysis and examples. Five criteria, best-fit per criterion. Feeding the criteria you naturally starve is worth more than polishing the ones you naturally feed.

</details>

**3.** What must a five-minute Section B plan contain, and what is the test of a good one?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Claim, evidence, counter, response, plus the case-study facts you will use. The test: could a stranger write the essay from your plan? If not, what is missing from the plan is what the examiner would find missing from the essay.

</details>

**4.** Why does peer-marking a script teach more than being marked?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because you read the mark scheme as a marker once, band words and all, before it is used on you. The scheme stops being an enemy the moment you have wielded it. The mark you award is a by-product. The reading is the lesson.

</details>

**5.** Write your two loss-point cards. Model: "I list techniques without effects, so every technique now gets a 'so that' clause." Name your own two most likely mark losses and the counter-move for each. Put the card in your revision deck.

**6.** Run the plan drill: three rounds of five-minute Section B plans from your own case studies, pens down at five exactly. Round by round the plans get denser and calmer. That is the drill working.
