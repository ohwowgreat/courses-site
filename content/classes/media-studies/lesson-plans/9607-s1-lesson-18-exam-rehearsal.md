## Overview

This is the last teaching week, and it removes surprises from the exam: format, timing, and how the mark scheme awards marks. You produce **[[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS13]]**, a full Section B script written under time on your own case studies, on Wed 2027-01-06. You peer-mark a classmate's script and then see it moderated. Everything this week feeds straight into the End of Term Exam.

## The ideas

### The paper, end to end

You need the **Paper 2 structure** and its timing by memory. The rubric's clock runs: 30 minutes viewing and notes (four screenings, with no notes on the first screening and notes crossed out), then 45 minutes for Section A, then 45 minutes for Section B. Inside each section, set a personal minute budget: time for paragraphs, plan time, and check time.

The **frozen Q1 stem** is the wording of question 1. It is verbatim-identical in every sitting, so you can know it cold before you walk in.

### Planning a Section B answer

A Section B plan holds four moves plus support: **claim, evidence, counter, response**, together with your case-study facts. A good plan is one a stranger could write your essay from. If something is missing from your plan, the examiner will miss it too. With practice, five-minute plans get denser and calmer.

A **pairing sentence** joins technique and content in one sentence (WS 4.5): you name a technique and say what it does in the text.

### Marking as an examiner

**Beat the Examiner** is the peer-marking routine. You mark a classmate's script against the official five-criteria rubric, using the exact band words, and write comments in the WS 4.6 comment-bank style. The point is to read the mark scheme as a marker at least once before it is used on you. The mark you give is secondary. Your teacher then moderates a sample of scripts aloud, showing where peer marks drifted.

### The AO economy

Section B is worth 25 marks. They split as **AO1: 15 marks** (concepts, contexts, terminology) and **AO2: 10 marks** (analysis, examples). There are five criteria, marked best-fit per criterion. Knowing where the marks sit tells you which criteria you feed naturally and which you starve.

A **loss point** is a habit that costs you marks, paired with a **counter-move** that fixes it. Example: "I list techniques without effects → every technique gets a 'so that' clause." You leave the week with two named loss points and a counter-move for each, written on a card for your revision deck.

## Day by day

- **Mon 01-04.** [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|HW2]] is due today. Walk through the full Paper 2 end to end using a past paper, learn the rubric clock and the frozen Q1 stem, then write your own per-section minute budget.
- **Tue 01-05.** Five-minute Section B plans, three rounds, from the CS13 question bank: read, plan (claim, evidence, counter, response, plus case-study facts), pens down at five minutes. Swap round-three plans to check they could be written from. One last round of WS 4.5 pairing sentences.
- **Wed 01-06 (double).** Sit CS13: a full Section B, timed 45 minutes, on your own case studies, on a question chosen on the day. Peer-mark a classmate's script against the five-criteria rubric, then watch three scripts moderated aloud.
- **Fri 01-08 (last teaching day).** Map the AO economy against the class CS13 results, write your two loss points and counter-moves on a card, and go over exam logistics once: the window opens Monday, bring nothing but pens. Homework is individual revision from your marked CS13 and the matrix; there are no new tasks.

## Review

Check you can:

- State the full Paper 2 structure and your per-section minute budget from memory.
- Write a complete Section B answer in 45 minutes using your own case studies.
- Build a Section B plan with claim, evidence, counter, and response plus case-study facts.
- Mark a peer script against the five-criteria rubric and justify the band you award.
- Split Section B's 25 marks into AO1 (15) and AO2 (10) and say which criteria you feed and starve.
- Name your two most likely loss points and the counter-move for each.

## Practice

The week's goal is to make the exam boring. These make it boring faster.

**1.** State the paper's clock from memory.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Thirty minutes of viewing and notes: four screenings, no notes on the first. Then forty-five minutes for Section A and forty-five for Section B. If you had to look that up, run this question again tomorrow.

</details>

**2.** Where do Section A's 25 marks actually sit?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

AO1 carries 15, across concepts, contexts, and terminology. AO2 carries 10, across analysis and examples. Five criteria, best-fit per criterion. Feeding the criteria you naturally starve is worth more than polishing the ones you naturally feed.

</details>

**3.** What must a five-minute Section B plan contain, and what is the test of a good one?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Claim, evidence, counter, response, plus the case-study facts you will use. The test: could a stranger write the essay from your plan? If not, what is missing from the plan is what the examiner would find missing from the essay.

</details>

**4.** Why does peer-marking a script teach more than being marked?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because you read the mark scheme as a marker once, band words and all, before it is used on you. The scheme stops being an enemy the moment you have wielded it. The mark you award is a by-product. The reading is the lesson.

</details>

**5.** Write your two loss-point cards. Model: "I list techniques without effects, so every technique now gets a 'so that' clause." Name your own two most likely mark losses and the counter-move for each. Put the card in your revision deck.

**6.** Run the plan drill: three rounds of five-minute Section B plans from your own case studies, pens down at five exactly. Round by round the plans get denser and calmer. That is the drill working.
