## Overview

This is the last teaching week, spent removing any surprise in format, timing, or mark scheme. You walk through the whole paper, drill plans against the clock, then write **[[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|CS9]]**, a full timed Section B answer on your own case studies on Wed 2027-01-06. You peer-mark a script before yours is marked, so you read the scheme as a marker once. You leave naming exactly where you lose marks. All of this feeds straight into the End of Term Exam.

## The ideas

### The paper, end to end

**Paper 2** runs on a fixed clock: **30 minutes viewing and notes**, then **45 minutes Section A**, then **45 minutes Section B**. In the viewing time there are four screenings; you take no notes on the first screening, and notes on later screenings are crossed out before you start writing. Build a personal minute budget inside each section: time for paragraphs, plan time, and check time.

The **Question 1 stem** is frozen. It is verbatim-identical in every sitting, so you can know it by heart before the exam.

### Planning a Section B answer

A strong Section B plan carries **claim, evidence, counter, response**, plus the case-study facts you will cite. Plan in five minutes: read, plan, pens down at five minutes exactly. A test of a plan: could a stranger write your essay from it? If not, what is missing is what the examiner would miss too.

Pair technique with content in one sentence, so each technique is named with its effect.

### Peer-marking: Beat the Examiner

In **Beat the Examiner** you mark a peer's script against the **official five-criteria rubric**, using the band words and writing comments in the comment-bank style. The closest banding to the moderated mark wins the round. Reading the scheme as a marker is the point; the mark is a by-product.

### The AO economy

The 25 marks split by objective: **AO1 is 15 marks** (concepts, contexts, terminology) and **AO2 is 10 marks** (analysis, examples). There are five criteria, marked best-fit per criterion. Map this against your own CS9 result: see which criteria you feed naturally and which you starve.

From that, name your **two loss points and the counter-move for each**, written on one card for your revision deck. Example: "I list techniques without effects, so every technique gets a 'so that' clause."

## Day by day

- **Mon 01-04.** [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-course-skills-and-homework|HW2]] is due today. Walk through the full Paper 2 on a past paper: the clock, the four screenings, and the frozen Question 1 stem read aloud. Write your personal minute budget for each section.
- **Tue 01-05.** Drill five-minute Section B plans in three rounds, using the CS9 question bank: read, plan claim / evidence / counter / response with case-study facts, pens down at five minutes. Swap round-three plans and check whether a stranger could write from them. Finish with technique-plus-content pairing sentences at speed.
- **Wed 01-06 (double).** One question is chosen on the day. Write **CS9**, the full Section B, timed at 45 minutes on your own case studies. Then peer-mark a script against the five-criteria rubric in Beat the Examiner, and watch the teacher moderate three scripts aloud.
- **Fri 01-08 (last teaching day).** Map where the 25 marks sit, AO1 15 and AO2 10, against the class's CS9 results. Leave with two named loss points and a counter-move for each on one card. The exam window opens Monday; your slot comes from the exam office, screening facilities are booked, and you bring only pens.

## Review

Check you can:

- State the full **Paper 2** structure and your per-section minute budget from memory.
- Recall that the **Question 1 stem** is frozen and identical in every sitting.
- Write a complete Section B answer in 45 minutes using your own case studies.
- Build a plan with **claim, evidence, counter, response**, and case-study facts.
- Mark a peer script against the **five-criteria rubric** and justify the band you award.
- Split the 25 marks into **AO1 (15)** and **AO2 (10)** and say which criteria you feed and which you starve.
- Name your two most likely loss points and the counter-move for each.

## Practice

The week's goal is to make the exam boring. These make it boring faster.

**1.** State the paper's clock from memory.

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Thirty minutes of viewing and notes: four screenings, no notes on the first. Then forty-five minutes for Section A and forty-five for Section B. If you had to look that up, run this question again tomorrow.

</details>

**2.** Where do Section A's 25 marks actually sit?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

AO1 carries 15, across concepts, contexts, and terminology. AO2 carries 10, across analysis and examples. Five criteria, best-fit per criterion. Feeding the criteria you naturally starve is worth more than polishing the ones you naturally feed.

</details>

**3.** What must a five-minute Section B plan contain, and what is the test of a good one?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Claim, evidence, counter, response, plus the case-study facts you will use. The test: could a stranger write the essay from your plan? If not, what is missing from the plan is what the examiner would find missing from the essay.

</details>

**4.** Why does peer-marking a script teach more than being marked?

<details class="reveal"><summary>Show answer</summary>

Because you read the mark scheme as a marker once, band words and all, before it is used on you. The scheme stops being an enemy the moment you have wielded it. The mark you award is a by-product. The reading is the lesson.

</details>

**5.** Write your two loss-point cards. Model: "I list techniques without effects, so every technique now gets a 'so that' clause." Name your own two most likely mark losses and the counter-move for each. Put the card in your revision deck.

**6.** Run the plan drill: three rounds of five-minute Section B plans from your own case studies, pens down at five exactly. Round by round the plans get denser and calmer. That is the drill working.
