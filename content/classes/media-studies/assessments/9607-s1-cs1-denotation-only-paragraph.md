# Media Studies · S1 CS1: Denotation-Only Paragraph

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Mon 2026-09-07, in class, 25 minutes |
| What | One paragraph on an unseen still, describing only |
| Marked on | AO2 |
| Feeds | A1 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Describe what an image literally shows, without interpreting it
2. Recognise an evaluative adjective as it arrives, and hold it back
3. Survive a peer hunting your paragraph for smuggled meaning

## Description

One paragraph about an unseen still, **describing only what is literally there**.

This is the hardest paragraph in the subject, and it looks like the easiest. Your mind wants to tell me what the image means. The task is to stop at what it shows.

"A woman is seated, hand at her chin. Two children face away, heads on her shoulders" is description. "A worried mother" is interpretation, and it costs you the mark.

**Evaluative adjectives are the giveaway.** Worried, poor, desperate, beautiful, striking. If you can argue about the word, it does not belong here.

Sentence frames are available and you may use them. Describing before meaning is what A1 rewards, so it is installed in week two rather than discovered in October.

## Guidelines

- One paragraph. Twenty-five minutes is generous, so spend the first five looking.
- Work through the frame in order: foreground, background, figures, text.
- Use the frames if you want them: "The image shows…", "In the foreground…", "The figure is positioned…", "The text reads…"
- No adjectives that carry a judgement. Colours and positions are fine.
- Before you hand it in, read it back hunting for one interpretive word. There is usually one.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Zero interpretive claims. Coverage is systematic, and the detail is precise enough that a reader could sketch the image from your words. |
| **Secure** | One or two evaluative words slip through, but the paragraph is fundamentally description and covers the frame. |
| **Developing** | Description and interpretation mixed throughout, or coverage is patchy and stops at the obvious. |
| **Not yet** | The paragraph explains what the image means. Removing the interpretation would leave very little. |

Marked for discipline, not polish. Zero interpretive claims is the criterion.
