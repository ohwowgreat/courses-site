# Media Studies · S1 Course Skills and Homework

[[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]] · [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-assessments|S1 Assessment Register]] · Course Skills and homework, all fifteen items

The four attainments each have their own page with a full rubric. The fifteen Course Skills items are smaller by design, and this page is where they are all written down: what each one is, when it happens, what you produce, what it is marked on, and what it feeds. Nothing here is a surprise task. Each one rehearses a move an attainment will ask for later.

## How these count

**Course Skills is 20% of your grade**, made of the thirteen CS items and the two homework tasks below. They are marked on whether the specific skill is there, not on polish, and they carry **72 hours' notice** rather than the week an attainment gets. Most are done in class. The two that are not are the break homework tasks, and both are capped: **three to four hours across a holiday, not more.** If a task is taking longer than its cap, stop and say so.

The rule that runs through most of them: **each CS task is direct practice for something larger.** The register names which one. Do them as rehearsals and the attainments stop being events.

**CS3 to CS6 work differently, and you should know why.** Each is a practical task on one technical element, made in class on your own kit and posted to your Component 1 blog the same day. They are marked, but they are also *evidence*: under Cambridge's own Foundation Portfolio structure (research, practice, planning, production, reflection) these four posts are your practice stage. You are not doing an exercise about the portfolio. You are building the portfolio, and being marked on it as you go.

## Units 1 and 2: the foundation, then the four technical elements

### CS1 · Denotation-only paragraph

**Fri 2026-09-04, in class, 25 minutes.** One paragraph about an unseen still, describing only what is literally there. Sentence frames are available. Marked on **AO2**, and the criterion is discipline: zero interpretive claims. Evaluative adjectives are the giveaway. It feeds A1, where the same describing-before-meaning move is worth marks under time.

### CS2 · Annotated media text

**Fri 2026-09-11, homework-based, set on the Monday.** Choose an everyday media text and annotate it with **at least six accurate code labels**. Peer-checked against the exemplar before it is collected. Marked on **AO1**. The 30-minute cap applies: size the text, not the ambition.

### CS3 · Camera: the six-shot rebuild

**Thu 2026-09-17, in class, shot in the second half of the session.** In pairs, you are given a six-frame storyboard you did not draw and you shoot it: establishing shot, mid-shot, a slow tilt to reveal, a close-up on an object, a low angle, a point-of-view shot. Phones are fine. Three controls are non-negotiable: **hold focus, expose for the face, frame to the thirds.** Posted and dated on the blog with **one line of intention per shot**, in the syllabus's terms.

Marked on **AO1 + AO3**: terms used accurately, the three controls held, an intention stated per shot. It is not marked on how good the footage looks, and acting is not assessed anywhere in this syllabus.

### CS4 · Sound: the 30-second soundscape

**Fri 2026-09-18, in class.** A 30-second sound design for the opening you are actually making, built from your own recordings, the foley takes from Wednesday, and one non-diegetic element. Four things must be in it: one clearly diegetic sound, one clearly non-diegetic, one with a near or far perspective, and one that carries across an implied cut as a bridge. Posted with a rationale naming every choice by its term.

Marked on **AO1 + AO3**. Sound is the element Cambridge itself says candidates neglect, which makes this the cheapest advantage in the course.

### CS5 · Mise-en-scène: the design board

**Thu 2026-09-24, in class.** A six-cell design board for your own opening: location (with the photograph you took), costume for one character, two meaningful properties, a lighting plan named by set-up and quality, a color palette of three to five swatches, and a one-sentence claim about what the design says. **Every cell carries a connotation line.** A cell without one is a moodboard entry, and moodboards earn nothing.

Marked on **AO1 + AO3**: coverage of the syllabus elements, a connotation per cell, internal consistency. This board is your group's C1 design document and it is checked against your plan at the planning lock.

### CS6 · Editing: continuity held and broken

**Mon 2026-09-28, in class.** Two versions of the three-shot sequence you shot on Wednesday. Version one holds the 180-degree rule. Version two breaks it on purpose. Both posted, with two or three sentences naming what breaks in version two and what the continuity system was doing for the viewer in version one.

Marked on **AO1 + AO3**: the continuity version actually holds, the break is the intended one rather than an accident, and the written difference uses the right terms.

### CS7 · The four-element paragraph

**Thu 2026-10-08, in class, 15 minutes.** This is the first session after the break, four school days before A1. One analytical paragraph on an unseen extract, in exam register: technique named → correct terminology → meaning created → link to how a person, place or event is represented. At least three of the four elements must appear.

Marked on **AO2**. This is A1 with the stakes off, two weeks early, and it is the single best predictor of how A1 will go.

### CS8 · The technical lexicon quiz

**Mon 2026-10-12, in class, 20 minutes.** Thirty items, define-the-term and label-the-example, drawn from all four technical elements plus the semiotic base and the [[concepts/media-vocabulary|Cambridge glossary]]. At least four items on sound and four on editing, because those are the families scripts drop. Marked on **AO1**; you self-mark immediately, and the self-marking does more teaching than the quiz. It sits two days before A1 on purpose: whatever you miss here is your revision list.

## The break tasks

### HW1 · Break analysis and the production window

**Set Wed 2026-09-30, due Fri 2026-10-09.** Two things across the National Day break. First, an **A1-format analysis** of a 2 to 3 minute sequence you choose yourself: not a text taught in class, and on a platform your teacher can view. Second, the **production window**: three to four hours of shooting for your Component 1 product, with diary entries as you go. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. The analysis is debriefed with margin notes rather than a grade, which makes it A1 feedback five days early.

### HW2 · Case-study revision pack and an essay plan

**Set Wed 2026-12-23, due Mon 2027-01-04.** Across the winter break: a revision pack from your case-study document, plus **one Section B essay plan** aimed at your weakest cell in the coverage matrix. Marked on **AO1**. It feeds CS13 directly, so the plan you write over the holiday is the one you rehearse against the clock in the first week back.

## Units 3 to 5: the coursework and craft items

### CS9 · Peer blog audit

**Wed 2026-10-21, in class, in the double.** You audit a partner's blog post by post against the **mid-point checklist**, using describe, question, suggest, and you write findings as actions rather than comments: "post 3 has no audience data, add the survey screenshot" beats "more detail". Marked on **AO3**. The checklist is the one **A2 is marked with**, which makes this your A2 marking preview with three teaching days left to act on it.

### CS10 · Representation close reading

**Fri 2026-11-13, in class, 20 minutes.** One close-reading paragraph on a provided still or short clip, with a **theorist doing analytical work rather than decoration**: "as Mulvey's camera-look predicts…" not "Mulvey said the male gaze". The theorist sentence-starters card is on the desk. Marked on **AO2**. Papers swap at the end so a partner can hunt for a decorated theorist before they come in.

### CS11 · CCR draft review

**Thu 2026-11-19, in class.** Your full creative critical reflection draft, completed in the Wednesday double the day before, reviewed against the **real C1 criteria** by teacher and peers. You leave with a written action list in C1 criteria language. Marked on **AO2**, and it is direct practice for A3, where the CCR is where AO2 lives in the portfolio.

### CS12 · Guided Section A

**Wed 2026-12-09, in class, 50 minutes.** A Section A response written **with the mark scheme visible and consultable**: you may stop, check the band language, and annotate your own margin. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. Afterwards you label which criterion each paragraph was earning and find the one you never fed. That gap is your A4 target, and the scaffold is removed there.

### CS13 · Timed Section B

**Wed 2027-01-06, in class, 45 minutes.** A full Section B answer under time, on your own case studies, from a question chosen on the day. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. It is **peer-marked against the official five-criteria rubric and then moderated live**, so you read the mark scheme as a marker once before it is used on you.

## Learning Behaviour postings

The five **LB** postings are not tasks you prepare for. They record how you work: blog punctuality, returning homework on time, participation in a screening or seminar, and turning feedback into a named target. They are posted on the dates in the [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-assessments|register]] and they reward the habits the rest of this page describes. LB only covers weeks that have neither a CS task nor an attainment.
