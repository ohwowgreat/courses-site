# Media Studies · S1 Course Skills and Homework

[[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]] · [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-assessments|S1 Assessment Register]] · Course Skills and homework, all eleven items

The four attainments each have their own page with a full rubric. The eleven Course Skills items are smaller by design. This page lists them all: what each one is, when it happens, what you produce, what it is marked on, and what it feeds. None of these are surprise tasks. Each one rehearses a move an attainment will ask for later.

## How these count

**Course Skills is 20% of your grade.** It is made of the nine CS items and the two homework tasks below. They are marked on whether the specific skill is there, not on polish. They carry **72 hours' notice** rather than the week an attainment gets. Most are done in class. The two that are not are the break homework tasks, and both are capped: **three to four hours across a holiday, not more.** If a task is taking longer than its cap, stop and say so.

One rule runs through all of them. Each CS task is direct practice for something larger. The register names which one. Do them as rehearsals and the attainments stop being events.

## Unit 1 and 2: the vocabulary items

### CS1 · Denotation-only paragraph

**Fri 2026-09-04, in class, 25 minutes.** Write one paragraph about an unseen still, describing only what is literally there. Sentence frames are available. Marked on **AO2**. The criterion is discipline: zero interpretive claims. Evaluative adjectives are the giveaway. It feeds A1, where the same describing-before-meaning move is worth marks under time.

### CS2 · Annotated media text

**Fri 2026-09-11, homework-based, set on the Monday.** Choose an everyday media text and annotate it with **at least six accurate code labels**. It is peer-checked against the exemplar before it is collected. Marked on **AO1**. The 30-minute cap applies: size the text, not the ambition.

### CS3 · Vocabulary quiz

**Fri 2026-09-18, in class, 20 minutes.** Twenty items: twelve define-the-term and eight label-the-example, drawn from the terms so far and from the [[concepts/media-vocabulary|Cambridge glossary]]. Marked on **AO1**. The target is 15 of 20, or every missed term logged in your lexicon with its correction. You self-mark immediately, and the self-marking teaches more than the quiz.

### CS4 · Genre and camera paragraph

**Wed 2026-09-23, in class.** Write one analytical paragraph on an unlabeled clip, in exam register: a genre claim, then technical evidence in camera verbs, then symbolic evidence, then what the audience is promised. Marked on **AO2**. This is A1 with the stakes off. It is marked in the official Paper 2 rubric's language so the feedback transfers directly.

## The break tasks

### HW1 · Break analysis and the production window

**Set Wed 2026-09-30, due Fri 2026-10-09.** Two things across the National Day break. First, an **A1-format analysis** of a 2 to 3 minute sequence you choose yourself: not a text taught in class, and on a platform your teacher can view. Second, the **production window**: three to four hours of shooting for your Component 1 product, with diary entries as you go. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. The analysis is debriefed with margin notes rather than a grade, so you get A1 feedback five days early.

### HW2 · Case-study revision pack and an essay plan

**Set Wed 2026-12-23, due Mon 2027-01-04.** Across the winter break: a revision pack from your case-study document, plus **one Section B essay plan** aimed at your weakest cell in the coverage matrix. Marked on **AO1**. It feeds CS9 directly, so the plan you write over the holiday is the one you rehearse against the clock in the first week back.

## Units 3 to 5: the coursework and craft items

### CS5 · Peer blog audit

**Wed 2026-10-21, in class, in the double.** Audit a partner's blog post by post against the **mid-point checklist**, using describe, question, suggest. Write findings as actions rather than comments: "post 3 has no audience data, add the survey screenshot" beats "more detail". Marked on **AO3**. The checklist is the one A2 is marked with, so this is your A2 marking preview with three teaching days left to act on it.

### CS6 · Representation close reading

**Fri 2026-11-13, in class, 20 minutes.** Write one close-reading paragraph on a provided still or short clip, with a **theorist doing analytical work rather than decoration**: "as Mulvey's camera-look predicts…" not "Mulvey said the male gaze". The theorist sentence-starters card is on the desk. Marked on **AO2**. Papers swap at the end so a partner can hunt for a decorated theorist.

### CS7 · CCR draft review

**Wed 2026-11-18, in class, in the double.** Your full creative critical reflection draft is reviewed against the **real C1 criteria** by teacher and peers. You leave with a written action list in C1 criteria language. Marked on **AO2**. It is direct practice for A3, where the CCR is where AO2 lives in the portfolio.

### CS8 · Guided Section A

**Wed 2026-12-09, in class, 50 minutes.** Write a Section A response **with the mark scheme visible and consultable**: you may stop, check the band language, and annotate your own margin. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. Afterwards you label which criterion each paragraph was earning and find the one you never fed. That gap is your A4 target, where the scaffold is removed.

### CS9 · Timed Section B

**Wed 2027-01-06, in class, 45 minutes.** Write a full Section B answer under time, on your own case studies, from a question chosen on the day. Marked on **AO1 and AO2**. It is **peer-marked against the official five-criteria rubric and then moderated live**, so you read the mark scheme as a marker once before it is used on you.

## Learning Behaviour postings

The seven **LB** postings are not tasks you prepare for. They record how you work: blog punctuality, returning homework on time, participation in a screening or seminar, and turning feedback into a named target. They are posted on the dates in the [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-assessments|register]], and they reward the habits the rest of this page describes.
