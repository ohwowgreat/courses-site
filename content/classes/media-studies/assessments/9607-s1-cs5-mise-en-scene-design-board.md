# Media Studies · S1 CS5: Mise-en-Scene, the Design Board

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Thu 2026-09-24, in class |
| What | A six-cell design board for your own opening |
| Also | Your group's C1 design document |
| Marked on | AO1 + AO3 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Design every element placed in your own frame, on purpose
2. State a connotation for each design decision
3. Keep a design internally consistent

## Description

A six-cell design board for your own opening. Each cell carries **one connotation line**:

1. **Location**, with the photograph you took for homework
2. **Costume** for one character
3. **Two properties** that carry meaning
4. **Lighting plan**, named by set-up and quality
5. **Colour palette**, three to five swatches
6. **The one-sentence claim**: what this design says about the person, place or world it shows

This board is not a side exercise. It **is** your group's Component 1 design document, and it is checked against your plan at the planning lock.

## Guidelines

- Six cells. All of them, even if one is harder than the rest.
- Use your own location photograph, not one found online.
- Name the lighting by set-up and quality: high key or low key, hard or soft.
- Three to five swatches. Twelve is not a palette.
- One connotation line per cell. What does this element make the audience think?
- Check the board against itself: does the palette agree with the lighting, does the costume agree with the location?

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | All six cells complete, each with a stated connotation, and the whole board holds together: palette, lighting, costume and location tell one story. |
| **Secure** | Six cells present with connotations, though one or two are generic, and consistency is broadly held. |
| **Developing** | Cells missing, or connotations absent on several, or elements that contradict each other with no acknowledgement. |
| **Not yet** | Fewer than four cells, or no connotations stated. |

Marked on coverage of the syllabus elements, a connotation per cell, and internal consistency. Not on visual polish.
