## Objectives

This assessment asks you to show, in writing and without help, that you can:

1. Analyze an extract you have never seen, naming how camera, sound, mise-en-scène and editing construct its meaning.
2. Tie every observation to an effect. A technique that is named but not explained does not count.
3. Cover all four code families systematically rather than writing at length about the one you noticed first.
4. Use precise media terminology throughout, including the sound and editing vocabulary, not only the camera terms.
5. Where you bring in a theory or a context, say where the extract complicates it as well as where it fits.
6. Sustain 60 minutes of continuous analytical writing under exam conditions.

## The task

You are given an unseen extract from a television drama, 2 to 3 minutes long, and the Paper 2 Section A prompt: analyze how the extract constructs meaning through camera, sound, mise-en-scène and editing. It is the same wording as the real examination.

The extract is deliberately unfamiliar. Everything you need in order to read it has been taught since Week 1: signs and codes ([[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-03-sign-types-and-codes|L03]]), genre and camera ([[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-04-genre-and-camera|L04]]), narrative under load ([[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-05-narrative-and-the-planning-lock|L05]]), and the four-pass [[concepts/the-viewing-protocol|viewing protocol]]. What is new is the text, not the skill.

## Instructions

**Before the sit.** Bring your personal code checklist, the one you built from the L03 exit ticket. It is collected at the door and handed back to you at your desk, because systematic coverage is one of the things this assessment samples. Review your strain log from the two lessons before this one.

**In the room.**

1. Conditions are briefed once, then the room goes silent.
2. The extract is screened **four times**, following the examination's own protocol: watch the first time without writing, then take a note pass on each of the three remaining screenings.
3. Write for **60 minutes**, continuously.
4. Hand in your script. Unfinished at 60 minutes is submitted at 60 minutes. The timebox is part of what this samples.

**Writing the answer.** Work through the code families, not through the extract's timeline. For each observation, name the technique, point to the exact moment, and say what it does to the viewer. One well-chosen moment analyzed properly is worth more than five moments listed. If you use Propp or Todorov, include at least one sentence on where the extract strains the model.

**If you are absent or need an extension.** Contact your teacher **at least 24 hours before** the sitting. Illness needs a doctor's note. Without advance contact, the mark may be 0, and that rule applies even when the absence is genuine illness (AL Student Guide p.8). Extensions are also requested 24 hours ahead. See [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS Assessment Framework]].

## Rubric

Marked on the **official Cambridge Paper 2 criteria**. Five criteria, 25 marks, and each criterion is banded **independently** on a best-fit basis. A script can sit in different bands on different criteria, so a strong terminology mark does not lift a weak analysis mark.

| # | Criterion | AO | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Media Concepts (language and representation) | AO1 | 0 to 5 |
| 2 | Contexts and Critical Debates (social significances) | AO1 | 0 to 5 |
| 3 | Use of Terminology | AO1 | 0 to 5 |
| 4 | Analysis of how meaning is created, including use of theory | AO2 | 0 to 5 |
| 5 | Use of Examples | AO2 | 0 to 5 |

Totals: **AO1 15, AO2 10.** Cambridge's band words, per criterion: **5** sophisticated or insightful · **4** thorough or effective · **3** clear or appropriate · **2** limited · **1** basic · **0** no creditable content.

### What each band looks like in this task

The five criteria and their band words are Cambridge's. The descriptions in this table are written for this course so that the criteria are checkable before you write, not only after you are marked. They show how the bands are applied here; they are not Cambridge's published wording.

| Criterion | Band 5 | Band 3 | Band 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Media Concepts** | Reads language and representation together: the technical choices are shown doing something to who or what is represented | Applies media language accurately, but representation is handled separately from the technical reading, or briefly | Concepts are named without being applied to this extract |
| **Contexts and Critical Debates** | Genre, industry or audience context is used to explain a specific choice, and any theory used is tested against the extract, including where it strains | One relevant context is stated and connected to at least one choice | Context is asserted and left unconnected |
| **Use of Terminology** | Precise throughout, sound and editing included, used because it is the shortest accurate description available | Correct in the familiar areas, camera and mise-en-scène, thinner on sound and editing | Everyday description with occasional labels attached |
| **Analysis of meaning** | Every observation reaches an effect. Nothing is listed | Most observations reach an effect, some stop at description | What happens is described, and the meaning is left implied |
| **Use of Examples** | Specific moments, described precisely enough that a marker can find them, drawn from all four code families | Real examples, unevenly distributed across the code families | Examples are general or refer to the extract as a whole |

### How the mark becomes a grade

The 25-mark total is **not** what is recorded as your grade. Attainments are curved against CIE past-paper data, allowing for where the class is in its learning, so the grade reflects your trajectory rather than what the script would score on a Cambridge paper today. You will always be told how the boundaries and curve were set. The grade scale is on [[shared/bnds-assessment-framework|BNDS Assessment Framework]].

Two things worth knowing now: this is one of **four** attainments this semester, and because the course runs four rather than six, **no lowest attainment can be dropped.** That is a fact about how the course is built, not about conduct.
