# Media Studies · S1 CS10: Representation Close Reading

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | Fri 2026-11-13, in class, 20 minutes |
| What | One close-reading paragraph with a theorist doing real work |
| Marked on | AO2 |

## Objectives

By the end of this task you can:

1. Make a theorist predict something in a text
2. Support that prediction with evidence you can point at
3. Tell analytical work from decoration

## Description

One close-reading paragraph on a provided still or short clip, with **a theorist doing analytical work rather than decoration**.

The difference is everything:

- Decoration: "Mulvey said the male gaze."
- Work: "As Mulvey's camera-look predicts, the lens finds her body before her face, so the audience is positioned with the looker rather than the looked-at."

In the second, the theory predicts something, and the evidence confirms it. Name plus claim plus evidence, never name alone.

This is the exact move A4 and the End of Term exam reward.

## Guidelines

- Choose one theorist: van Zoonen, Mulvey, or hooks. One, used well.
- State what the theory predicts before you show the evidence.
- Point at something specific: a framing, an order, a cut, an absence.
- Twenty minutes. Two to plan, fifteen to write, three to check.
- Read it back and ask: is the theorist doing work here, or standing next to my point?
- If you could delete the theorist's name and lose nothing, you have decorated.

## Rubric

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | The theory predicts a specific feature, the evidence confirms it, and the paragraph could not be written without the theorist. |
| **Secure** | Theorist named and applied, prediction implied rather than stated, evidence present. |
| **Developing** | Theorist named beside an analysis they are not driving, or the evidence is general. |
| **Not yet** | Theory dropped in as a quotation with no application. |
