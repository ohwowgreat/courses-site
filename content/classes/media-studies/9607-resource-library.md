# Media Studies · Resource Library

This page lists the resources for the course: the endorsed coursebook, past papers with mark schemes, and Cambridge's published portfolio exemplars.

## The coursebook

Marshall, Naisbett & Stoklosa, *Cambridge International AS & A Level Media Studies Coursebook*, CUP, ©2016/2023, Cambridge-endorsed. It has eight units:

| Unit | Book pp. |
|---|---|
| 1 Introduction to Media | 2–15 |
| 2 Media Language: Micro elements | 16–58 |
| 3 Media Language: Macro elements | 59–82 |
| 4 Textual Analysis | 83–104 |
| 5 Media Contexts | 105–136 |
| 6 Media Production: AS and A Level portfolios | 137–180 |
| 7 Media Debates | 181–221 |
| 8 Media Ecology | 222–252 |

Terms are defined inline as you read. The book has no glossary and no index.

*My Life is Mine* (§4.1) is the practice extract that anchors Unit 4. It is the exam-style extract used to model the A1 task, with the four-viewings protocol and worked note charts.

The coursebook covers a wide range of theorists: Barthes, Saussure, Hall (on the active audience in §1.2, representation in §3.3, and encoding/decoding in §5.3), Lacey, Neale, Buckingham, Fiske, Todorov, Propp, Lévi-Strauss, Curran & Seaton, Jenkins, Shirky, Hesmondhalgh, Turkle, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Eco, Jameson, Strinati, Gauntlett, McLuhan, Postman, Innis, Ong, Logan, Tapscott, Leadbeater, boyd, and Lanier. §8.3 uses a WeChat/Tencent super-app case study as its main China-local example.

## Past papers

Past papers cover Components 2 and 4 only, from the June 2024 and June 2025 sessions. Components 1 and 3 are coursework, so they have no written paper.

**Paper 2** has two 25-mark essays. In Section A you analyze how an extract constructs meaning through camera, sound, mise-en-scène, and editing. You watch the extract four times, on a 30/45/45-minute rubric. Section B is a contexts essay.

**Paper 4** (A2) has two sections. Section A gives three topics in a fixed order: regulation, postmodern media, and power, as 15-mark essays. How many you answer will be confirmed in class. Section B is a compulsory Media Ecology synoptic worth 30 marks.

Command words across all papers: Analyse, Explain, Evaluate, Assess, and To what extent.

> [!note] If you sit the exam in 2028
> Paper 2 is taken digitally. The extract stays available throughout the exam and you may watch it as many times as you like. Watch it once straight through before you make notes. Section A lasts 1 hour 15 minutes and Section B lasts 45 minutes.

### How your work is marked

**Paper 2** (both Section A extract analysis and Section B contexts essay, 25 marks each): five criteria, each marked independently on a 0–5 band, best-fit. A script can sit at different levels on different criteria.

| AO | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 (15) | Media Concepts (language and representation) | /5 |
| AO1 | Contexts and Critical Debates (social significances) | /5 |
| AO1 | Use of Terminology | /5 |
| AO2 (10) | Analysis of how meaning is created, including use of theory | /5 |
| AO2 | Use of Examples | /5 |

Band words per criterion: **5** Sophisticated / Insightful; **4** Thorough / Effective; **3** Clear / appropriate; **2** Limited; **1** Basic; **0** no creditable content. Paper 2 is AO1 60 / AO2 40.

**Paper 4** (A2): Section A 15-mark essays use the same five criteria at 3 marks each; the Section B Media Ecology 30-marker uses generic level descriptors (Higher = Levels 4–5, Middle = Level 3, Basic = Levels 1–2). AO1 60 / AO2 40 overall.

What the exemplars show:

- Candidate A was well dated and showed progression but capped at Level 3: the blog carried too much preliminary material and not enough on the set brief.
- Candidate B's planning includes recce reports, casting information, scripting, storyboarding, and test shots for effects.
- Both candidates used only one digital application in the CCR. For Candidate B, that was the one strand off the top band.
