# Media Studies · Resource Library

This page lists the materials for the course: the endorsed coursebook, past papers with mark schemes, Cambridge specimen answers, and published exemplars.

## The syllabus straddle

The course sits across a spec change, so the materials come in two editions of the same series on opposite sides of the [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|syllabus straddle]].

| Edition | For examination from | Right for |
|---|---|---|
| Coursebook, ©2016/2023 (ISBN 978-1-009-26224-8) | 2024 | G11 / AS June 2027 |
| 2nd-edition Teacher's Resource, ©2026 | 2028 | G12 / [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a2-blog-mid-point\|A2]] June 2028 |

The differences fall mostly in the coursework and the final unit. In the 2024 edition, Unit 6 sets an individual blog, §6.7 is the four CCR questions presented creatively, and Unit 8 is "Media Ecology". In the 2028 syllabus, the portfolio is a slide portfolio with a set-question evaluation essay, the A Level minor task is a website home page, and Unit 8 becomes "Changing Media Environments".

## The coursebook

Marshall, Naisbett & Stoklosa, *Cambridge International AS & A Level Media Studies Coursebook*, CUP ©2016/2023, Cambridge-endorsed. Eight units:

| Unit | Book pp. | Topics |
|---|---|---|

| 3 Media Language: Macro elements | 59–82 | §3.1 genre, §3.2 narrative, §3.3 representation |
| 4 Textual Analysis | 83–104 | §4.1 exam-style prompt anatomy, §4.2 note method, §4.3 essay craft |
| 5 Media Contexts | 105–136 | §§5.1–5.5 contexts and audience theory |
| 6 Media Production | 137–180 | coursework portfolios; §6.7 CCR |
| 7 Media Debates | 181–221 | regulation §7.1, postmodernism §7.2, power §7.3 |
| 8 Media Ecology | 222–252 | §8.3 WeChat/Tencent super-app case study |

Worth remembering:

- **§4.1's practice extract *My Life is Mine*** anchors all of Unit 4: the exam-style prompt, the four-viewings protocol, and the worked note charts all use it. It is the print model for the [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a1-timed-media-language-analysis|A1]] task.
- China-local assets in the book, such as the own-culture color wheel (§2.4), the WeChat case study (§8.3), and SAPP named as China's game regulator (Table 7.3), are used as asides only under the exam-example rule in [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]]. Core taught examples, case studies, and assessed work stay exam-eligible.
- The theorist roster is wide: Barthes, Saussure, Hall (three times: Active Audience §1.2, representation §3.3, encoding/decoding §5.3), Lacey, Neale, Buckingham, Fiske, Todorov, Propp, Lévi-Strauss, Curran & Seaton, Jenkins, Shirky, Hesmondhalgh, Turkle, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Eco, Jameson, Strinati, Gauntlett, McLuhan, Postman, Innis, Ong, Logan, Tapscott, Leadbeater, boyd, Lanier.
- There is no glossary and no index. Terms are defined inline only. There is no gaze theory anywhere: Mulvey, hooks, and van Zoonen are outside the material.

## Past papers

Past papers cover Components 2 and 4 (Components 1 and 3 are coursework), for June 2024 and June 2025.

**Paper 2** has the same Section A stem in every paper: analyze how the extract constructs meaning through camera, sound, mise-en-scène, and editing, 25 marks, four screenings, on a 30/45/45-minute rubric. Section B is a contexts essay, also 25 marks.

**Paper 4** Section A is always the same three-topic menu in fixed order: regulation, postmodern media, power. Section B is a compulsory Media Ecology synoptic 30-marker.

Command words across all papers: Analyse, Explain, Evaluate, Assess, To what extent.

### How the papers are marked

**Paper 2** (Section A extract analysis and Section B contexts essay, 25 marks each): five criteria, each marked independently on a 0–5 band, best-fit, so a script can sit at different levels on different criteria.

| AO | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 (15) | Media Concepts (language & representation) | /5 |
| AO1 | Contexts & Critical Debates (social significances) | /5 |
| AO1 | Use of Terminology | /5 |
| AO2 (10) | Analysis of how meaning is created, incl. use of theory | /5 |
| AO2 | Use of Examples | /5 |

Per-criterion band words: **5** Sophisticated / Insightful, **4** Thorough / Effective, **3** Clear / appropriate, **2** Limited, **1** Basic, **0** no creditable content. So Paper 2 is AO1 60 / AO2 40.

**Paper 4** ([[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a2-blog-mid-point|A2]]): Section A three 15-mark essays, each scored on the same five criteria at 3 marks apiece; Section B Media Ecology 30-marker uses generic level descriptors (Higher = Levels 4–5, Middle = Level 3, Basic = Levels 1–2). AO1 60 / AO2 40 overall.

## Specimen answers, 2028 spec

Cambridge's own marked exemplars, written against the 2028–30 spec, for the G12 cohort. Two answers per question, high and middle, each with an examiner comment, a per-AO mark breakdown, and a "common errors" list. Cambridge states the marks are "for guidance only".

| Booklet | Questions covered | High | Middle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Component 2** (12 pp.) | Q1 Section A only, *Industry* S1 E1 'Induction' (2020, dir. Dunham), analyse construction of meaning through camera / sound / mise-en-scène / editing, 25 marks | **21/25** (AO1 13, AO2 8) | **12/25** (AO1 7, AO2 5) |
| **Component 4** (20 pp.) | Q1 regulation (15), Q3 power (15), Q4 Section B changing media environments, impact of technology and audience behavior on media funding (30) | **13/15 · 12/15 · 27/30** | **8/15 · 7/15 · 17/30** |

The five-criteria rubric is the same as the current one above: AO1 15 (concepts 5, contexts and critical debates 5, terminology 5), AO2 10 (analysis incl. theory 5, examples 5), 25 per question, AO1 60 / AO2 40. The Component 4 marks fit the same shape at different totals.

### The exam becomes digital from 2028

Both Component 2 and Component 4 are digital exams from 2028. For Component 2 this changes exam technique directly: the extract is available throughout the exam and may be watched as many times as you like, with the instruction to watch it once straight through before you take notes. Timing is 1 hr 15 for Section A (viewing included) and 45 min for Section B. The current regime, which the G11 cohort sits in June 2027, is four fixed screenings on the 30/45/45-minute rubric.

In the 2028 spec Section A of Paper 4, you answer two of the three debate questions, for a paper total of 15 + 15 + 30 = 60.

### What they are good for

- The Component 4 exemplars pair with the three debate concept pages: [[concepts/media-regulation|Media Regulation]] (Q1), [[concepts/power-and-the-media|Power and the Media]] (Q3), and [[concepts/media-ecology|Media Ecology]] (Q4). Each has a marked model answer and a stated route to the marks it did not get.
- The middle answers show the common gap: real knowledge, described rather than analyzed, with theory bolted on. That is the gap the [[concepts/the-viewing-protocol|viewing protocol]] drills.
- The Component 4 high answers use late-2025 material (the Online Safety Act, shadow-banning research, the TikTok/Oracle sale). Under the [[concepts/case-study-method|five-year contemporary rule]] they work now but will be stale by 2030, so learn the move, not the examples.

Both exemplars are the film brief; no magazine exemplar is published. Full reading is on [[concepts/process-evidence-and-reflection|Process Evidence and Reflection]]. The criteria PDF matches the strand table on [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a3-c1-portfolio|Media Studies · S1 A3: Component 1 Portfolio]]: five strands of 10, AO3i–iv plus AO2, level bands 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 1–2.

What these show about coursework:

- A blog can be too heavy in the wrong place. Candidate A was well dated and showed progression but still capped at Level 3 for carrying too much preliminary material and not enough on the set brief.
- Planning is what separates the two. Candidate B's planning is listed as recce reports, casting information, scripting, storyboarding, and test shots for effects.
- Both lost the same mark in the same place: each used only one digital application in the CCR. For Candidate B, otherwise top of Level 5, that was the only strand off the top band.
