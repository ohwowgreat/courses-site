# Media Studies · Resource Library

This page lists the main resources for the course: the endorsed coursebook, past exam papers, the official marking rubric, and Cambridge exemplar answers.

## The syllabus straddle

The course covers two syllabus versions, and your coursework format depends on your cohort. See the [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|syllabus straddle]] for the full picture.

| Cohort | Coursework and process |
|---|---|
| G11 / AS (June 2027) | Unit 6 sets an individual blog; the four CCR questions are "presented creatively"; the minor task is a social media page; Unit 8 is "Media Ecology". |
| G12 / [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a2-blog-mid-point\|A2]] (June 2028) | The coursework is a slide portfolio plus a syllabus-set evaluation essay; the A Level minor task is a website home page; Unit 8 is "Changing Media Environments". |

## The coursebook

Marshall, Naisbett & Stoklosa, *Cambridge International AS & A Level Media Studies Coursebook*, CUP ©2016/2023 (ISBN 978-1-009-26224-8). This is the Cambridge-endorsed coursebook. It has eight units:

| Unit | Book pages |
|---|---|
| 1 Introduction to Media | 2–15 |
| 2 Media Language: Micro elements | 16–58 |
| 3 Media Language: Macro elements | 59–82 |
| 4 Textual Analysis | 83–104 |
| 5 Media Contexts | 105–136 |
| 6 Media Production: AS and A Level portfolios | 137–180 |
| 7 Media Debates | 181–221 |
| 8 Media Ecology | 222–252 |

Worth knowing:

- Unit 4 is built around the practice extract *My Life is Mine* in §4.1. The exam-style prompt, the four-viewings protocol, and the worked note charts all use it.
- In §8.3 the coursebook uses a WeChat/Tencent super-app case study as a China-local example. China-local examples like this are used as asides only; your assessed work and case studies must use exam-eligible examples (see [[classes/media-studies/media-studies|Media Studies]]).
- The theorists in the book include Barthes, Saussure, Hall (three times: Active Audience §1.2, representation §3.3, encoding/decoding §5.3), Lacey, Neale, Buckingham, Fiske, Todorov, Propp, Lévi-Strauss, Curran & Seaton, Jenkins, Shirky, Hesmondhalgh, Turkle, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Eco, Jameson, Strinati, Gauntlett, McLuhan, Postman, Innis, Ong, Logan, Tapscott, Leadbeater, boyd and Lanier.
- There is no glossary and no index; terms are defined inline only. The book has no gaze theory, so Mulvey, hooks and van Zoonen are not in it.

## Past papers

The exam has two written papers, Paper 2 and Paper 4. Components 1 and 3 are coursework. Past papers cover June 2024 and June 2025, and the format is the same across both sessions.

Paper 2, Section A is an extract analysis worth 25 marks. The question asks you to analyze how the extract constructs meaning, including the representations of individuals, groups, events and places, through camera, sound, mise-en-scène and editing. In the June 2027 regime you get four screenings, on a 30/45/45-minute rubric.

Paper 2, Section B is a contexts essay worth 25 marks. It offers two questions, one on industry and one on audience. Every question needs references to at least one [[concepts/case-study-method|case study]].

The four key concepts split across Paper 2: Section A examines [[concepts/media-language|language]] and [[concepts/representation|representation]]; Section B examines [[concepts/media-audience|audience]] and [[concepts/media-industry|industry]].

Paper 4 ([[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a2-blog-mid-point|A2]]): Section A offers one 15-mark essay on each of three topics, [[concepts/media-regulation|Regulation]], [[concepts/postmodern-media|Postmodern]] media and [[concepts/power-and-the-media|Power]], and you answer two of the three. Section B is a compulsory 30-mark synoptic essay, the [[concepts/media-ecology|Ecology 30-marker]]. The cover rubric reads: "Answer three questions in total: Section A: answer two questions. Section B: answer Question 4" (15 + 15 + 30 = 60).

Most exam questions are a syllabus point turned into a sentence.

## How the papers are marked

Paper 2 (both Section A extract analysis and Section B contexts essay, 25 marks each): five criteria, each marked on its own 0–5 band, best fit. A script can sit at a different level on each criterion.

| AO | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 (15) | Media Concepts (language & representation) | /5 |
| AO1 | Contexts & Critical Debates (social significances) | /5 |
| AO1 | Use of Terminology | /5 |
| AO2 (10) | Analysis of how meaning is created, incl. use of theory | /5 |
| AO2 | Use of Examples | /5 |

Band words per criterion: **5** Sophisticated / Insightful · **4** Thorough / Effective · **3** Clear / appropriate · **2** Limited · **1** Basic · **0** no creditable content. So Paper 2 is AO1 60 / AO2 40.

Section B uses the same criterion names and the same band ladder, but the concept content differs. Its Media Concepts criterion reads "the key concepts of audience and industry" where Section A reads language and representation; its Analysis criterion at Levels 4–5 expects "texts from multiple case studies" (Level 3 says "one or more," Level 2 "normally from only one case study"); Use of Examples wants "examples from a wide range of texts." So "at least one case study" is the floor, not the top band. The [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-eot-component-2-paper|EoT]] Section B runs on one student-built case study.

Paper 4 (A2): each 15-mark Section A essay is scored on the same five criteria at 3 marks each, and you answer two of the three topics. The Section B 30-marker uses generic level descriptors (Higher = Levels 4–5, Middle = Level 3, Basic = Levels 1–2). Overall AO1 60 / AO2 40.

## Exemplar answers

### Cambridge specimen answers (2028 spec)

Cambridge's marked exemplars, written against the 2028–30 spec, so they apply to the G12 / June 2028 cohort. Each question has two answers, high and middle, with an examiner comment, a per-AO mark breakdown, and a "common errors" list. Cambridge states the marks are "for guidance only".

| Booklet | Questions covered | High | Middle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Component 2 | Section A extract analysis, 25 marks | 21/25 (AO1 13, AO2 8) | 12/25 (AO1 7, AO2 5) |
| Component 4 | Q1 regulation (15), Q3 power (15), Q4 Section B changing media environments (30) | 13/15 · 12/15 · 27/30 | 8/15 · 7/15 · 17/30 |

In the Component 4 booklet, the middle answer's Q4 AO1 mark is printed "out of 15." This is a typo; the correct denominator is 18 (18 + 12 = 30).

### The digital exam from 2028

From 2028, Papers 2 and 4 are digital exams. For Paper 2 this changes your technique: the extract is available throughout the exam and you may watch it as many times as you like, with the instruction to watch it once straight through before you take notes. Timing is 1 hr 15 for Section A (viewing included) and 45 minutes for Section B. The June 2027 regime instead uses four fixed screenings on a 30/45/45-minute rubric; the [[classes/media-studies/lesson-plans/9607-s1-lesson-18-exam-rehearsal|L18 rehearsal]] and the [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-eot-component-2-paper|EoT]] use that four-screening regime.

The assessment criteria match the strand table on [[classes/media-studies/assessments/9607-s1-a3-c1-portfolio|Media Studies · S1 A3: Component 1 Portfolio]] (five strands of 10, AO3i–iv + AO2, level bands 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 1–2). What these show:

- A blog can carry too much preliminary material and too little on the set brief, which caps it at Level 3 even when it is well dated and shows clear progression.
- Strong planning includes recce reports, casting information, scripting, storyboarding, and test shots for effects.
- Both exemplars lost a mark for using only one digital application in the CCR. Using more than one is easy credit.
